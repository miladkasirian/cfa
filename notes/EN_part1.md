## 1.2 What changed in the 2026 curriculum

### The four Level III ethics learning modules

1. Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (LM1)
2. Guidance for Standards I-VII (LM2)
3. Application of the Code and Standards: Level III (LM3)
4. Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct (LM4)

Source: [CURRICULUM] Volume 5 table of contents; [OFFICIAL] https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/application-of-the-code-and-standards-level-iii ; [PROVIDER] IFT listing https://ift.world/?p=264154

### The 2023 revisions (effective 1 January 2024), now in the curriculum

| Change | New text or rule | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **New I(E) Competence** | "Members and Candidates must act with and maintain the competence necessary to fulfill their professional responsibilities." | (curriculum L1121) |
| I(E) detail | Competence criteria differ by role; facts and circumstances decide. I(E) does **not** require any specific professional development or continuing education program | (curriculum L947 to L948) |
| I(E) vs the Code | The Code says "maintain and improve their professional competence"; I(E) says "act with and maintain." I(E) makes the Code's requirement explicit | (curriculum L946) |
| **V(B) costs disclosure** | V(B)(1) now requires disclosing "the nature of the services provided, along with information about the costs to the client associated with those services." | (curriculum L1196, L929) |
| **VI(A) renamed** | Now "Avoid or Disclose Conflicts": "must avoid or make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity..." Disclosures must be prominent, in plain language, and effective | (curriculum L1217, L931) |
| VI(A) priority | Avoid conflicts "whenever reasonably possible"; avoiding is preferred; when avoiding is not reasonable, clear and complete disclosure is still required | (curriculum L959) |

Source for the summary: [CURRICULUM] "Summary of the 2023 Revisions to the Code and Standards" (curriculum L925 to L959); [OFFICIAL] https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2024/01/11/revisions-to-the-cfa-institute-code-of-ethics-and-standards-of-professional-conduct/

### Asset Manager Code (AMC) is a Level III module

- [CURRICULUM] LM4 outcomes: explain the AMC's purpose and benefits; explain the six General Principles of Conduct; determine whether a manager's practices are consistent with the AMC; recommend practices that prevent violations (curriculum L6971 to L6974).
- [CURRICULUM] The AMC applies to **firms** ("Managers"), not individuals; it is **voluntary**; it covers all employees; it should be adopted by senior management and the board (curriculum L6980 to L6986).

- [YOUTUBER] Almost no YouTube video covers the AMC in detail (one 2021 Mark Meldrum video called it a list that "doesn't seem intuitive", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jJTopkPmPms). Use the curriculum for it.

## 1.3 How official item-set questions are built

Based on the 2026 curriculum practice problems: LM2 (39 questions, curriculum L5119 to L5613) and LM3 (8 item sets, 47 questions, curriculum L6131 to L6963).

### Vignette build (LM3 item sets)

- Opens with "The following information relates to questions X-Y."
- About 490 to 990 words; most 500 to 760.
- 2 to 6 named people. At least one CFA charterholder and often a candidate. CFA status is always stated, because it decides who is bound.
- Told in time order, in paragraph "chunks." Each question points to one chunk ("When meeting with Vanderon...", "When shorting Tefla stock..."). Questions follow the story order.
- Often embeds structured material: quoted dialogue, a bulleted draft policy, numbered "Statement 1 to 4" or "Procedure 1 to 3", a fee table, a performance exhibit, or compliance-manual definitions.
- Small numbers are planted for light math (fee vs commissions saved; $25.00 vs $25.01).
- Some facts are decoys with no question, or tempting reasons that do not matter (political pressure; "the price was distorted").
- Each set touches about 5 to 8 different standards. One question can test two standards.
- Curriculum sets have 5 or 6 questions; the live exam has 4 per set [OFFICIAL]. The style is the same.

### Stem types and how often they appear

| Stem type | Example form | Count in LM3 (47 Qs) | Also in LM2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Violation yes/no with reasons | "When [action], did [person] violate any of the Standards?" A: No / B: Yes, because... / C: Yes, because... (or the reverse: "Is X consistent?" A: Yes / B, C: No, because...) | about 22 | Yes, the dominant form |
| Two-item grid | Table with columns for two items; rows give three of the four Yes/No combinations (usually No/No, No/Yes, Yes/No) | 6 | Two-statement "neither / only I / only II" (Q5) |
| Compound consistency | "Are X and Y both consistent?" Yes / only X / only Y | about 5 | |
| Best or most appropriate action | "Locke's best course of action", "Riser should:" | 6 | "best course", "best policy" |
| Least likely violated | Options are three standard names; pick the one NOT implicated | 3 | "least likely to violate" |
| Negative requirement | "Riser is not required to disclose to clients his:" | 1 | "which statement violates" |
| Duty owed | "Locke owes his primary duty of loyalty to:" | 1 | "duty is owed to:" |
| No stem | Facts then three full-sentence options; implied "which is correct" | 0 | 7 |

Sources: [CURRICULUM] the item-set and practice-problem sections of the curriculum, L6131 to L6963 and L5119 to L5613.

### How the options are built

- The bare "No" (no violation) option is usually **A**. The two "Yes" options carry "because..." or "relating to [standard]."
- Often **two options share the same verdict** and differ only in the reason. The reason decides the answer.
- A right verdict with a wrong reason is a wrong answer (LM3 Q7: option C says "Yes, because he was an unpaid intern": right verdict, wrong reason).
- Sometimes the verdict is fixed and all three options say "No, because..." (LM3 Q10). You only pick the reason.
- Grid and least-likely items have no reasons.
- Recommended procedures are tested by asking which action or policy is "most consistent", not by asking you to list them.

### How often the answer is "no violation"

| Source | Result |
|---|---|
| LM3 item sets | **13 of 47 (about 28%)** are "no violation" or "consistent" (Q4, Q7, Q13, Q14, Q19, Q20, Q24, Q25, Q30, Q39, Q42, Q43, Q46). In 12 of 13 the answer is option A; Q7 is the exception (B) |
| LM2 questions about a person's conduct | 12 violation, 3 no violation, 1 split verdict (of 16) |
| Answer letters, LM3 | A 18, B 16, C 13 |
| Answer letters, LM2 | A 9, B 19, C 11 |

- [DERIVED] About one question in four says the conduct was fine, and those vignettes are deliberately loaded with suspicious-looking facts. Never assume a violation because something "feels" wrong.
- [DERIVED] Answer letters are not a pattern to rely on.

### Distractor types (merged from LM2 and LM3 solutions)

| # | Distractor type | Curriculum example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Right verdict, wrong reason | LM2 Q17 B, Q28 A; LM3 Q7 C |
| 2 | Wrong standard named | LM2 Q28 A (V(B) instead of V(A)); LM3 Q34 C (MNPI instead of Misconduct) |
| 3 | Overreaction: too strict or extreme | LM2 Q9 A ("permanently"); LM3 Q4 C (suspend immediately), Q9 A (resign), Q44 B (terminate broker) |
| 4 | Disclosure offered as a cure when the act is banned or needs consent | LM2 Q2 C, Q17 A, Q26 A |
| 5 | Notifying the client afterwards "cures" it | LM3 Q2 C (IPS breach), LM2 Q23 C |
| 6 | Half-measure or compromise | LM2 Q3 A (move rating only one notch); LM3 Q6 A (reduce allocation) |
| 7 | Literal-truth defense | LM2 Q6 B ("true and accurate" but misleading) |
| 8 | Technical workaround | LM2 Q11 B (bought the ETF, not the stock), Q10 A (wait one day); LM3 Q35 (traded a peer company) |
| 9 | Good motive or client benefit | LM2 Q10 B, Q23 A, Q11 C |
| 10 | Wrong timing or order of steps | LM2 Q23 C, Q35 C; LM3 Q45 (research done after buying) |
| 11 | True statement that does not answer the question | LM2 Q8 A and C, Q20 B and C |
| 12 | Invented requirement | LM2 Q24 C, Q34 C; LM3 Q43 C |
| 13 | Concern is real but not prohibited | LM2 Q7 B and C, Q31 A and C |
| 14 | Decoy fact that looks important | LM3 Q4 B (political factors), Q14 B (big price impact), Q22 B (higher fee) |
| 15 | "I followed firm policy" or "lawyer said fine" | [CURRICULUM] Level III cases: firm policy is no defense (curriculum L5878, L5981) |
| 16 | Same facts, different time point | LM3 Q4 (keep manager during review: fine) vs Q6 (after review: fire) |

### How Level III frames ethics (LM3)

- [CURRICULUM] Learning outcomes: **evaluate** practices, policies, and conduct against the Code and Standards, and **explain** how they do or do not violate them (curriculum L5619 to L5622).
- [CURRICULUM] Exhibit 1, Framework for Ethical Decision Making (curriculum L5628 to L5636):
  1. **Identify:** relevant facts, stakeholders and duties owed, ethical principles, conflicts of interest.
  2. **Consider:** situational influences, additional guidance, alternative actions.
  3. **Decide and act.**
  4. **Reflect:** was the outcome as expected, and why?
  - The steps do not have to be in this order, but a review of the outcome should end the process (curriculum L5626).
- [CURRICULUM] Cases are set in private wealth and institutional asset management, and judge actions "taken or not taken": **omissions count** (curriculum L5638).
- [CURRICULUM] Lessons that repeat across the Level III cases:
  - Situational influences matter: political pressure, bonuses, a supervisor's pressure.
  - Following an employer policy is not a defense (curriculum L5878, L5981).
  - Disclosure in a prospectus is "necessary but not sufficient" (curriculum L5811, L5985).
  - Disclosure does not replace independent judgment (curriculum L5665).
  - Silence can be a violation (curriculum L5703 to L5707).
  - A client's request does not override suitability (curriculum L6110, L6126).
  - Supervisors must investigate, not just escalate or trust the employee (curriculum L5999, L6011).
  - When in doubt, go to compliance or legal counsel (curriculum L6101).
- [CURRICULUM] "The curriculum, including the practice questions, is the basis for all exam questions." (curriculum L852)

## 1.4 Step-by-step answering method

[DERIVED] This method combines the official framework (Identify, Consider, Decide and act, Reflect), the question patterns above, and provider tips. Sources are shown for each step.

**Before you start the set**
1. **Budget about 12 minutes per set** (132 minutes / 11 sets), about 3 minutes per question. [DERIVED]; same figure from [PROVIDER] https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf and [YOUTUBER] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MpfE43Xa9mA
2. **Skim the question stems first** so you know what to look for. Questions follow the vignette order. [PROVIDER] KYee deck; https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf

**Reading the vignette**

3. **Read chunk by chunk.** When a paragraph clearly sets up a question, answer that question, then read on ("segment and strike"). [YOUTUBER] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc
4. **For each person, note:** CFA status (charterholder, candidate, none), role (supervisor, analyst, PM), who the client is, and any firm policy. Use initials for long names. [CURRICULUM] the status decides who is bound; [YOUTUBER] W5vlKCv3djc
5. **Mark trigger words**, because a single word can decide the answer:
   - "most" vs "all", "will" vs "should"/"may", "immediately", "verbal" vs "written", "additional", "before" vs "after", "material", "possess".
   - Also: "reasonable" vs "extravagant" gifts, "knowingly", "intent".
   - Sources: [PROVIDER] KYee deck; [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q27 ("will" vs "should"), Q36 (verbal vs written); [YOUTUBER] W5vlKCv3djc, aT8sGNsyFo0, DZ3nkZcbVyo

**Answering each question (Identify, Consider, Decide)**

6. **Identify the act and the time point.** What exactly did the person do, and when (before or after leaving; before or after public release; before or after the review)? [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q4 vs Q6, Q7, Q13
7. **Identify who is owed the duty and who is harmed:** client, employer, market, profession. That points to the standard family: client = III, employer = IV, market = II. [PROVIDER] KYee deck
8. **Decide the verdict yourself before reading the options.** Judge from the story: did the person act right or wrong? [YOUTUBER] "SGP: Setup, Gut, Precision", W5vlKCv3djc; "form an answer in your head", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eyh4l5Y7iw
9. **Check precision:** the exact rule, its exceptions, whether the step is **required** or only **recommended**, and the stricter law. [PROVIDER] "Separate what the Standards require from good office practice", KYee deck; 300Hours https://300hours.com/cfa-ethics-tips/
10. **Then match the reason.** In "Yes, because X / Yes, because Y" items, the verdict alone is not enough: confirm that the reason and the standard are the right ones. [CURRICULUM] LM2 Q17, Q28; LM3 Q7, Q10
11. **Run the distractor checklist** (table in 1.3) on each wrong-looking option. Is it extreme? Is it disclosure offered as a cure? Is it the right verdict for the wrong reason? Does it invent a requirement? Is it a decoy fact? Does it contradict the facts given? (Options that contradict given facts "can never be the answer", [YOUTUBER] W5vlKCv3djc)

**Special stem types**

12. **"Best action" stems:** prefer the graduated, proportionate response: avoid over disclose, decline in writing, investigate, dissociate. The "least rigid and most rigid course of action is often not the best." [PROVIDER] https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf; [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q9, Q44; LM2 Q2, Q3, Q32
13. **"Least likely violated" stems:** find the one standard that is NOT implicated. Check all three options, because two will probably be violations. [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q29, Q37, Q40; [YOUTUBER] aT8sGNsyFo0
14. **Grid stems:** judge each item on its own, then find the row. [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q8, Q11, Q12, Q16 to Q18
15. **Do not invent facts.** If nothing shows bad intent, assume intent was fine; a wrong number may be a typo. [YOUTUBER] DZ3nkZcbVyo, t7rq5_qdUfM
16. **Remember the base rate.** About 28% of official LM3 answers are "no violation." [CURRICULUM] count in 1.3

**Finishing**

17. **Answer every question** (no penalty), flag doubtful ones, and move on when the 12 minutes are up. [OFFICIAL] Level III exam page; [PROVIDER] https://www.cfala.org/files/Test_Taking_Skills_2024(1).pdf
18. **If ethics appears in an essay:** state the verdict and give one-line reasons. Write up the **primary** violation, give only as many reasons as asked, and do not recite standard numbers. [PROVIDER] Test Taking Skills 2024 deck; EJohnsen deck

## 1.5 Study plan for someone who keeps scoring low

### Step 0: Diagnose

- [OFFICIAL] Use your topic results: below 50% means "substantial remediation"; consistently above 70% is a sign of mastery (Understanding Your Exam Results PDF).
- [YOUTUBER] A candidate stuck at about 50% after a month of 1 to 1.5 hours a day was told: "You don't need to practice more questions, just analyze the questions you have done." https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc
- [DERIVED] Before doing new questions, sort your past misses into error types (see the error log below). Low scorers usually miss for a few repeated reasons, not because of random gaps.

### Principles backed by evidence

| Principle | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Practice testing** (answering questions, recalling rules from memory) and **spaced practice** work best | [RESEARCH] Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated only these two as "high utility"; rereading, highlighting and summarizing were "low utility." https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html |
| **Mixed (interleaved) practice** beats practicing one topic at a time | [RESEARCH] Rohrer, Dedrick and Stershic (2014): better test scores 1 day (d = 0.42) and 30 days (d = 0.79) later, because you learn to tell which method fits a problem. https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2014-rohrer-2.pdf |
| Why mixing fits ethics | [DERIVED] Mixed-standard vignettes train you to identify which Standard applies, which CFALA calls the hardest part: "complexity is with overlapping standards." https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf |
| Review decides the score | [PROVIDER] "Ethics points are decided in your review of missed questions - not in the reading." https://www.finquiz.com/blog/cfa-ethics-questions-tips-guide/ |
| The curriculum is the source | [CURRICULUM] "The curriculum, including the practice questions, is the basis for all exam questions." Use the LOS as a self-check before and after study |

### Material priority

1. Curriculum LM2 Guidance examples (the "Application of the Standard" examples) and LM2 practice problems.
2. Curriculum LM3 Level III cases and the 8 LM3 item sets.
3. LM4 Asset Manager Code and its practice problems.
4. CFA Institute Learning Ecosystem questions and the two official mocks (available 8 weeks before the exam). [OFFICIAL] https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa-program/candidate-resources/mock-exam-and-practice-questions
5. Extra official cases: Ethics in Practice Casebook (100+ scenarios). [OFFICIAL] https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/ethics-resources
6. Prep-provider question banks last. [YOUTUBER] FinTree says Schweser questions give "a false sense of confidence" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lAk6orRwPV8). [CANDIDATE] AnalystForum: QBank ethics is "easier than the CFAI vignettes" (https://www.analystforum.com/t/how-do-you-study-for-ethics/115137).

### The error log (core tool)

[DERIVED] Keep one row per missed question, and also per question you got right by guessing. [YOUTUBER] "Check your correct answers too: was it skill or luck?" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fj_ug-noWt8

| Column | What to write |
|---|---|
| Source and date | e.g., LM3 Q22, 12 Nov |
| Standard(s) tested | e.g., VI(C) Referral Fees |
| My answer / correct answer | B / C |
| Error type | One of: misread a fact or word; wrong time point; wrong standard; right verdict wrong reason; required vs recommended mixed up; overreaction; disclosure treated as a cure; invented fact or requirement; knowledge gap; ran out of time |
| The rule in one line, in my own words | "Saying 'I get a referral fee' is not enough: disclose its nature and estimated dollar value" |
| Curriculum line | (curriculum L6897) |
| Re-test dates | When you will answer it again from memory |

[DERIVED] Every week, count the error types. If most misses are "misread" or "decoy fact", fix your reading method (1.4, steps 3 to 6). If they are "wrong standard", do more mixed sets. If they are "knowledge gap", go back to the Guidance examples for that standard.

[PROVIDER] Writing out why each option is right or wrong "helped a lot." https://www.analystforum.com/t/regular-70-ethics-performers-advice/123546

### Active recall drills

- **Blank-page drill:** from memory, write each standard in plain language, plus its key requirement and one exception. Then check against the curriculum. [RESEARCH] practice testing; [YOUTUBER] write your own example of each standard's required and recommended conduct (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gg6Vrn45Oxo)
- **Example flip:** read only the facts of a Guidance example, decide the verdict and reason, then read the comment. [PROVIDER] "Read the full Code and Standards especially the examples", CFALA 2025 deck; [CANDIDATE] go through "every word" of the examples
- **Play with the variables:** for each example ask, "if this fact changed, would the verdict change?" [YOUTUBER] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ug9-r1KILf4
- **Flag "looks like a violation but is not" cases** and review them before the exam. [CANDIDATE] https://www.analystforum.com/t/how-to-study-ethics/44353
- **Redrawable mind map:** one page of the Code and Standards you can redraw from memory in about 10 minutes. [YOUTUBER] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmlzrKbCHPg

### Spaced and mixed practice

- [PROVIDER] Daily 10 to 15 minutes of mixed-standard vignettes; about 30 to 40 hours of ethics over four months. https://acadifi.com/community/cfa-level-3-ethics-study-strategy

- [DERIVED] Re-test each error-log item a few days later and again about a week later, from memory, before rereading the answer. Mix items from different standards in each session.
- [DERIVED] When to study ethics: sources disagree on "ethics last" vs "start early." For a repeat low scorer, the spacing evidence favors light daily practice from the start plus a heavy final review. [PROVIDER] ethics last so it is fresh (300Hours; former grader in the Test Taking Skills 2024 deck); start early and review daily (Salt Solutions, https://www.saltsolutions.com/blog/cfa-study-tips/cfa-ethics-and-professional-standards-deep-dive-and-study-tips/)

## 1.6 The most common traps

Rules come from the curriculum solutions. The "flagged by" column shows where research or YouTube also stressed the trap.

| # | Trap | Correct rule | Curriculum | Also flagged by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Required vs recommended | Only the required part is a violation if missing; recommended procedures are best practice | LM3 Q36 (written report is recommended; written consent is required) | 300Hours; KYee deck; YouTube 9_As0t5P4fU, Gg6Vrn45Oxo |
| 2 | "It is legal here" | Follow the stricter of law and the Standards; legal is not the same as ethical | (curriculum L1102); LM2 Q1; LM3 Q17 | Handbook; LalyW-mSiU4, blRxYLK7K6g |
| 3 | Disclosure as a cure | Disclosure does not fix a banned act or replace needed consent; avoid first under VI(A) | LM2 Q2, Q17, Q26; (curriculum L959) | KYee deck caveat to "when in doubt, disclose" |
| 4 | Possessing vs acting on MNPI | Possessing or overhearing is not II(A); acting is. Eavesdropping is I(D) | LM3 Q34, Q35 | DZ3nkZcbVyo ("possess" keyword) |
| 5 | Waiting for the slowest channel | Once released publicly (e.g., wire service), you may trade | LM3 Q13 | |
| 6 | Trading a related company | Using the same MNPI on a peer stock or an ETF is still acting on MNPI | LM3 Q35; LM2 Q11 | MnttDw7EX4Q |
| 7 | Price impact = manipulation | Manipulation needs intent to mislead | LM3 Q14 | DZ3nkZcbVyo, HLpRMhMPEX4 |
| 8 | Fair = equal | Different service levels are fine if disclosed and nobody is disadvantaged | LM3 Q30, Q32 | CFALA 2025 deck; DZ3nkZcbVyo |
| 9 | Telling the client later | IPS deviation is not allowed "under no circumstances"; telling the client afterwards does not cure it | LM3 Q2 | |
| 10 | Trade-error fixes | The client never bears the loss; no interest debited from accounts that should have got shares; suitability checked before allocation | LM3 Q3, Q5 | |
| 11 | Overreacting | Resigning, terminating, or suspending at once is rarely the best answer; decline in writing, investigate, dissociate | LM3 Q4, Q9, Q44 | CFALA 2025 deck; Acadifi; Ar8-0Oogeuo |
| 12 | Supervisor "did enough" | Writing procedures is not enough; they must be enforced; a warning is not enough after a violation; delegating is allowed | LM3 Q12, Q23, Q31 | CFALA Jul26 deck |
| 13 | Verbal or "discussed" consent | Outside compensation that may conflict needs **written** employer consent | LM3 Q12, Q36 | YouTube on gifts tied to future performance (W5vlKCv3djc) |
| 14 | Referral fee "mentioned" | Disclose nature and estimated dollar value, before the relationship starts | LM3 Q22; LM2 Q35 | CFALA 2025 deck |
| 15 | Materiality filter on conflicts | Disclose all conflicts that could reasonably impair objectivity, prominently and in plain language, not just those you judge material | LM3 Q11, Q18 | |
| 16 | Job offers and friendships | A prior relationship or job offer is a conflict to disclose when recommending a firm | LM3 Q38 | |
| 17 | Permission cures plagiarism | Written permission does not remove the duty to attribute | LM3 Q16 | |
| 18 | Performance presentation | Include terminated accounts; state gross or net of fees; simulated results are OK if labeled; no partial GIPS claim | LM3 Q10, Q24, Q26, Q28 | YouTube 8KhntljYnrQ, UZwVCjyk4uE |
| 19 | Fact vs opinion | "Will" presented as certain violates V(B); "should" signals opinion | LM3 Q27 | aT8sGNsyFo0 |
| 20 | Personal trades and V(A) | V(A) does not apply to personal trades; for clients, "strong buy" from a third party or speed alone is no reasonable basis, and research done afterwards does not cure it | LM3 Q35, Q45 | |
| 21 | Family accounts | In oversubscribed IPOs, accounts with beneficial interest (even a family discretionary account) are excluded; fee-paying family clients are treated like other clients | LM3 Q46 | ZeKYZYJQBGg, -JAwzhU-A0o |
| 22 | Leaving an employer | Preparing is fine; soliciting before leaving is not; after leaving (no noncompete) it is fine; intern status is irrelevant | LM3 Q7, Q39; LM2 Q25, Q26 | CFALA 2025 deck; qd_2KJKO-8c |
| 23 | CFA designation wording | No partial designation or expected completion date; "Level III candidate" is fine; "as a CFA charterholder I am the best qualified" violates | LM3 Q21, Q42; LM2 Q37 to Q39 | nYIQmXyINfU, t7rq5_qdUfM |
| 24 | Pension clients | The duty runs to the beneficiaries, not trustees or the sponsor | LM3 Q1; LM2 Q13 | |
| 25 | Selectively shared MNPI | Keep it confidential and do not use it; publicizing it is not your role | LM3 Q44 | |
| 26 | Confidentiality exceptions | Illegal activity, required by law, client permission. Sharing with the CFA Institute Professional Conduct Program is not a violation | (curriculum L1160 to L1168) | W5vlKCv3djc ("trap answer") |
| 27 | Code and Standards vs AMC | Code and Standards bind individuals (firms may adopt them); the AMC is for firms; no partial AMC claim | LM3 Q8; (curriculum L6999) | CFALA Jul26 deck |

### Useful YouTuber techniques (opinion)

- **SGP:** Setup (understand the story), Gut (decide right or wrong; he claims this handles about 70% of questions), Precision (exact rule, for exceptions). In one worked 4-question set, 3 were solvable by story and gut. (W5vlKCv3djc)
- **Two "cheat codes"** when stuck between two answers: did the person disclose, and did they follow the stricter rule? (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA)
- **Answers that contradict the given facts** can never be correct. (W5vlKCv3djc)
- **Speed tactic** for three-statement items: once one statement is clearly a violation and one clearly is not, answer. (W5vlKCv3djc) [DERIVED] Risky: the third statement can change the answer.
