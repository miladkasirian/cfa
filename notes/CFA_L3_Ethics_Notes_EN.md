## 1.2 What changed in the 2026 curriculum

### The four Level III ethics learning modules

1. Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (LM1)
2. Guidance for Standards I-VII (LM2)
3. Application of the Code and Standards: Level III (LM3)
4. Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct (LM4)

Source: [CURRICULUM] Volume 5 table of contents; [OFFICIAL] https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/application-of-the-code-and-standards-level-iii ; [PROVIDER] IFT listing https://ift.world/?p=264154

### The 2023 revisions (effective 1 January 2024), now in the curriculum

| Change | New text or rule | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **New I(E) Competence** | "Members and Candidates must act with and maintain the competence necessary to fulfill their professional responsibilities." | (curriculum L1121) |
| I(E) detail | Competence criteria differ by role; facts and circumstances decide. I(E) does **not** require any specific professional development or continuing education program | (curriculum L947 to L948) |
| I(E) vs the Code | The Code says "maintain and improve their professional competence"; I(E) says "act with and maintain." I(E) makes the Code's requirement explicit | (curriculum L946) |
| **V(B) costs disclosure** | V(B)(1) now requires disclosing "the nature of the services provided, along with information about the costs to the client associated with those services." | (curriculum L1196, L929) |
| **VI(A) renamed** | Now "Avoid or Disclose Conflicts": "must avoid or make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity..." Disclosures must be prominent, in plain language, and effective | (curriculum L1217, L931) |
| VI(A) priority | Avoid conflicts "whenever reasonably possible"; avoiding is preferred; when avoiding is not reasonable, clear and complete disclosure is still required | (curriculum L959) |

Source for the summary: [CURRICULUM] "Summary of the 2023 Revisions to the Code and Standards" (curriculum L925 to L959); [OFFICIAL] https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2024/01/11/revisions-to-the-cfa-institute-code-of-ethics-and-standards-of-professional-conduct/

### Standards of Practice Handbook edition

- [OFFICIAL] The governing Handbook is the **12th edition (2024)**; the 11th edition "is no longer the governing document." https://www.cfainstitute.org/ethics-standards/codes/standards-of-practice-guidance (PDF: https://www.CFAinstitute.org/-/media/documents/code/code-ethics-standards/standards-practice-handbook-12th-edition.pdf)

- [DERIVED] Rule for you: answer every question using the current standards (I(E), V(B) with costs, VI(A) avoid or disclose).
- [CANDIDATE] A YouTube commenter claimed a curriculum module tells candidates to follow the 11th edition (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, comment 99). This conflicts with the official page above. Treat it as wrong for the current standards.

### Asset Manager Code (AMC) is a Level III module

- [CURRICULUM] LM4 outcomes: explain the AMC's purpose and benefits; explain the six General Principles of Conduct; determine whether a manager's practices are consistent with the AMC; recommend practices that prevent violations (curriculum L6971 to L6974).
- [CURRICULUM] The AMC applies to **firms** ("Managers"), not individuals; it is **voluntary**; it covers all employees; it should be adopted by senior management and the board (curriculum L6980 to L6986).
- [CURRICULUM] Six areas: loyalty to clients, investment process and actions, trading, risk management/compliance/support, performance and valuation, disclosures (curriculum L7012 onward). The second edition added risk management provisions and guidance on claiming compliance (curriculum L6985).
- [CURRICULUM] Compliance is all or nothing: a firm cannot claim partial compliance (curriculum L6998 to L7000).
- [PROVIDER] "If one masters the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct, AMC questions should be relatively easy." Read the AMC module quickly and do its practice problems. https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf
- [YOUTUBER] Almost no YouTube video covers the AMC in detail (one 2021 Mark Meldrum video called it a list that "doesn't seem intuitive", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jJTopkPmPms). Use the curriculum for it.

## 1.3 How official item-set questions are built

Based on the 2026 curriculum practice problems: LM2 (39 questions, curriculum L5119 to L5613) and LM3 (8 item sets, 47 questions, curriculum L6131 to L6963).

### Vignette build (LM3 item sets)

- Opens with "The following information relates to questions X-Y."
- About 490 to 990 words; most 500 to 760.
- 2 to 6 named people. At least one CFA charterholder and often a candidate. CFA status is always stated, because it decides who is bound.
- Told in time order, in paragraph "chunks." Each question points to one chunk ("When meeting with Vanderon...", "When shorting Tefla stock..."). Questions follow the story order.
- Often embeds structured material: quoted dialogue, a bulleted draft policy, numbered "Statement 1 to 4" or "Procedure 1 to 3", a fee table, a performance exhibit, or compliance-manual definitions.
- Small numbers are planted for light math (fee vs commissions saved; $25.00 vs $25.01).
- Some facts are decoys with no question, or tempting reasons that do not matter (political pressure; "the price was distorted").
- Each set touches about 5 to 8 different standards. One question can test two standards.
- Curriculum sets have 5 or 6 questions; the live exam has 4 per set [OFFICIAL]. The style is the same.

### Stem types and how often they appear

| Stem type | Example form | Count in LM3 (47 Qs) | Also in LM2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Violation yes/no with reasons | "When [action], did [person] violate any of the Standards?" A: No / B: Yes, because... / C: Yes, because... (or the reverse: "Is X consistent?" A: Yes / B, C: No, because...) | about 22 | Yes, the dominant form |
| Two-item grid | Table with columns for two items; rows give three of the four Yes/No combinations (usually No/No, No/Yes, Yes/No) | 6 | Two-statement "neither / only I / only II" (Q5) |
| Compound consistency | "Are X and Y both consistent?" Yes / only X / only Y | about 5 | |
| Best or most appropriate action | "Locke's best course of action", "Riser should:" | 6 | "best course", "best policy" |
| Least likely violated | Options are three standard names; pick the one NOT implicated | 3 | "least likely to violate" |
| Negative requirement | "Riser is not required to disclose to clients his:" | 1 | "which statement violates" |
| Duty owed | "Locke owes his primary duty of loyalty to:" | 1 | "duty is owed to:" |
| No stem | Facts then three full-sentence options; implied "which is correct" | 0 | 7 |

Sources: [CURRICULUM] the item-set and practice-problem sections of the curriculum, L6131 to L6963 and L5119 to L5613.

### How the options are built

- The bare "No" (no violation) option is usually **A**. The two "Yes" options carry "because..." or "relating to [standard]."
- Often **two options share the same verdict** and differ only in the reason. The reason decides the answer.
- A right verdict with a wrong reason is a wrong answer (LM3 Q7: option C says "Yes, because he was an unpaid intern": right verdict, wrong reason).
- Sometimes the verdict is fixed and all three options say "No, because..." (LM3 Q10). You only pick the reason.
- Grid and least-likely items have no reasons.
- Recommended procedures are tested by asking which action or policy is "most consistent", not by asking you to list them.

### How often the answer is "no violation"

| Source | Result |
|---|---|
| LM3 item sets | **13 of 47 (about 28%)** are "no violation" or "consistent" (Q4, Q7, Q13, Q14, Q19, Q20, Q24, Q25, Q30, Q39, Q42, Q43, Q46). In 12 of 13 the answer is option A; Q7 is the exception (B) |
| LM2 questions about a person's conduct | 12 violation, 3 no violation, 1 split verdict (of 16) |
| Answer letters, LM3 | A 18, B 16, C 13 |
| Answer letters, LM2 | A 9, B 19, C 11 |

- [DERIVED] About one question in four says the conduct was fine, and those vignettes are deliberately loaded with suspicious-looking facts. Never assume a violation because something "feels" wrong.
- [DERIVED] Answer letters are not a pattern to rely on.

| 3 | Overreaction: too strict or extreme | LM2 Q9 A ("permanently"); LM3 Q4 C (suspend immediately), Q9 A (resign), Q44 B (terminate broker) |

### How Level III frames ethics (LM3)

- [CURRICULUM] Learning outcomes: **evaluate** practices, policies, and conduct against the Code and Standards, and **explain** how they do or do not violate them (curriculum L5619 to L5622).
- [CURRICULUM] Exhibit 1, Framework for Ethical Decision Making (curriculum L5628 to L5636):
  1. **Identify:** relevant facts, stakeholders and duties owed, ethical principles, conflicts of interest.
  2. **Consider:** situational influences, additional guidance, alternative actions.
  3. **Decide and act.**
  4. **Reflect:** was the outcome as expected, and why?
  - The steps do not have to be in this order, but a review of the outcome should end the process (curriculum L5626).
- [CURRICULUM] Cases are set in private wealth and institutional asset management, and judge actions "taken or not taken": **omissions count** (curriculum L5638).
- [CURRICULUM] Lessons that repeat across the Level III cases:
  - Situational influences matter: political pressure, bonuses, a supervisor's pressure.
  - Following an employer policy is not a defense (curriculum L5878, L5981).
  - Disclosure in a prospectus is "necessary but not sufficient" (curriculum L5811, L5985).
  - Disclosure does not replace independent judgment (curriculum L5665).
  - Silence can be a violation (curriculum L5703 to L5707).
  - A client's request does not override suitability (curriculum L6110, L6126).
  - Supervisors must investigate, not just escalate or trust the employee (curriculum L5999, L6011).
  - When in doubt, go to compliance or legal counsel (curriculum L6101).
- [CURRICULUM] "The curriculum, including the practice questions, is the basis for all exam questions." (curriculum L852)

## 1.4 Step-by-step answering method

[DERIVED] This method combines the official framework (Identify, Consider, Decide and act, Reflect), the question patterns above, and provider tips. Sources are shown for each step.

**Before you start the set**
1. **Budget about 12 minutes per set** (132 minutes / 11 sets), about 3 minutes per question. [DERIVED]; same figure from [PROVIDER] https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf and [YOUTUBER] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MpfE43Xa9mA
2. **Skim the question stems first** so you know what to look for. Questions follow the vignette order. [PROVIDER] KYee deck; https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf

**Reading the vignette**

3. **Read chunk by chunk.** When a paragraph clearly sets up a question, answer that question, then read on ("segment and strike"). [YOUTUBER] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc
4. **For each person, note:** CFA status (charterholder, candidate, none), role (supervisor, analyst, PM), who the client is, and any firm policy. Use initials for long names. [CURRICULUM] the status decides who is bound; [YOUTUBER] W5vlKCv3djc
5. **Mark trigger words**, because a single word can decide the answer:
   - "most" vs "all", "will" vs "should"/"may", "immediately", "verbal" vs "written", "additional", "before" vs "after", "material", "possess".
   - Also: "reasonable" vs "extravagant" gifts, "knowingly", "intent".
   - Sources: [PROVIDER] KYee deck; [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q27 ("will" vs "should"), Q36 (verbal vs written); [YOUTUBER] W5vlKCv3djc, aT8sGNsyFo0, DZ3nkZcbVyo

**Answering each question (Identify, Consider, Decide)**

6. **Identify the act and the time point.** What exactly did the person do, and when (before or after leaving; before or after public release; before or after the review)? [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q4 vs Q6, Q7, Q13
7. **Identify who is owed the duty and who is harmed:** client, employer, market, profession. That points to the standard family: client = III, employer = IV, market = II. [PROVIDER] KYee deck
8. **Decide the verdict yourself before reading the options.** Judge from the story: did the person act right or wrong? [YOUTUBER] "SGP: Setup, Gut, Precision", W5vlKCv3djc; "form an answer in your head", https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eyh4l5Y7iw
9. **Check precision:** the exact rule, its exceptions, whether the step is **required** or only **recommended**, and the stricter law. [PROVIDER] "Separate what the Standards require from good office practice", KYee deck; 300Hours https://300hours.com/cfa-ethics-tips/
10. **Then match the reason.** In "Yes, because X / Yes, because Y" items, the verdict alone is not enough: confirm that the reason and the standard are the right ones. [CURRICULUM] LM2 Q17, Q28; LM3 Q7, Q10
11. **Run the distractor checklist** (table in 1.3) on each wrong-looking option. Is it extreme? Is it disclosure offered as a cure? Is it the right verdict for the wrong reason? Does it invent a requirement? Is it a decoy fact? Does it contradict the facts given? (Options that contradict given facts "can never be the answer", [YOUTUBER] W5vlKCv3djc)

**Special stem types**

12. **"Best action" stems:** prefer the graduated, proportionate response: avoid over disclose, decline in writing, investigate, dissociate. The "least rigid and most rigid course of action is often not the best." [PROVIDER] https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf; [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q9, Q44; LM2 Q2, Q3, Q32
13. **"Least likely violated" stems:** find the one standard that is NOT implicated. Check all three options, because two will probably be violations. [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q29, Q37, Q40; [YOUTUBER] aT8sGNsyFo0
14. **Grid stems:** judge each item on its own, then find the row. [CURRICULUM] LM3 Q8, Q11, Q12, Q16 to Q18
15. **Do not invent facts.** If nothing shows bad intent, assume intent was fine; a wrong number may be a typo. [YOUTUBER] DZ3nkZcbVyo, t7rq5_qdUfM
16. **Remember the base rate.** About 28% of official LM3 answers are "no violation." [CURRICULUM] count in 1.3

**Finishing**

17. **Answer every question** (no penalty), flag doubtful ones, and move on when the 12 minutes are up. [OFFICIAL] Level III exam page; [PROVIDER] https://www.cfala.org/files/Test_Taking_Skills_2024(1).pdf
18. **If ethics appears in an essay:** state the verdict and give one-line reasons. Write up the **primary** violation, give only as many reasons as asked, and do not recite standard numbers. [PROVIDER] Test Taking Skills 2024 deck; EJohnsen deck

## 1.5 Study plan for someone who keeps scoring low

### Step 0: Diagnose

- [OFFICIAL] Use your topic results: below 50% means "substantial remediation"; consistently above 70% is a sign of mastery (Understanding Your Exam Results PDF).
- [YOUTUBER] A candidate stuck at about 50% after a month of 1 to 1.5 hours a day was told: "You don't need to practice more questions, just analyze the questions you have done." https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc
- [DERIVED] Before doing new questions, sort your past misses into error types (see the error log below). Low scorers usually miss for a few repeated reasons, not because of random gaps.

### Principles backed by evidence

| Principle | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Practice testing** (answering questions, recalling rules from memory) and **spaced practice** work best | [RESEARCH] Dunlosky et al. (2013) rated only these two as "high utility"; rereading, highlighting and summarizing were "low utility." https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html |
| **Mixed (interleaved) practice** beats practicing one topic at a time | [RESEARCH] Rohrer, Dedrick and Stershic (2014): better test scores 1 day (d = 0.42) and 30 days (d = 0.79) later, because you learn to tell which method fits a problem. https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2014-rohrer-2.pdf |
| Why mixing fits ethics | [DERIVED] Mixed-standard vignettes train you to identify which Standard applies, which CFALA calls the hardest part: "complexity is with overlapping standards." https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf |

## 2.1 Background: Preface, Professional Conduct Program, adoption

### 2.1.1 The Handbook and the Code vs the Standards
- The Standards of Practice Handbook is the main CFA Institute resource for interpreting and applying the Code and Standards (curriculum L913).
- **Code** = high-level, aspirational ethical principles. **Standards** = practical rules of conduct that members and candidates must follow (curriculum L902).
- Read the Code and Standards together, "as an interwoven tapestry", not one principle at a time (curriculum L902).
- Handbook Application Examples: names are fictional; **unless stated otherwise, everyone in an example is subject to the Code and Standards**; examples are not all inclusive (curriculum L915).
- Members are "strongly urged" (RECOMMENDED) to discuss the Code and Standards with supervisors and legal and compliance staff (curriculum L915 to L919).
- A code of ethics can create a "false sense of security" unless it is understood, enforced and used every day (curriculum L921).
- Members and candidates are **required** to follow the Code and Standards. Firms and regulators often adopt them too (curriculum L909).

### 2.1.2 The 2023 revisions (three areas: one new standard, two revised)
Approved by the Board of Governors in 2023 (curriculum L908, L925). Effective 1 January 2024 per the CFA Institute blog (official web source, not the curriculum: https://blogs.cfainstitute.org/blog/2024/01/11/revisions-to-the-cfa-institute-code-of-ethics-and-standards-of-professional-conduct/).

| Change | What it does | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| NEW I(E) Competence | Must act with and maintain the competence needed for one's role (curriculum L927) | Role specific, judged on facts and circumstances; **no specific continuing education program is required** (curriculum L947 to L948) |
| REVISED V(B) | Must disclose the **nature of services** and the **costs to the client** of those services (curriculum L929) | Financial impact was "not explicitly addressed" before; the revision fills that gap (curriculum L953) |
| REVISED and RENAMED VI(A) "Avoid or Disclose Conflicts" | Must **avoid** conflicts **or** disclose them (curriculum L931 to L933) | Avoid whenever reasonably possible; if avoiding is not reasonable, clear and complete disclosure is still required (curriculum L959). Avoidance was already "best practice" in the old guidance |

### 2.1.3 CFA Institute Professional Conduct Program (PCP)
Who does what (curriculum L963, L987):

| Body | Role |
|---|---|
| Board of Governors | Oversight of the PCP; approved the 2023 revisions |
| PCP (Professional Conduct staff) together with the Disciplinary Review Committee (DRC) | Enforce the Code and Standards; staff investigate |
| DRC | Volunteer CFA charterholders who sit on hearing panels and help set conduct policies |
| Standards of Practice Council (SPC) | CFA charterholder volunteers from many countries who **maintain and interpret** the Code and Standards |
| Bylaws and Rules of Procedure for Professional Conduct | The basic structure for enforcement |

- Professional Conduct also enforces testing policies of other CFA Institute programs and the conduct of CIPM certificants (curriculum L963).

**Five sources of inquiries** (curriculum L964):
1. Self-disclosure on the annual Professional Conduct Statement (mandatory: e.g., civil litigation, criminal investigation, written complaint).
2. Written complaints.
3. Evidence of questionable conduct in the media, regulatory notices or other public sources.
4. Proctor reports on exam day.
5. Post-exam analysis of scores and materials, and monitoring of online and social media.

**Investigation** (curriculum L968): staff **may** request a written explanation, interview the member or candidate, the complainant and third parties, and collect documents.

**Three possible staff outcomes** (curriculum L968): (1) close with no sanction, (2) cautionary letter, (3) continue proceedings to discipline.

**Accept or reject**: if staff believe a violation occurred, the person may accept or reject the charges and proposed sanction. **Only if rejected** does the case go to a DRC hearing panel, which decides whether a violation occurred and what sanction applies (curriculum L968 to L969).

**Sanctions** (curriculum L970): members: public censure, suspension of membership and of the right to use the CFA designation, revocation of the charter. Candidates: suspension or prohibition from further participation in the CFA Program. The Preamble words it as revocation of membership, of candidacy, and of the right to use the CFA designation (curriculum L1074).

### 2.1.4 Adoption of the Code and Standards
- The Code and Standards apply to **individuals** (members and candidates). Firms are **encouraged**, not required, to adopt them (curriculum L974).
- A firm or nonmember claiming compliance should state: "[Name] claims compliance with the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct. This claim has not been verified by CFA Institute." (curriculum L977)
- The **Asset Manager Code** applies to **firms**, and gives guidance in six areas: loyalty to clients, the investment process, trading, compliance, performance, and disclosure (curriculum L980).
- The SPC tests the Code and Standards against six criteria: high standards of conduct, relevant to a changing profession, globally applicable, comprehensive, practical and specific, enforceable, and testable for the CFA Program (curriculum L988 to L1000).

### 2.1.5 Ethics and the investment industry (key ideas)
- **Ethics** = moral principles or rules of conduct that guide behavior when it affects others. Widely accepted principles: honesty, fairness, diligence, and care and respect for others (curriculum L1010).
- **Legal** = what is required; **ethical** = what is morally correct. Laws do not cover all unethical behavior, and regulators lack resources, so law alone is not enough (curriculum L1037 to L1039).
- Without trust, investors stay out of markets or demand a **higher risk premium**, which cuts capital, innovation and jobs (curriculum L1022).
- Ethics for a global industry should be universal and rank above acceptable local customs (curriculum L1023).
- People in authority have a special duty to consider market sustainability, especially in **risk management and product development**; pay schemes should not push people into unethical conduct (curriculum L1033).
- A strong culture of integrity shown by senior management "may be the single most important factor" for ethical behavior; a code of ethics is necessary but not sufficient (curriculum L1053).
- Build an ethical framework **before** you need to act; practice builds "muscle memory" (curriculum L1043, L1054).
- "Situational influences can overpower the best of intentions" (curriculum L1065).

### 2.1.6 Preamble
- The Code and Standards date from the 1960s (curriculum L1074) and have bound members for "more than 60 years" (curriculum L1059). They have been revised over time, so they are **not unchanged** since creation (LM1 PP3).
- Members and candidates have a personal responsibility to uphold them and are **encouraged** (RECOMMENDED) to tell their employer about this responsibility (curriculum L1074).

## 2.5 Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law

### Standard text (curriculum L1100 to L1102)
> "Members and Candidates must understand and comply with all applicable laws, rules, and regulations (including the CFA Institute Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct) of any government, regulatory organization, licensing agency, or professional association governing their professional activities. In the event of conflict, Members and Candidates must comply with the more strict law, rule, or regulation. Members and Candidates must not knowingly participate or assist in and must dissociate from any violation of such laws, rules, or regulations."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- **Understand and comply** with applicable laws, rules and regulations, including the Code and Standards, based on a reasonable and good faith understanding (curriculum L1423).
- When law and the Code differ, follow the **more strict** one. If there is **no law**, follow the Code and Standards (curriculum L1431, L1443).
- Do **not knowingly participate or assist** in a violation, and **dissociate** from it (curriculum L1102).
- Dissociation trigger: "reasonable grounds to believe" that imminent or ongoing client or employer activity is illegal or unethical (curriculum L1457).
- I(A) applies when you **know or should know** your conduct may contribute to a violation (curriculum L1455).
- Members must follow CFA Institute's Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws, Code and Standards, Rules of Procedure, Membership Agreement and other rules. Non-member candidates follow the same **except** the Membership Agreement, **plus** the exam rules, the Candidate Responsibility Statement and the Candidate Pledge (curriculum L1447 to L1451).

### Key definitions
- **Applicable law**: the law that governs the member's conduct; which one depends on the facts (curriculum L1433).
- **More strict**: puts greater limits on the action, or requires more action that protects investors (e.g., the Code requires referral fee disclosure even where law does not) (curriculum L1433).
- Law is the **minimum threshold**; going beyond it supports I(A) (curriculum L1445).

### NOT required
- Being a compliance expert, or knowing every law that could possibly apply (curriculum L1425).
- Reporting violations to regulators, unless the law requires it. Voluntary reporting ("whistleblowing") may be prudent; consult legal and compliance (curriculum L1459).

### Dissociation steps (escalation order)
1. Talk to the person committing the violation.
2. If that fails, bring it to the employer through a supervisor or compliance (the first formal step) (curriculum L1457).
3. If that fails, step away: remove your name from reports, ask for another assignment, refuse a new client or stop advising one (curriculum L1457).
4. Document the violation and urge the firm to stop the wrongdoers (RECOMMENDED, curriculum L1573).
5. In extreme cases, leave the job (curriculum L1457).
- **Inaction plus continued association can count as participation** (curriculum L1457).
- Reporting fellow members to CFA Institute is **strongly encouraged** (RECOMMENDED). Failing to report is **less likely** to be a violation than failing to dissociate (curriculum L1459).
- Anyone can file a written complaint with the PCP by e-mail or website (curriculum L1463).

### Investment products and cross-border work
- People who create or maintain products should understand the laws where products **originate** and where they are **expected to be sold**, and make reasonable efforts to check that distributing firms obey local laws (curriculum L1467).
- When unsure which law applies, ask compliance, legal, or outside counsel (curriculum L1468).

### Which law applies: three-country table (curriculum L1470 to L1547)
NS = no securities law; LS = less strict than the Code; MS = more strict than the Code. Method: first find the applicable law (a home rule may point to the law where business is done, or the client's home country), then follow the stricter of that law and the Code.

| Lives in | Does business in | Special rule | Follow |
|---|---|---|---|
| NS | LS | none | Code (LS law is weaker) |
| NS | MS | none | MS law |
| LS | NS | none | Code |
| LS | MS | none | MS law |
| LS | NS | LS law says local law governs | Code (no local law) |
| LS | MS | LS law says local law governs | MS law |
| MS | LS | MS law applies | MS law |
| MS | LS | MS law says local law governs | Code (local law is LS) |
| MS | LS, client is LS citizen | MS law says client's home law governs | Code |
| MS | LS, client is MS citizen | MS law says client's home law governs | MS law |

### Recommended procedures (all RECOMMENDED)
Members and candidates (curriculum L1553 to L1573):
- Stay informed: set up, or ask the employer to set up, regular updates on changes in law, rules and case law (compliance memos, training).
- Review the firm's written compliance procedures regularly.
- Keep current reference copies of key statutes, rules and cases.
- Make reasonable efforts to understand laws where products are developed and likely distributed.
- When in doubt, ask compliance or legal counsel, including when a colleague may be breaking the law.
- Dissociation: document the violation and urge the firm to persuade wrongdoers to stop; resignation may be needed.

Firms (curriculum L1579 to L1585):
- Adopt a code of ethics (the Asset Manager Code can be the basis); ethical culture starts at the top and can prevent the need for public whistleblowing.
- Provide information on applicable laws (central location or distribution).
- Set written procedures for reporting suspected violations.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notification of known violations (Allen, overstated earnings in an underwriting) | Counsel's view that the regulator could not prove it does **not** excuse him | Report to supervisor, get an independent legal opinion, decide whether to notify the regulator. Legal advice is not a safe harbor |
| 2 | Dissociating from a violation (Brown, issuer hid foreign losses) | Must act | Report to supervisors; if the firm does not dissociate from the underwriting, he must sever all ties with it; get legal advice on more reporting |
| 3 | Dissociating from a violation (Washington, composite omits terminated accounts) | Using it would assist a violation | Raise it, then go to supervisor or compliance; if not fixed, refuse to use the material and tell the firm why; consider leaving if the firm insists |
| 4 | Following the highest requirements (Collins, country with weak law) | Must follow the Code | Code applies where law is weaker; MNPI rules of II(A) still apply |
| 5 | Following the highest requirements (Jameson, local ban on IPO participation) | **Violation** | Local law where she is registered and works governs regardless of home law; it is stricter, so Code disclosure is not enough |
| 6 | Laws based on religious tenets (Janney, Islamic-law prospects) | No violation stated | Be aware of cultural and religious laws; the firm "could be proactive" (RECOMMENDED) in flagging where the fund may not fit |
| 7 | Reporting potential unethical actions (Blume, odd research payments) | Must follow firm reporting policies | Use supervisor or compliance channels; push for disclosure of the broker relationship |
| 8 | Failure to maintain knowledge of the law (White, social media posts) | **Violation** | Must keep up with new rules; get guidance from compliance or counsel unless she tracks the rules herself |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q1**: if no specific law exists, the Code governs; if the law is stricter, follow the law. Complying only with weaker local law is not enough, and no local MNPI law does not permit trading on MNPI (curriculum L5491).

> **Traps: I(A)**
>
> Official:
> - Two steps: find the applicable law, then follow the stricter of it and the Code. Where you live does not decide it alone (curriculum L1470 to L1547).
> - A local **ban** beats the Code's disclosure rule; citizenship does not matter when you are registered and working locally (Ex 5, curriculum L1623).
> - "My lawyer said so" is no defense; get an independent opinion (Ex 1, curriculum L1593).
> - Dissociation is REQUIRED; reporting to CFA Institute is only encouraged; reporting to regulators is not required unless law demands it (curriculum L1457 to L1459).
> - You can violate I(A) by using material you did not create if you know it is false (Ex 3).
> - Falling behind on new rules (social media) is a violation (Ex 8).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - "Legal is the floor, not the ceiling"; a question saying the act is legal locally is the classic trap (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LalyW-mSiU4, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA).

Avoid extreme answers like "resign immediately" or "do nothing" (https://acadifi.com/community/cfa-level-3-ethics-study-strategy).
> - "When stuck, ask: did the person disclose, and did they follow the stricter rule?" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA)

## 2.6 Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity

### Standard text (curriculum L1106)
> "Members and Candidates must use reasonable care and judgment to achieve and maintain independence and objectivity in their professional activities. Members and Candidates must not offer, solicit, or accept any gift, benefit, compensation, or consideration that reasonably could be expected to compromise their own or another's independence and objectivity."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- Use reasonable care and judgment to **achieve and maintain** independence and objectivity.
- Do not **offer, solicit, or accept** anything that "reasonably could be expected to compromise" your **own or another's** independence. Giving is covered, not only receiving.
- Recommendations must state your **true opinion**, free of internal or external pressure, in **clear and unambiguous** language. Softening tone or vague wording to cope with pressure is not acceptable (curriculum L1683 to L1685).
- Allocation of shares in **oversubscribed IPOs** to managers' **personal accounts** is **prohibited** (curriculum L1675).
- **Client gifts**: should disclose to the employer **before** accepting when possible; if not possible, **must** disclose after accepting (curriculum L1679). Disclosure lets the employer judge the effect.
- **Issuer-paid research**: must do thorough, independent, unbiased analysis; must fully disclose conflicts **including the nature of compensation**; must distinguish fact from opinion; conclusions must have a reasonable and adequate basis; must strictly limit the type of compensation (curriculum L1745 to L1747). Minimum content: financial statement analysis from public data, peer benchmarking, industry analysis (curriculum L1746).
- IB and research: firms **must** adopt and follow best practices and manage firewalls (curriculum L1710).

### Key ideas and definitions
- Avoid situations that could cause **or be perceived to cause** a loss of independence (curriculum L1669 to L1673).
- Modest gifts and entertainment are acceptable; best practice is to reject any gift that could be expected to threaten independence (curriculum L1675).
- **Client gift vs third-party gift**: a client gift is like extra pay inside an existing relationship, so the risk is lower; gifts from parties seeking influence carry more risk (curriculum L1677 to L1679).
- Pressure sources: issuers wanting coverage, underwriters, brokers wanting commissions, the company that requests a credit rating, your own firm's IB or sales staff, buy-side clients (curriculum L1675, L1681, L1695).

### Guidance by area
- **Buy-side clients**: portfolio managers must respect sell-side research; threatening or retaliating against an analyst (e.g., reporting them to the company) is improper (curriculum L1696).
- **Fund manager and custodian selection**: should not accept gifts, entertainment or travel that may look like it impairs decisions; must review each event invitation on its own merits (curriculum L1700).
- **Investment banking**: collaboration is OK only if conflicts are managed and disclosed. Enhanced firewall: separate reporting lines; IB has **no authority** to approve, reject or change research; analyst pay should not be tied directly to IB deals; disclosures should be **prominent and specific**, not generic (curriculum L1705 to L1710).
- **Performance measurement**: analysts must not let pressure change composites or findings (curriculum L1714).
- **Public companies**: a good company is not always a good stock; companies may retaliate (cut access, sue) (curriculum L1718 to L1723).
- **Credit rating agencies**: need procedures against undue influence and firewalls between business lines; users of ratings may need to validate ratings themselves (curriculum L1727 to L1729).
- **Manager selection**: on the hiring side, should not **solicit** gifts or contributions (even to a favorite charity or political group; the benefit need not be personal) and should **refuse** offered gifts. On the selling side, should not **offer** gifts or donations to influence the decision maker (curriculum L1734 to L1735). Background: US "pay-to-play" scandals led to political contribution laws (curriculum L1739).
- **Travel**: best practice is **always** commercial transport paid by you or your firm. If commercial travel is not available, you may accept **modest** arrangements for real information-gathering (e.g., a property tour) (curriculum L1751 to L1755).

### Recommended procedures (RECOMMENDED unless noted)
Adhere to these and encourage your firm to adopt them (curriculum L1761 to L1779):
- Protect the integrity of opinions: research on corporate clients reflects the analyst's unbiased view; pay systems protect independence.
- **Restricted list**: if the firm will not allow negative views on a corporate client, remove it from coverage and publish only factual information.
- Restrict special cost arrangements: pay your own commercial travel and hotel; no issuer should reimburse air travel; corporate aircraft only when commercial travel is unavailable; the issuer should not always host frequent meetings.
- **Limit gifts**: the procedure text says members **must** limit gifts to **token** items. Ordinary business entertainment is fine if not meant to influence or reward. Firms should set a value limit based on local custom, per gift or annual aggregate.
- Restrict investments: firm policy on employee IPO purchases; **prior approval** for IPO participation with prompt disclosure of later trades; strict limits on private placements.
- Supervisory review of personal investing.
- Written research independence policy; analysts must not report to departments that could compromise them (see CFA Institute Research Objectivity Standards).
- Appoint a senior compliance officer and give every employee a way to report unethical conduct.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Travel expenses (Taylor, mine tour on charter flights, modest motels) | **No violation**, as long as objectivity is kept | Strictly business, not lavish, charters needed by the itinerary. Adams paying his own hotel follows firm policy and avoids even the appearance |
| 2 | Research independence (Dillon promises coverage) | OK to promise **coverage** | Must never promise a **favorable** rating |
| 3 | Intrafirm pressure (Fritz sees underwriting proposal) | Must stay objective | Firm could have put the company on a restricted list ahead of the deal |
| 4 | Issuer relationship pressure (Fritz fears losing access) | Must stay objective | Stress that the view rests on relative valuation, including management quality |
| 5 | Sales pressure (Warner, unsold bonds) | Must refuse to push the bonds | Only if she can show the price already reflects the problem |
| 6 | Prior coverage (Jorund told never to change a buy rating) | Two acceptable choices | Decline the coverage, or take it and give independent views to the boss or other supervisors |
| 7 | Gifts from related party (Grant, World Cup trip from a broker, not disclosed) | **Violation** | Substantial gifts may impede independence; opens him to favoring the broker |
| 8 | Gifts from client (Green, Wimbledon and London flat, disclosed to supervisor) | **No violation** | Client gifts allowed with disclosure to the employer; firm can monitor costs and returns vs other accounts |
| 9 | Travel paid by external manager (Wayne, trip co-sponsored by Penguin) | Recommendation "may be tainted" | Employer should pay travel; contact limited to educational events; trip made public record; mind perception |
| 10 | Issuer-paid research bonus (Herrero, flat fee plus bonus if investors buy) | Accepting would **violate** | Take only a flat fee not tied to conclusions |
| 11 | Service fees (Wade, hidden 0.25% fee, keeps pushing funds) | **Violation** | Fee hurt objectivity; also a VI(A) conflict issue |
| 12 | Recommendation objectivity (Thompson, negative house-price scenario) | **Compliant** | Did not bow to market trends or the manager's view; see also V(A) |
| 13 | Influencing manager selection (Mandel, campaign gifts to union leader) | **Violation** | Offering and giving is covered; disclosure to his own firm and expensing do **not** cure it |
| 14 | Influencing manager selection (Mandel entertains a consultant) | **Violation** | Applies to influencing consultants and intermediaries, not just final decision makers |
| 15 | Fund manager relationships (Scott, manager moved a bad account to a new composite) | Would **violate** if not reported | Personal ties and allocation size must not shape the analysis |
| 16 | Intrafirm pressure (Stein asked to drop a lost account from the composite) | Must refuse | An account run under the composite strategy cannot be removed for bad results |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q2**: issuer offers to pay an analyst's meals, hotel and airfare for a research visit. **Best** answer: the analyst pays all costs. Avoiding beats disclosing; being objective does not remove the appearance of conflict (curriculum L5495).
- **LM2 Q3**: IB head asks to change a "sell" to "buy" during an offering. Permitted: put the company on a **restricted list** and give only factual information. A one-notch move to "hold" still violates; assigning a new analyst does not fix the conflict (curriculum L5497 to L5499).
- **LM2 Q4**: keynote speaker at a third-party manager's conference. **Least likely** to violate: accept only conference-related costs. Free passes for staff and an exclusive golf day are inducements (curriculum L5501 to L5505).

> **Traps: I(B)**
>
> Official:
> - Appearance matters: a violation can rest on what clients would perceive (curriculum L1669, L1789).
> - Both directions: offering a gift to influence someone else is a violation (Ex 13, Ex 14).
> - Telling your own boss does not cure an improper gift you gave (Ex 13).
> - Client gift with disclosure to employer = OK (Ex 8). Broker's lavish package = violation (Ex 7). Disclosure goes to the **employer**, not the client.
> - Compensation from a client tied to **future** account performance is an IV(B) matter: disclose in writing and get employer permission **before** entering the arrangement (LM2 Q27, curriculum L5579).
> - Promise coverage, never a rating (Ex 2). A one-notch compromise is still a violation (LM2 Q3).
> - The restricted list is the right answer both for pressure conflicts and for MNPI (LM2 Q3, Q10).
> - Paid travel: an issuer paying for a research trip should be avoided; a host paying a speaker's own conference costs may be fine; extras are inducements (LM2 Q2 vs Q4). Charter flights can be acceptable when the itinerary needs them (Ex 1).
> - Oversubscribed IPO shares for personal accounts: prohibited (curriculum L1675). Prior approval for employee IPOs is a separate recommended procedure (curriculum L1769).
> - Issuer-paid research is allowed with independence and full disclosure; best practice is a flat fee agreed before writing; bonuses or warrants linked to the outcome impair independence (curriculum L1747, Ex 10).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - "Perception counts as much as reality" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc).
> - Keyword: "reasonably be expected to compromise"; the appearance of bias is enough (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo).
> - The question usually tells you if a gift is unreasonable with words like "exorbitant" or "extravagant"; also watch for "intends to establish a close rapport" with a selection manager (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HLpRMhMPEX4, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RkxXLlKJ4iI).
> - Large donations to a referral source's cause created "at the very least the perception" of compromised independence; one case turned on a firm gift-value limit (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc).
> - "Coverage yes, a good grade never" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).
> - On "best" questions about a conflict, avoiding the conflict is almost always the answer (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).
> - Client gifts: "past performance means disclose; future performance means get consent" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ). ## 2.7 Standard I(C) Misrepresentation

### Standard text (curriculum L1110)
> "Members and Candidates must not knowingly make any misrepresentations relating to investment analysis, recommendations, actions, or other professional activities."

### Key definitions
- **Misrepresentation**: any untrue statement, omission of a fact, or statement that is otherwise false or misleading (curriculum L1929).
- **Knowingly**: you know **or should have known** the statement was false, or that the omitted fact could change investment decisions (curriculum L1929).
- **Plagiarism**: copying or using others' material in substantially the same form without naming the source, author or publisher (curriculum L1971).

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- Do not knowingly omit or misstate facts, or give a false impression of a firm, organization or security, in any form: spoken, advertising, electronic (websites, apps, e-mail) or written, public or not (curriculum L1929).
- Do not misrepresent your own or your firm's qualifications, credentials, services or performance, or the features of an investment (curriculum L1937).
- Do not **guarantee** a specific return on **volatile** investments, or guarantee no loss (e.g., "you will earn 8% on equities this year") (curriculum L1933).
- Third-party material (ratings, research, testimonials, marketing) becomes **your** responsibility when it affects your business (curriculum L1941).
- Must **disclose** use of external managers and must not present their process as your own (curriculum L1943).
- Social media: same rules as traditional communications (curriculum L1957 to L1962).
- Model results must **not** be presented as fact (curriculum L1966).
- No plagiarism, including in internal and oral communications (curriculum L1971 to L1978).
- You **cannot reissue** a firm's old report solely under your own name (curriculum L1986).

### What is allowed
- Describing products with **built-in guarantees** or where an institution agreed to cover losses (curriculum L1933).
- Using research or models built by others **in your own firm**, even after they leave; the firm owns them and may reuse them without attribution (curriculum L1986).
- Using and repackaging third-party research, if you do not claim to be the author and disclose the source (curriculum L1977).
- Not showing a benchmark at all: I(C) does **not** require one. If you use a reference index that does not reflect the opportunity set (e.g., "cash plus"), make reasonable efforts to explain why (curriculum L1947).
- Unknowing errors are not violations, but once found they must be corrected (Ex 2).

### Performance and valuation points
- Benchmarks must be comparable to the strategy and reported on a comparable basis; best practice is the most appropriate available benchmark; discuss the benchmark with clients on an ongoing basis (curriculum L1947 to L1949).
- Shopping among pricing sources for higher values of illiquid securities can misstate value and inflate fees; do not switch providers just because one reports a higher value (curriculum L1951 to L1953).
- Omitting accounts that fit a composite ("cherry picking") misrepresents skill; firms are encouraged to have strict composite rules (curriculum L1967).

### Forms of plagiarism (curriculum L1973 to L1980)
- Taking another's report, changing names, releasing it as your own (most flagrant).
- Using excerpts verbatim or slightly reworded without credit.
- Quoting unnamed "leading analysts" or "investment experts".
- Using others' forecasts with the source but **without their caveats**.
- Using charts and graphs without sources.
- Copying proprietary spreadsheets or algorithms without permission.
- Building a report from many unacknowledged sources so it looks original.

### Recommended procedures (RECOMMENDED)
(curriculum L1990 to L2018)
- Factual presentations: firms can list available services and qualifications, suggest accurate descriptions, and name who may speak for the firm.
- Qualification summary: each member keeps a summary of own qualifications and services; firms review correspondence periodically.
- Verify outside information: members share responsibility for third-party marketing materials; encourage verification procedures.
- Maintain webpages: check for current information, security and full disclosure.
- Plagiarism policy: keep copies of all materials relied on; attribute direct quotations, projections, tables, statistics, model ideas and new methods, **except** those from recognized financial and statistical reporting services; attribute summaries and paraphrases.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Undisclosed issuer-paid research (McGuire's "independent" website) | **Violation** of I(C) and VI(A) | Hiding the paid relationship misleads even if the research is sound |
| 2 | Unintentional error (Yao, typo of 350 vs 35 billion) | **No violation** | Not knowing; but must stop distribution and correct it with recipients; follow GIPS error correction guidance |
| 3 | Noncorrection of known errors (Muhammad's false degree in marketing) | **Violation** | Used it many times over years, so he should have known |
| 4 | Plagiarism (Grant circulates Barton's report as her own, internally) | **Violation** | Internal circulation still counts |
| 5 | Misrepresentation (Marks sells IO strips as "government guaranteed") | **Violation** | Only the mortgages were guaranteed, not the IO cash flows |
| 6 | Potential misrepresentation (Abdrabbo, insured CDs called "guaranteed") | **Not inappropriate** | Fine within the insured limit; explain the facts to clients |
| 7 | Plagiarism (Swanson told to sign Davis's report) | Would **violate** | Cite Davis for the parts he agrees with, add his own analysis, then sign |
| 8 | Plagiarism (Browning's "new model" from a seminar) | Must credit the originator | He can claim his tests and results, not the original idea |
| 9 | Plagiarism (Zubia copies plain-language definitions) | **Violation** if copied without credit | Even general concepts; use your own words or cite |
| 10 | Plagiarism via media (Schneider reads of a study in the press) | Must cite the actual source | Best: get the original and cite the author; or cite both original and intermediary |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q5**: "payment is guaranteed by the US government" for a Treasury fund is a true fact; "you will earn 10% a year" guarantees a return and is a violation. Only Statement II violates; "in my opinion, investors may earn..." would cure it (curriculum L5507 to L5509).
- **LM2 Q6**: an average of 26% that is true but driven by one client's penny stock (8% without it) is a **violation**. Literal truth does not cure a misleading picture; "I don't guarantee future returns" does not fix misstated past results (curriculum L5513 to L5515).

> **Traps: I(C)**
>
> Official:
> - "Knowingly" includes "should have known" (Ex 3). An honest typo is not a violation, but correction is required (Ex 2).
> - "Guaranteed" is not always a violation: a factual statement about a product's structural or government guarantee is fine (LM2 Q5, Ex 6). It is a violation when the guarantee is false (Ex 5) or promises a return on a volatile investment.
> - Technically true but misleading = violation (LM2 Q6, Ex 16 risk).
> - "We don't control the third party's materials" is no defense (Ex 12, Ex 15).
> - Plagiarism covers internal reports, oral talks, plain-language definitions, forecasts without caveats, unnamed experts, and charts without sources. The attribution exception is for **recognized financial and statistical reporting services** (curriculum L2016).
> - The firm owns research done for it; the individual cannot reissue it solely in his or her name (curriculum L1986).
> - No benchmark is not automatically a violation (curriculum L1947).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - Keyword is "knowingly" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo).
> - "Will" vs "may": an opinion stated as fact is the trap (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aT8sGNsyFo0).
> - The word "immediately" (e.g., updating materials with a new third-party rating without checking it) signals a violation; verify third-party data first (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).
> - Do not assume fraud: a wrong number may be a typo; weigh all facts (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7rq5_qdUfM). Do not invent bad intent the question does not give (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo).
> - Some videos say "guarantees are always misrepresentation" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AKNjkuCMcUU). That is too broad: the curriculum allows describing built-in guarantees (curriculum L1933).
> - Social media posts are held to the same standard as formal reports (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).

## 2.8 Standard I(D) Misconduct

### Standard text (curriculum L1114)
> "Members and Candidates must not engage in any professional conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, or deceit or commit any act that reflects adversely on their professional reputation, integrity, or competence."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- Two prongs: (1) no **professional** conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit; (2) no **act** (professional or not) that reflects badly on professional reputation, integrity or competence.
- I(A) is about obeying the law; I(D) covers **all** conduct that reflects poorly on professional integrity, legal or not (curriculum L2151).
- Lying, cheating, stealing or other dishonesty violates I(D) **if** it reflects on professional activities (curriculum L2151).
- Conduct need not be illegal: drinking during business hours that impairs work can violate (curriculum L2152).
- **Lack of effort** can violate: you are expected to do the due diligence to understand an investment before recommending it; relying on someone else to do it may break client trust (curriculum L2153).

### What is generally NOT covered
- Personal bankruptcy alone, unless it involved fraud or deceit (curriculum L2152).
- Civil disobedience in support of personal beliefs (Ex 4).
- The Code is aimed mainly at professional life, though CFA Institute discourages all unethical behavior (curriculum L2151).
- Using the PCP to settle personal or political disputes is an abuse that CFA Institute has procedures to handle (curriculum L2154).

### Recommended procedures (RECOMMENDED: encourage the firm to adopt)
(curriculum L2160 to L2166)
- A code of ethics every employee signs, stating poor personal behavior will not be tolerated.
- A list of potential violations and sanctions, up to dismissal.
- Reference checks on new hires for good character and past legal problems.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Professionalism and competence (Sasserman drinks at lunch, trades intoxicated) | **Violation** | Reflects on his competence, employer and industry |
| 2 | Fraud and deceit (Hoffman, altered medical bills to firm health plan) | **Violation** | Workplace fraud |
| 3 | Fraud and deceit (Brink, charity van kickback) | **Violation** | Dishonesty in volunteer work still counts |
| 4 | Personal actions (Garcia, arrested at nonviolent protests) | **Generally not** a violation | Civil disobedience for personal beliefs does not reflect on professional integrity |
| 5 | Professional misconduct (Rasmussen, hedge fund marks ignore a market break) | Must pursue it | Gather proof, follow firm procedures; if not satisfied internally, consider reporting to the regulator (see IV(A) whistleblowing and IV(C)) |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q7** (also III(A)): a PM who files personal trading reports only in months with no trades violates I(D) by hiding trades and ignoring policy (curriculum L5517). Full lesson is under III(A) below.

> **Traps: I(D)**
>
> Official:
> - I(D) reaches personal-life acts only when they involve dishonesty or reflect on professional integrity (Ex 3 violates; Ex 4 does not).
> - Not being illegal does not mean no violation (curriculum L2152).
> - Skipping due diligence and relying on others can itself be misconduct (curriculum L2153).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - Volunteer work counts; a retired charterholder signing a scheme with "CFA" violates (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).
> - Purely private behavior (eating alone, being unfriendly) does not violate (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HLpRMhMPEX4).
> - Intoxication at work violates; civil disobedience generally does not (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qd_2KJKO-8c, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ).

## 2.9 Standard I(E) Competence (new in the 2023 revisions)

### Standard text (curriculum L1120)
> "Members and Candidates must act with and maintain the competence necessary to fulfill their professional responsibilities."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- Act with **and maintain** the knowledge, skills and diligence needed for a high standard of service (curriculum L2214).
- Achieve **and** keep competence as the role changes; continuously maintain or improve it (curriculum L2228).
- What counts as competent depends on the **role** and the **facts and circumstances** (curriculum L2215).

### Key definitions (curriculum L2218 to L2222)
- **Knowledge**: the body of information applied directly to a function, and how well it is applied.
- **Skills**: the ability to perform role-specific tasks and reach professional goals.
- **Abilities**: capabilities and attitudes that lead to observable outcomes.

### NOT required / not proof
- No specific continuing education program or development plan is required (curriculum L2228).
- A bad outcome does **not** prove incompetence; competent managers lose money (curriculum L2224).
- Education level or passing an exam does **not** by itself prove competence (curriculum L2226, Ex 3).

### Recommended procedures (RECOMMENDED: members should consider)
(curriculum L2236 to L2248)
- Regular professional development or continuing education.
- Studying for or earning certifications or designations.
- Conferences, seminars, webinars.
- Regular employer training.
- Informal self-study (articles, books).
- Joining expert groups.
- Becoming proficient in any new skill when duties change.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maintaining competence (Lee reads a new trade deal and asks experts) | **Satisfied** | Keeping up with role-relevant changes |
| 2 | Improving competence (Choe adds ESG: hires an ESG analyst, team takes training and a certificate) | **Satisfied** | Fill gaps before offering the new service |
| 3 | Change in role (Glusker moves to sales, passes license exam without learning fair-dealing rules) | **Failed** I(E) | Passing an exam alone is not competence |
| 4 | Supervisory responsibility (Evans promoted, ignores management and compliance duties) | Must extend competence | New role needs supervision skills (management courses, compliance credentials, internal training) |
| 5 | Choosing investments (Mifune, insurer collapses from CFO fraud) | **No** incompetence by itself | Thorough research that misses an industry-wide fraud is not incompetence |
| 6 | New products (Halsey buys a hyped crypto product to keep clients) | **Violation** | Invested without understanding the product or its suitability |

> **Traps: I(E)**
>
> Official:
> - Outcome is not the test; process is (Ex 5, curriculum L2224).
> - Credentials or a passed exam are not enough (Ex 3).
> - New duties, including supervising people, need new competence (Ex 4).
> - Avoiding products you cannot understand is compliant (I(C) Ex 13); buying them anyway violates I(E) (Ex 6).
> - The Code says "maintain and improve"; I(E) says "act with and maintain" (curriculum L1092, L1120).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - Passing an exam does not prove competence; a promotion creates new competence duties; client pressure or fear of missing out is no defense (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).
> - A wrong recommendation is not by itself a violation (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RkxXLlKJ4iI).
> - Mnemonic for I(A) to I(E): "Rules, Pressure, Truth, Character, Capability" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g).
> - Older videos list only four sub-standards under Standard I; they predate I(E) (flagged in https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HLpRMhMPEX4 comments).

## 2.10 Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information

### Standard text (curriculum L1126)
> "Members and Candidates who possess material nonpublic information that could affect the value of an investment must not act or cause others to act on the information."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- If you **possess** MNPI, do not **act** on it and do not **cause others to act** (tipping is a violation even if the person tipped never trades: Ex 7).
- Applies to **any** trading: stocks, bonds, derivatives (swaps, options), mutual funds, alternatives (curriculum L2322).
- If public dissemination is not possible, you **must** tell only designated supervisory and compliance staff, and **must not** trade **or change current recommendations** on the information (curriculum L2413).
- You **must not** knowingly do anything that may induce insiders to disclose MNPI privately (curriculum L2413).
- With expert networks, you are responsible for not requesting or acting on confidential information; if an expert gives you MNPI, you may not act until it is public (curriculum L2398 to L2399).

### Key definitions
- **Material**: disclosure would probably affect the price, **or** reasonable investors would want to know it before deciding (curriculum L2328). Factors: specificity, how different it is from public information, nature, reliability (curriculum L2330).
- Less reliable source = less likely material (insider fact vs competitor guess; trial staff vs outside experts) (curriculum L2362).
- More ambiguous price effect = less material; old information can lose materiality over time (curriculum L2364).
- Examples of material information (curriculum L2332 to L2360): earnings; mergers, acquisitions, tender offers, joint ventures; asset quality changes; new products or discoveries; licenses, patents, regulatory approvals or rejections; customer or supplier contracts won or lost; management changes; auditor changes or reliance problems; security events (defaults, calls, buybacks, splits, dividend changes, holder rights changes, new issues); bankruptcies; major legal disputes; government economic reports; **large orders before they are executed**; **new or changed third-party ratings** (sell-side recommendations, credit ratings).
- **Nonpublic**: not yet disseminated to the market in general. Public needs a reasonable expectation that people have received it, but you need not wait for the slowest channel (curriculum L2368).
- Selective disclosure (to a room of analysts, a conference call, the top 10 shareholders) does **not** make information public. Company "guidance" on public filings may itself be selective disclosure (curriculum L2370 to L2372).
- **Mosaic theory**: you may act on conclusions from **public information plus nonmaterial nonpublic information**, even if the conclusion would have been material inside information had the company told you directly (curriculum L2380).
- **Due diligence exception**: insider information given for due diligence under a business agreement (mergers, loan underwriting, credit ratings, offerings) may be used for that purpose only; trading on it is not allowed (curriculum L2376).
- A respected analyst's report can be material, but the analyst need not publish it to the public; clients paid for it (curriculum L2403 to L2407).

### Social media and experts
- Not all online information is public. In membership-limited groups, check the information is also available from public sources (curriculum L2391 to L2392).
- Using social media to reach all clients or the public is fine (like e-mail or press releases); complete any related regulatory filings (curriculum L2393).
- Paying expert networks is allowed (curriculum L2398).

### Recommended procedures (RECOMMENDED unless noted)
(curriculum L2411 to L2484)
- **Achieve public dissemination**: if information is material, make reasonable efforts to get it public, usually by urging the company to release it. (The fallback steps above are REQUIRED.)
- Encourage firms to adopt **compliance procedures**: review employee and proprietary trading and recommendations, document procedures, supervise interdepartmental communication; scale to firm size. Inform supervisors or compliance of suspected misuse.
- Encourage **disclosure procedures** so information reaches the market equitably; companies should treat small and large, buy-side and sell-side analysts alike and not blackball negative analysts; research firms need fair procedures to distribute new opinions.
- Companies should consider **press releases** before analyst meetings and script calls; if MNPI comes out in a meeting, issue a press release promptly.
- **Firewall minimum elements**:
  1. control of interdepartmental communication, preferably through a clearance area in compliance or legal;
  2. review of employee trading using **watch, restricted and rumor lists**;
  3. documentation of procedures and enforcement actions;
  4. heightened review or restriction of proprietary trading while holding MNPI.
- Even small firms should formalize procedures.
- **Physically separate** departments and files (IB from sales and research; commercial lending from trust and research).
- **No personnel overlap**; one side of the wall at a time. An analyst who helps bankers "over the wall" is treated as a banker until the information is public.
- **Reporting system**: a designated compliance officer decides whether and how much to share across the wall; one officer decides if information is material and public; ideally the person passing information to research is not part of research.
- **Personal trading limits**: restrictions, monitoring, periodic reports of own and family trades, restricted lists. A widely circulated restricted list can trigger the trading it aims to stop, so a **watch list** seen only by a few compliance staff is often used together with it.
- **Records**: written records of interdepartmental communication; training, especially in sensitive areas.
- **Proprietary trading**: a total ban while holding MNPI is **not** appropriate. Market makers may continue but stay **passive** (only take the other side of unsolicited customer trades), because withdrawing would tip the market. **Risk arbitrage**: most prudent to suspend when the stock is on the watch list.
- Circulate written policies and training to all employees.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acting on nonpublic information (tender offer tip chain to broker Halsey) | **Violation** by Staple (tipped his broker) and Halsey (traded) | Liability runs down the chain |
| 2 | Controlling nonpublic information (Peter's call overheard by colleagues) | **Violation** by Peter and by those who traded | The holder must control MNPI; firewalls needed |
| 3 | Selective disclosure (Levenson hears of a strike at a top-10-holder meeting) | Cannot use it | A small group meeting is not public |
| 4 | Materiality (Fechtman's doctor predicts a buyout) | **No violation** | Unreliable source, so not material |
| 5 | Mosaic theory (Teja, furniture maker's weak sales) | **No violation** | Public plus nonmaterial nonpublic pieces |
| 6 | Mosaic theory (Clement, auto maker downgrade) | **No violation** | Built from public and nonmaterial pieces; kept support files |
| 7 | Analyst recommendation as MNPI (journalist Zito trades before the TV show and tells her father) | **Violation** twice | A respected analyst's change is material to a non-client with early access; tipping violates even if no one trades |
| 8 | Acting on nonpublic information (Kellogg, golf tip from an executive) | **Violation** | Assuming the insider obeys the law is no defense; losing money is no defense |
| 9 | Mosaic theory (Doll, executive's comment at a wedding) | No violation stated | Avoid soliciting MNPI; weigh the comment against public facts for materiality before publishing |
| 10 | Materiality (Nadler, text from another trader) | **No violation** | Whisper numbers are usually noise unless the source has a business tie to the company; the PM's order came independently |
| 11 | Expert network (McCoy learns about therapies) | **No violation** | Did not seek or get MNPI |
| 12 | Expert network (Watson, scientist reveals poor test results) | **Violation** | Network agreements do not shift responsibility; trial staff information is material; options trades count |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q8**: the mosaic theory lets an analyst use material public information and nonmaterial nonpublic information; conclusions become material only once assembled (curriculum L5519).
- **LM2 Q9**: best policy when IB gets MNPI: **physical and informational barriers (firewalls)**. A permanent ban on recommendations is too strict; monitoring exchanges is too weak because no exchange should occur (curriculum L5521 to L5525).
- **LM2 Q10**: a job candidate mentions an unannounced merger. **Least likely** violation: put the securities on the **restricted list**. Waiting one day does not make it public; telling other managers is not allowed (curriculum L5529).
- **LM2 Q11**: a CFO's golf tip leads a friend to buy a retail ETF holding the stock: **violation**. Trading through a fund is no defense; the CFO's sharing, even between friends, also violates II(A) (curriculum L5531 to L5533).

> **Traps: II(A)**
>
> Official:
> - Two materiality tests (price impact OR reasonable investor wants it); either is enough (curriculum L2328).
> - Same type of news, different source, different answer: the doctor's buyout tip is not material (Ex 4), the trial scientist's comment is (Ex 12).
> - Disclosure to analysts, top shareholders or a closed online group is not public (Ex 3, curriculum L2372, L2392).
> - One piece of **material** nonpublic information breaks the mosaic defense.
> - Documenting mosaic research is RECOMMENDED (link to V(C)) as evidence; it is not a condition of the defense (curriculum L2385).
> - Changing a recommendation counts as acting (curriculum L2413).
> - Tipping is a violation even if no one trades (Ex 7); assuming the insider knows the law is no defense (Ex 8); an ETF or option instead of the stock is no defense (LM2 Q11, Ex 12).
> - Do not ban all proprietary trading: market makers stay passive; risk arbitrage should be suspended (curriculum L2474 to L2479).
> - Large unexecuted orders and third-party rating changes are on the material list: MNPI need not come from the issuer (curriculum L2358 to L2360).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - Keyword is "possess": how you got the information does not matter, even when the question spends a paragraph on it (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo).
> - Information given to analysts but not announced is still nonpublic (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=33okJwJt6yw).
> - Trading on MNPI is a violation even if other analysts had it, and selling for clients first does not cure it (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc).
> - A practice question treated shorting a correlated stock after hearing a nonpublic downgrade as a violation; a commenter argued intent, not correlation, decides (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q). This is a disputed provider item, not curriculum.
> - Mosaic theory explained with a worked case (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3TdQhWj-NZU); CFALA summary: "material public + immaterial nonpublic may be combined; material nonpublic may not be used" (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf).

## 2.11 Standard II(B) Market Manipulation

### Standard text (curriculum L1130)
> "Members and Candidates must not engage in practices that distort prices or artificially inflate trading volume with the intent to mislead market participants."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- No practices that distort prices or artificially inflate volume **with intent to mislead** (curriculum L2591).
- **Intent is critical** to whether there is a violation (curriculum L2611).
- Legitimate strategies that exploit perceived market inefficiencies are **not** prohibited (curriculum L2611).

### Two types (curriculum L2593 to L2609)
| Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Information-based** | Spreading false rumors to make others trade; "pump and dump" (push the price up with misleading positive news or overly rosy forecasts, then sell) |
| **Transaction-based** | (1) Trades that artificially affect price or volume to fake activity or price moves; (2) taking a dominant position in an instrument to manipulate a related derivative and/or the underlying. Standard: the member **knew or should have known** the actions could affect price |

- Harm: lower trust, higher risk premiums, less participation, weaker economies; cross-border investing exposes all investors (curriculum L2591 to L2593).

### Recommended procedures
- None are given for II(B) in the curriculum (the text goes from guidance straight to examples).

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Paid promotion (FIS e-mails and newsletter for microcaps) | **Violation** of II(B), also V(A) and VI(A) | Misleading "independent" analysis to lift prices; no basis; pay undisclosed |
| 2 | Personal trading and price (Gray, rumors plus trading among many accounts) | **Violation** | Both information and transaction manipulation; applies to his own holdings |
| 3 | Artificial volatility (Murphy times a speculative negative report in the quiet period) | **Violation**, also V(A) | Sensational timing with no basis shows intent |
| 4 | Personal trading and volume (Sekar trades between his two funds) | **Violation** | Good motive for fund holders does not cure faked volume and price |
| 5 | "Pump-priming" (ACME exchange pays members for minimum volume) | **Violation if undisclosed**; OK if fully disclosed | Real liquidity shows in volume and spreads; disclosure removes the deception |
| 6 | Artificial volatility (Gordon floats an "ambitious" estimate to momentum clients) | **Violation** of II(B) and III(B) | Acceptable alternative: a range of outcomes, clear assumptions, sent to all clients |
| 7 | Pump and dump (Weinberg posts railroad rumors) | **Violation** | False information with intent to mislead |
| 8 | Manipulating model inputs (Mandeville uses rosy scenarios for ratings) | **Violation** | Input manipulation to win ratings is information manipulation |
| 9 | Information manipulation (King posts fake merger rumors to hurt his PM) | **Violation** | No personal profit needed; revenge motive still misleads the market |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q12**: a bank president who has hired the market maker to find a merger partner places a personal buy order 4 times average weekly volume: **violation** (pushing up price). A press release on the search for a partner, or reaffirming earlier guidance, is normal disclosure, not manipulation, even if the price moves (curriculum L5535).

> **Traps: II(B)**
>
> Official:
> - Intent decides it; moving the price by itself is not manipulation (LM2 Q12, curriculum L2611).
> - No profit motive needed (Ex 9). Benefit to clients does not excuse it (Ex 3, Ex 4).
> - Disclosure can cure a liquidity arrangement (Ex 5).
> - Look for companion violations: V(A) no basis (Ex 1, Ex 3), VI(A) undisclosed pay (Ex 1), III(B) selective preview (Ex 6).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - The test is intent to mislead; price or volume effects in the question are a distraction (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HLpRMhMPEX4).

## 2.12 Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care

### Standard text (curriculum L1136)
> "Members and Candidates have a duty of loyalty to their clients and must act with reasonable care and exercise prudent judgment. Members and Candidates must act for the benefit of their clients and place their clients' interests before their employer's or their own interests."

### What it requires (REQUIRED)
- Loyalty to clients; reasonable care; prudent judgment; act for the client's benefit; put clients **before** employer and self (not equal to them: LM1 PP7).
- Act for the **sole benefit** of the client, in the way you believe is in the client's best interest, with the same care you would use for your own affairs (curriculum L2725).
- Comply with any legal fiduciary duty, and the stricter of law and Code (curriculum L2731).
- Know whether you have **custody** (any direct or indirect access to client funds); if so, a heightened duty applies (curriculum L2731).
- Manage pools of assets under the **governing documents** (trust deeds, management agreements), which are the main source of the manager's powers (curriculum L2731).
- Using client brokerage to pay higher commissions **without** a matching client benefit violates loyalty (curriculum L2771).
- Proxies have economic value; you **must** safeguard and maximize it (curriculum L2777).
- **Seek best execution** unless the client, as ultimate beneficiary, directs otherwise (curriculum L2818).

### Key definitions
- **Prudence**: caution and discretion; the care, skill and diligence a reasonable person in a like role, familiar with such matters, would use. In portfolio work: follow client parameters and balance risk and return (curriculum L2727).
- **Care**: acting prudently and judiciously to avoid harm to clients (curriculum L2727).
- **Soft dollars / soft commissions**: using client brokerage to buy research services (curriculum L2771).
- **Directed brokerage**: the client tells the manager to use its brokerage to buy goods or services for the client. The commission is the client's asset, so this is **not** a loyalty breach (curriculum L2772).
- **Best execution**: a trading process that seeks to maximize the value of the client's portfolio within the client's objectives and constraints (curriculum L2772).

### Fiduciary status
- III(A) sets a **minimum** benchmark for everyone regardless of title, local law or culture, but it does **not** make all members fiduciaries (curriculum L2729, L2737 to L2739).
- Whether you are a fiduciary depends on the client type, whether you give advice, and the facts (curriculum L2737).
- **Execution-only**: work in the client's interest by executing on the most favorable terms within the client's instructions (curriculum L2741).
- **Blended role** (trades plus limited advice, e.g., proprietary products only): state the limits in the agreement at the start; recommend allowable products that fit the client (curriculum L2741).
- Ignore firm or personal interest in recommending a trade (curriculum L2743).

### Who is the client? (curriculum L2749 to L2756)
| Situation | Client |
|---|---|
| Individual's own assets | The individual |
| Pension plan or trust | The **beneficiaries**, not the sponsor or whoever hired the manager |
| Fund run to an index or mandate | Duty is to manage to the **stated mandate**; individual investors' needs belong to their own advisers |
| Company executive | The firm's public shareholders |
| Research for the public | Sometimes the investing public as a whole, so independence and objectivity outrank loyalty to one organization |

- Members must put client duties first and try to avoid all real or potential conflicts (curriculum L2755).

### Building the portfolio (curriculum L2760 to L2767)
- Make sure client objectives and expectations are realistic and risks suitable; strategies should usually reflect long-term goals.
- Watch for conflicts between the firm's goals (selling products, generating trades) and the client; if a conflict cannot be avoided, give clear, factual disclosure.
- Follow client guidelines (some clients ban asset types; others set total portfolio risk and return limits).
- Judge each investment in the context of the **total portfolio**: its role, risk of loss and chance of gain, taxes, and the diversification, liquidity, cash flow and return needs of the assets.

### Soft dollars and directed brokerage (curriculum L2771 to L2772)
- Soft dollars are OK only for research that benefits the client, at commissions in line with the value received.
- Directed brokerage is permitted, but you are still obligated to seek best price and execution, get the client's assurance that the purchases benefit the beneficiaries, and **should disclose** that the client may not get best execution.

### Proxy voting (curriculum L2776 to L2778)
- Vote in an informed, responsible way. Possible violations: not voting, voting without considering the impact, voting blindly with management on **nonroutine** issues (e.g., change in capitalization).
- A cost-benefit analysis may justify not voting every proxy.
- **Should disclose** proxy voting policies to clients (RECOMMENDED).

### Recommended procedures (RECOMMENDED unless noted)
Regular account information, for members with control of client assets (curriculum L2784):
1. Send each client, **at least quarterly**, an itemized statement of funds and securities held, plus all debits, credits and transactions.
2. Tell clients where assets are held and when or where they are moved.
3. Keep client assets separate from everyone else's, including your own.

Client approval (curriculum L2790): if unsure, ask what you would expect if you were the client; if in doubt, disclose the matter **in writing** and get client approval.

Firm policy topics (curriculum L2796 to L2820):
- Follow all applicable rules and laws.
- Establish client objectives (reasonable inquiry into experience, risk and return objectives, constraints) before acting.
- Consider all information: client needs, the investment's features, the total portfolio's features.
- Diversify, unless plan guidelines or account objectives say otherwise.
- Carry out regular reviews against governing documents.
- Deal fairly with all clients: trade allocation and recommendation dissemination policies.
- Disclose all actual and potential conflicts.
- Disclose all forms of manager compensation.
- Vote proxies: decide who is authorized and vote in clients' best interests.
- Maintain confidentiality.
- Seek best execution (REQUIRED unless the client directs otherwise).
- Place client interests first.

### Application examples

| Ex | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plan participants (Wiley buys overvalued sponsor stock to block a takeover) | **Violation** | Client is the plan beneficiaries, not sponsor management or the bank |
| 2 | Client commissions (JNI's broker pays JNI's rent) | **Violation** | Client brokerage used for the manager's overhead; no best price or execution |
| 3 | Brokerage arrangements (Everett routes other clients' trades to a referring broker without telling them) | **Violation** (undisclosed) | Referral-reward routing allowed only with best execution **and** disclosure; new clients may waive best execution only in writing, understanding the consequences |
| 4 | Brokerage arrangements (Rome gets better personal prices from the trust's broker) | **Violation** | Broker chosen partly for her own benefit |
| 5 | Client commissions (Parker uses commissions for a research trip plus five Carnival days) | **Violation** | Should check reasonableness and execution first; vacation days are not research |
| 6 | Excessive trading (Knauss trades to meet a commission target) | **Violation** | Churning, even with suitable securities |
| 7 | Family accounts (Dill gives his brother no share of a scarce IPO) | **Violation** | A regular fee-paying family account must be treated like other clients; exclude it only if outside the normal fee structure |
| 8 | Identifying the client (Hensley, expert witness hired by a law firm) | Guidance | Bound by independence and objectivity; the law firm must not shape testimony |
| 9 | Identifying the client (fund PM Miller and adviser Spears) | Guidance | The adviser alone owes loyalty to the investor; the fund PM follows the fund's IPS |
| 10 | Client loyalty (Nguyen finds an error that will show a big loss for a client on "watch") | **Compliant** | Correct errors even if the firm may be fired |
| 11 | Execution-only (Sulejman, broker with no advice) | No fiduciary duty | Duty is best execution within client parameters (quantity, price, timing) |

### Practice problem lessons
- **LM2 Q7**: PM gets better personal prices from the broker she uses for clients and files trade reports only in months with no trades. Most likely violation: failing to disclose personal trades to her employer. Official reasoning: better personal prices breach III(A); hiding trades breaches I(D); her supervisor breached IV(C) by not enforcing reporting. Owning the same securities as clients, or using the same broker, is **not** prohibited (should be disclosed to the employer) (curriculum L5517).
- **LM2 Q13**: a pension fund manager owes loyalty to the plan's **participants and beneficiaries**, not management or shareholders (curriculum L5537).
- **LM2 Q14**: voting with management after independent research is fine; directing trades to a broker with the lowest commissions and best execution is fine even if its research helps other accounts. **No violation** on either (curriculum L5539 to L5541).
- **LM2 Q15**: a low-fee execution-only service from a soft-dollar broker may be used for accounts that ban soft dollars, since fees are comparable and execution good. Trading more to meet a commission commitment would be churning (curriculum L5545).
- **LM2 Q16**: the client brokerage use that **violates** is paying the manager's operating expenses. Directed brokerage and research in clients' interest at fair value are permitted; always disclose brokerage practices (curriculum L5547).
- **LM2 Q17**: paying CFA Program fees with soft dollars is a **violation** because education is not research; prospectus disclosure does not make it acceptable (curriculum L5549).
- **LM2 Q23** (mainly III(E), covered with that standard): an endowment adviser must not send confidential financial information to an alumnus without the trustees' permission, even if it might help the fund (curriculum L5565).

> **Traps: III(A)**
>
> Official:
> - Clients come **before** employer and self, not "balanced" with them (LM1 PP7).
> - III(A) is a minimum standard but does not make everyone a fiduciary; execution-only brokers have no fiduciary duty, only best execution (Ex 11, curriculum L2737).
> - Pension or trust: the client is the beneficiaries. The sponsor's management is the classic wrong answer (Ex 1, LM2 Q13).
> - A fund manager answers to the mandate, not to any one investor (Ex 9, curriculum L2754).
> - Soft dollars: research that helps other accounts is acceptable if this account pays no more (LM2 Q14); manager overhead, vacations and education are not research (Ex 2, Ex 5, LM2 Q16, Q17). Disclosure does not cure a non-research use (LM2 Q17).
> - Directed brokerage is the client's asset, so it is allowed, but still seek best execution and disclose it may not be achieved (curriculum L2772).
> - Agreeing with management on a proxy is fine if you researched it independently (LM2 Q14). Not voting can be fine after a cost-benefit analysis (curriculum L2778).
> - Churning is a violation even when every security is suitable (Ex 6).
> - Excluding a fee-paying family account to "avoid the appearance" of a conflict is itself a violation (Ex 7).
> - Owning the same securities as clients, or using the same broker, is not by itself prohibited (LM2 Q7).
> - Recommended statements are **at least quarterly** (curriculum L2784). When in doubt: disclose in writing and get client approval (curriculum L2790).
>
> Prep providers and YouTubers say:
> - Think in plain language: loyalty, prudence and care simply means "don't treat clients unfairly"; don't stall on "was that 3B or 4C?" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc).
> - Identify which party is affected: client issues point to Standard III, employer issues to Standard IV (CFALA deck, https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf).
> - "The client's best interest is the core test in most conflicts" (https://acadifi.com/community/cfa-level-3-ethics-study-strategy).
> - Family members who are fee-paying clients must be treated like any other client; making a relative wait until others traded was ruled a violation in provider questions (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZeKYZYJQBGg, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-JAwzhU-A0o).
> - "When in doubt, disclose" is a popular provider "golden rule" (https://300hours.com/cfa-ethics-tips/). Caveat from CFALA: disclosure does **not** cure an act that breaks the law, is prohibited by a Standard, or needs consent (https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf). LM2 Q17 is a curriculum example where disclosure does not cure.

## III(B) Fair Dealing

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must deal fairly and objectively with all clients when providing investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment action, or engaging in other professional activities. (curriculum L1140)

### What it requires
- **MUST** treat all clients fairly when you (a) send out investment recommendations, (b) make material changes to earlier recommendations, and (c) take investment action (general purchases, new issues, secondary offerings). (curriculum L2921)
- The word is "fairly", not "equally" (curriculum L2923). Reasons:
  - You cannot reach every client at exactly the same moment.
  - Not every opportunity suits every client.
  - Clients who pay more (higher fees or brokerage) may get more personal or in-depth service.
- Premium service is allowed only if (curriculum L2923 to L2925):
  - it does not disadvantage other clients;
  - it is disclosed to clients and prospects (SHOULD);
  - it is available to everyone, not offered selectively (SHOULD).
- Two kinds of conduct are covered: investment recommendations and investment action (curriculum L2927).

**Investment recommendations**
- Covers people whose primary job is preparing recommendations that others act on, inside or outside the firm (curriculum L2933).
- **MUST** spread recommendations so all clients have a fair opportunity to act on every recommendation (curriculum L2935).
- SHOULD encourage the firm to design an equitable system that prevents selective disclosure, and SHOULD tell clients what kinds of communications they will receive (curriculum L2935).
- Changed recommendations: the duty may be **more** critical than for first recommendations (curriculum L2936).
  - Material changes SHOULD go to all current clients.
  - Take extra care to reach clients you know acted on the earlier advice.
  - A client who places an order against a changed recommendation, not knowing of the change, SHOULD be told of the change **before** the order is accepted.

**Investment action**
- Covers portfolio managers acting on internal or external recommendations (curriculum L2940).
- New issues and secondary financings go to all clients for whom they are appropriate, consistent with the firm's block-allocation policy (curriculum L2940).
- Oversubscribed issues (curriculum L2940):
  - prorate to all subscribers, in round lots (no odd lots);
  - members SHOULD forgo purchases for themselves and immediate family to free shares for clients;
  - exception: a family account managed like other client accounts should **not** be excluded.
- Treat individual and institutional clients fairly. One institution may have several relationships with the firm (trustee, pension manager, lender), and each must be treated fairly (curriculum L2944).
- SHOULD disclose documented allocation procedures to clients and prospects, clearly and completely (curriculum L2945).
- **Key rule:** even with full disclosure, client interests come before your own. Client consent to a patently unfair allocation procedure never overrides the duty of fairness and loyalty (curriculum L2945).
- **MUST NOT** withhold hot issues for your own benefit or use them as a reward or incentive. Hot issues need a bona fide public distribution (curriculum L2946).
- Trade allocation procedures **must** be fair and equitable. Disclosing an inequitable method does not relieve you of this duty (curriculum L2999).

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Investment recommendation | Any opinion on buying, selling, or holding a security or other investment. It can be a full report, a short update, adding to or removing from a recommended list, or a spoken comment (curriculum L2934). |
| Communication for general distribution | A recommendation sent to anyone outside the organization (curriculum L2934). |
| Hot issue | Securities of a public offering that trade at a premium in the secondary market as soon as trading starts, because of high demand (curriculum L2946). |

### Recommended procedures
**Develop firm policies** (curriculum L2950 to L2955)
- Encourage the firm to set compliance procedures for fair treatment. At minimum, recommend procedures to management if none exist, and alert management to possible violations you see.
- How formal depends on firm size and the securities involved. Even a sole proprietor with only discretionary accounts SHOULD have formal written procedures.
- You need not send a recommendation to every customer. But choose recipients by **suitability and known interest**, never by favored status.
- Common practice: release recommendations inside the firm and to customers at the same time.

**Points for fair-dealing procedures** (curriculum L2955 to L2995)
1. Limit the number of people who know a recommendation is coming.
2. Shorten the time between the decision and its release:
   - publish a short summary before a long report;
   - hold research and policy committee meetings on the same day if possible;
   - use a "flash" report (e-mail, wire, short note).
3. Publish rules for pre-release behavior: people who know must not discuss or act on it.
4. Release at approximately the same time to all clients:
   - send to all branch offices at the time of the general announcement;
   - employee trading restrictions stay in effect until the recommendation is widely distributed;
   - after distribution you may follow up with individual clients, but give no favored client advance information.
5. Keep a list of clients and their holdings, so changed recommendations reach holders and liquidations are fair.
6. Develop and document trade allocation procedures. Principles: fairness in priority of execution and in allocation of block prices; timely and efficient execution; accurate records. Procedures to consider:
   - time-stamp orders, changes, and cancellations;
   - first-in, first-out execution, with bundling where efficient;
   - a policy for execution prices and partial fills in block trades;
   - all accounts in a block get the **same price** and the **same commission**;
   - partial fills allocated pro rata by order size, but not below a minimum lot size for some securities (for example, bonds);
   - new issues: get advance indications of interest, allocate **by client** (not by portfolio manager), and have a method for calculating allocations.

**Disclose trade allocation procedures** (curriculum L2997 to L2999): how accounts are chosen for an order and how amounts are set. Disclosure does not cure an unfair method.

**Systematic account review** (curriculum L3001 to L3003): supervisors review accounts regularly for favoritism and suitability. Buying a security in one account while selling it in another is allowed if the reasons for **both** sides are documented.

**Disclose levels of service** (curriculum L3005 to L3007): tell all clients whether different service levels exist for the same or different fees. Do not offer levels selectively.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Selective Disclosure (Ames tells best clients at lunch) | Violation | Telling select clients a week before all clients get the report is unfair. |
| 2 | Fair Dealing between Funds (commingled fund first, discretionary before nondiscretionary) | Violation (Jackson) | Priority to one fund or to discretionary accounts is unfair; disclosure would not cure an unfair policy. |
| 3 | Fair Dealing and IPO Distribution (Morris takes hot-issue shares) | Violation; also VI(A) | Take no shares yourself; prorate among clients; disclose the options to firm and clients. |
| 4 | Fair Dealing and Transaction Allocation (Preston gives winners to big client Colby) | Violation | Allocate before execution, or systematically (pro rata) right after. Agency cross trades need disclosure and advance client consent. |
| 5 | Selective Disclosure (Roberts tells large clients EPS will beat guidance) | Violation | His view of a likely upside surprise had to go to all clients. |
| 6 | Additional Services for Select Clients (Weng e-mails all, then calls top 3) | No violation | Release to all first, then extra service is fine. Calling the top 3 first would be a violation. |
| 7 | Minimum Lot Allocations (Hampton's bond allocation) | No violation | Not strictly pro rata, but giving each client at least the minimum lot is fair and keeps positions saleable. |
| 8 | Excessive Trading (Chan trades other plans more to pay for research for his father's plan) | Violation | All clients who use the research share its cost; but never add trades just to equalize commissions. |
| 9 | Limited Social Media Disclosures (Burdette posts buy call on X first) | Violation (Burdette); supervisor see IV(C) | A social media post before client release is selective disclosure. |
| 10 | Fair Dealing between Clients (Rove's holdings and performance comparison reports) | No violation | Monitoring reports help check fair treatment after the fact. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q18: Fair treatment of a new recommendation means **minimize the time** between decision and release. Telling everyone in the firm early raises leak risk. Sending to institutional clients before individuals discriminates and violates III(B).
- LM2 Q19: A client calls to buy a stock you just changed to "sell". Tell the client about the change **before** accepting the order. If the client still wants to buy, you must follow the order.
- LM1 PP7: The III(B) wording is "deal fairly and objectively with all clients". Distractors twist III(C) timing ("after" action) and III(A) priority ("equal balance").

> **Traps: III(B)**
> - Fair is not equal. An option that says the Standard requires "equal" treatment is wrong. The target is "approximately the same time" (curriculum L2967).
> - Order matters: all clients first, then follow-up with big clients is fine (Ex 6). Select clients first is a violation (Ex 1, 5, 9).
> - Disclosure never cures an unfair allocation method, and client consent cannot approve a patently unfair one (curriculum L2945, L2999).
> - Family accounts managed like client accounts are not excluded from hot issues (curriculum L2940). See VI(B) Ex 3: putting parents last is also a violation.
> - New issues are allocated by client, not by portfolio manager. Block trades: same price and same commission.
> - Discretionary accounts do not get priority over nondiscretionary ones (Ex 2).
> - Allocating after seeing which trades went up is a violation (Ex 4).
> - Changed recommendations matter even more than first ones (curriculum L2936).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Fair is not equal": think of Netflix price tiers where every tier still gets the new release. (YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo)
> - Trade allocations: same price for all participating clients. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ)
> - CFA Society Los Angeles deck: "'Fairly' does not mean 'equally'"; IPO allocation is a common exam issue. (https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf)
> - Candidates' own notes often list "fair dealing is not equal dealing" and family accounts treated like other clients as heavily tested. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZeKYZYJQBGg comments)

## III(C) Suitability

### Standard text
> 1. When Members and Candidates are in an advisory relationship with a client, they must:
>    a. Make a reasonable inquiry into a client's or prospective client's investment experience, risk and return objectives, and financial constraints prior to making any investment recommendation or taking investment action and must reassess and update this information regularly.
>    b. Determine that an investment is suitable to the client's financial situation and consistent with the client's written objectives, mandates, and constraints before making an investment recommendation or taking investment action.
>    c. Judge the suitability of investments in the context of the client's total portfolio.
> 2. When Members and Candidates are responsible for managing a portfolio to a specific mandate, strategy, or style, they must make only investment recommendations or take only investment actions that are consistent with the stated objectives and constraints of the portfolio.
>
> (curriculum L1142 to L1152)

### What it requires
- Part 1 applies only in an **advisory relationship**. Consider the client's needs, circumstances, and objectives (curriculum L3106).
- Review the client's knowledge, experience, and financial situation, including: the investment's risk versus the client's constraints; its effect on diversification; whether the client has the means to bear the risk (curriculum L3106 to L3110).
- Test: only what a **prudent person** would be willing to do (curriculum L3110).
- A proper suitability check does **not** prevent losses (curriculum L3106).
- Who carries the duty: members giving advice in an advisory relationship. People who only execute a retail client's specific order, and sell-side analysts, may not be able to judge suitability for the end client (curriculum L3112).

**Developing an investment policy** (curriculum L3114 to L3116)
- **MUST** gather client information at the **start** of the relationship: finances, personal data (age, occupation), attitude to risk, objectives.
- Put it in a **written IPS**: risk tolerance, return requirement, and all constraints (time horizon, liquidity, taxes, legal and regulatory, unique circumstances).
- The IPS should also set out roles and responsibilities and a schedule for reviewing the IPS.

**Risk profile** (curriculum L3118 to L3122)
- Measuring risk tolerance is one of the most important factors.
- Analyze and quantify strategy risk in advance where possible.
- Derivatives and synthetic products: watch the built-in **leverage**. Leverage and limited liquidity bear directly on suitability.

**Updating the IPS** (curriculum L3124 to L3131)
- Update **at least annually** and also **before** material changes to recommendations or decisions for the client (curriculum L3126).
- Changes to look for:
  - Individuals: dependents, tax status, health, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, wealth outside the portfolio, how well income covers needs.
  - Institutions: unfunded pension liabilities, withdrawal rights in a savings plan, a foundation's payout rules.
- If the client withholds information, judge suitability on what the client actually gave you (curriculum L3131).

**Diversification** (curriculum L3133 to L3136)
- Reasonable diversification is the norm, especially for fiduciaries.
- A high-risk investment can be suitable inside the whole portfolio, or when the client's objectives allow speculation.
- You are responsible only on the basis of the information and criteria the client actually provided.

**Unsolicited trade requests** (curriculum L3138 to L3147)
1. If you **know** the request is unsuitable: SHOULD refrain from the trade until you discuss your concerns with the client.
2. Small impact (small trade, little change to risk): educate the client on how it deviates from the IPS, then follow firm policy on client approval. At minimum the client SHOULD acknowledge the discussion and accept the conditions that make it unsuitable.
3. Material impact: SHOULD use the chance to **update the IPS**.
4. Client refuses to change the IPS but insists: some firms allow the trade in a **new unmanaged account**. If no option exists, decide whether to continue the advisory relationship.

**Managing to an index or mandate** (curriculum L3149 to L3151)
- Part 2: invest consistently with the stated mandate. Example: a large-cap income fund buying small-cap start-ups breaks the mandate.
- Managers of pooled funds are **not** responsible for whether the **fund** suits each investor who buys it. Suitability duty attaches only to advisory relationships.

### Recommended procedures
**Investment policy statement** (curriculum L3155 to L3165): cover
- Client identification: (1) type and nature of client, (2) separate beneficiaries, (3) approximate share of total client assets you manage.
- Objectives: (1) return (income, growth, keep purchasing power), (2) risk tolerance (suitability, stability of values).
- Constraints: (1) liquidity, (2) expected cash flows, (3) investable funds (assets, liabilities, commitments), (4) time horizon, (5) taxes, (6) regulatory and legal, (7) preferences, prohibitions, unique needs, (8) proxy voting responsibilities and guidance.
- Performance measurement benchmarks.

**Regular updates** (curriculum L3167 to L3173)
- Review objectives and constraints periodically; compare client constraints with capital market expectations. A change in either can change the allocation.
- **Annual** review is reasonable unless market or business reasons call for more often.
- If a review cannot happen, **document** the attempts.

**Suitability test policies** (curriculum L3175 to L3184): tests should look beyond potential return and include: effect on diversification; investment risk versus assessed risk tolerance; fit with the required strategy.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Investment Suitability: Risk Profile (Smith gives same 20% tech idea to Robertson and Lanai) | Violation | One client's strategy cannot be copied to a client with different needs. The older, wealthier client was the risk-tolerant one. |
| 2 | Investment Suitability: Entire Portfolio (covered calls for risk-averse Crosby) | No violation | Judge the whole portfolio, not each holding; she explained all outcomes and tax effects. |
| 3 | IPS Updating (Jones's net worth rises fourfold) | IPS must be updated | Higher ability to take risk and lower income need; consider more equity and growth stocks. |
| 4 | Following an Investment Mandate (zero-dividend stock in a high-income fund) | Violation unless allowed by the disclosed mandate | Stay inside the mandate. |
| 5 | IPS Requirements and Limitations (Gubler puts 4% in a locked-up VC fund) | Violation of III(A) and III(C) | Lockup breaks a liquidity-focused IPS; being under the equity limit does not help; know the client's business beyond the IPS text. |
| 6 | Submanager and IPS Reviews (Ostrowski picks lowest-fee subadviser) | May be violating | Compare client risk profiles with the manager's strategy; fee alone is not enough; a request to diversify abroad is a time to review IPSs. See V(A). |
| 7 | Investment Suitability: Risk Profile (Snead chases quarterly bonus with high-beta stocks) | Violation; see VI(A) | Strategy drift driven by pay, not by IPS changes, breaks long-term IPSs. |
| 8 | Investment Suitability (Shrub's new fund, no one turned away) | Violation; see V(A) | Set an IPS for each client and recommend only matching products. You cannot refuse a client's direct order, but discuss risks; you may decline new clients whose requests are far out of line. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q20: Brown recommended a stock minutes into the first meeting. The fix is to learn the client's needs, objectives, and risk tolerance **before** any recommendation. Explaining the company or your CFA credential is fine but not the missing step.
- LM1 PP6: Under III(C) you consider investments in the context of the client's **total portfolio**. "Clients' interests first" is III(A); "no misrepresentation" is I(C).
- LM1 PP4: a manager to a specific mandate must follow the portfolio's stated objectives and is **not** required to judge whether the mandate suits all investors.
- LM1 PP7: III(C) inquiry comes **prior to** action, not after.

> **Traps: III(C)**
> - Part 1 applies only to advisory relationships. Order-takers, sell-side analysts, and fund managers running to a mandate do not judge whether the fund suits each buyer (curriculum L3112, L3151).
> - Timing: inquiry before any recommendation; IPS at the start; update at least annually and before material changes.
> - Age alone does not set risk tolerance; wealth matters (Ex 1).
> - A risky asset can be suitable in the total-portfolio view (Ex 2, curriculum L3136).
> - Staying under an asset-class limit does not make an illiquid asset suitable (Ex 5).
> - Withheld information: you are judged on what the client gave you (curriculum L3131).
> - Unsolicited unsuitable trade ladder: refrain and discuss; small impact = educate and get acknowledgment; big impact = update IPS; still insists = unmanaged account or end relationship.
> - Fee-only selection of a subadviser: book says "may be violating" (Ex 6), not a flat violation.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Choosing a sub-manager solely on fees violates suitability." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ) The curriculum's wording is softer: "may be violating" (Ex 6).
> - Judge suitability in the context of the total portfolio and the IPS. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=coTBbAxXxTA, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gsfdl2CzaSc)
> - CFA Society Los Angeles deck: do not act on time-sensitive opportunities without first assessing suitability. (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf) This phrase is not in the 2026 curriculum text, but it fits the "inquiry before action" rule.

## III(D) Performance Presentation

### Standard text
> When communicating investment performance information, Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that it is fair, accurate, and complete. (curriculum L1156)

### What it requires
- Give credible performance information; do not misstate or mislead. Full disclosure of performance data is encouraged (curriculum L3263).
- Covers any practice that misrepresents a record: both performance **presentation** and performance **measurement** (curriculum L3264).
- Covers **past** and **reasonably expected** performance (curriculum L3264).
- Applies to individual accounts, composites, and an analyst's or firm's results (curriculum L3264).
- Do not state or imply that clients will earn a past rate of return (curriculum L3264).
- Not limited to separate-account managers: pooled funds too, and **research analysts** promoting the accuracy of their calls (curriculum L3265).
- Brief presentations: **MUST** make the detailed supporting information available on request (curriculum L3265).
- Best practice (not required): a brief presentation mentions that the information is limited (curriculum L3266).
- It is a "reasonable efforts" duty, not a guarantee (curriculum L1156).

### Key definitions
- GIPS: the best way to meet III(D) for those showing performance of assets they manage. III(D) **recommends**, it does not require, GIPS (curriculum L3272).

### Recommended procedures
**Apply the GIPS standards** (curriculum L3270 to L3272): encourage your firm to comply.

**Without GIPS, you can still comply by** (curriculum L3274 to L3288):
- considering the audience's knowledge and sophistication;
- showing a **weighted composite** of similar portfolios, not one "representative" account;
- including **terminated accounts**, with when they ended;
- disclosures that explain results: say when results are **simulated** or model results; say when a record belongs to a **prior entity**; say whether returns are **gross of fees, net of fees, or after tax**;
- keeping the data and records used to calculate the performance.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Performance Calculation and Length of Time (Taylor: "expect steady 25%") | Violation of III(D) and I(C) | One good year shown as typical; a firm-wide claim must cover all account types; "you can expect" is a guarantee (I(C)). |
| 2 | Performance Calculation and Asset Weighting (Judd's GIPS claim, composites not asset weighted) | Violation | The violation is the false claim of GIPS compliance; claiming GIPS means meeting all requirements. |
| 3 | Performance Presentation and Prior Fund/Employer (McCoy shows G&P record as Mastermind's) | Violation | Prior-firm results may be shown only with disclosure of where they were earned and your role. |
| 4 | Performance Presentation and Simulated Results (Davis's back-tested results) | Violation | Clearly label simulated results and explain they come from applying the model backward. (The book's facts say 1996 to 2003; its comment says 1995 to 2003.) |
| 5 | Performance Calculation and Selected Accounts Only (Kilmer's "balanced" composite) | Violation | Cherry-picking accounts, undisclosed exclusions, and changing composite members distort the record. |
| 6 | Performance Attribution Changes (Purell switches method to flatter stock selection) | Would violate if changed without proper notice | A change made only to look better is unfair. If truly better, report both methods and why. |
| 7 | Performance Calculation Methodology Disclosure (Singh told to show unlabeled best of TWR or MWR) | Following the instruction would violate | Unlabeled, best-of returns are not complete information. |
| 8 | Performance Calculation Methodology Disclosure (Richmond fund picks the higher month-end date) | Clark complies by questioning it | Choosing the more favorable date each month is a problem; raising it shows proper concern. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q21: The firm's stated policy adds new accounts after the first full month. Cupp added a big account early to catch a gain: **violation**, because the result is inaccurate under the stated policy. Distractor cited a GIPS cash-flow rule though the firm does not claim GIPS.
- LM2 Q22: Paper (one of five team managers) showed the whole fund's record as his own: **violation** for not disclosing it was a team result. The prior employer's written permission solved the permission issue, not the team issue.
- LM1 PP9: Performance presentation sits in Standard III, not Standard V.
- Link: LM2 Q6 (I(C)): a true but misleading average driven by one client's penny stock is misrepresentation; "true and accurate" is not a defense.

> **Traps: III(D)**
> - GIPS is recommended, not required. But a GIPS claim that is not fully true is a violation (Ex 2).
> - Brief presentation: details on request is a MUST; noting the limited nature is only best practice.
> - Covers research analysts' track records and pooled funds, not only separate accounts.
> - Prior-employer results are allowed with disclosure of source and role (Ex 3). Team results must be shown as team results (Q22).
> - Simulated, back-tested, or model results must be labeled (Ex 4).
> - No cherry-picking: terminated accounts stay in; no rotating composite members (Ex 5); follow your own stated inclusion policy (Q21).
> - Method changes (attribution, TWR versus MWR, period-end date) need disclosure; never pick whichever looks better (Ex 6 to 8).
> - "You can expect X%" is also I(C) (Ex 1).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - Repeated by several lecturers: brief presentation is fine if details are available on request; include terminated accounts; use weighted composites, not one representative account. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UZwVCjyk4uE, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo) These match the curriculum.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles: "GIPS encouraged but not required by the Standards." (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf) Matches curriculum L3272.

## III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must keep information about current, former, and prospective clients confidential unless:
> 1. The information concerns illegal activities on the part of the client or prospective client,
> 2. Disclosure is required by law, or
> 3. The client or prospective client permits disclosure of the information.
>
> (curriculum L1162 to L1168)

### What it requires
- Protect information about **current, former, and prospective** clients (curriculum L3371).
- Scope test (both parts): you got the information because of your special ability to handle part of the client's affairs, **and** it relates to that part of the relationship (curriculum L3371).
- If the law requires disclosure, or the information concerns the client's illegal activity, you **may have an obligation** to report it to the authorities (curriculum L3371). Not automatic.
- **Status of client:** the duty continues after the relationship ends. A client or former client may expressly authorize disclosure, and you may follow its terms (curriculum L3375 to L3377).
- **Laws** (curriculum L3379 to L3381):
  - If the law requires disclosure, comply.
  - If the law requires confidentiality even about illegal activity, do **not** disclose.
  - Rules limiting information flow between departments can also create confidentiality issues.
  - When in doubt, consult compliance or legal counsel **before** disclosing.
- **Electronic information** (curriculum L3383 to L3385):
  - Watch for accidental disclosure.
  - You need not become an IT security expert, but understand your employer's policies.
  - Encourage regular confidentiality training for **all** staff, including receptionists and portfolio associates.
- **CFA Institute investigations** (curriculum L3387 to L3389):
  - III(E) does not stop you cooperating with the Professional Conduct Program (PCP).
  - Where the law allows, treat the PCP as an extension of yourself for investigations of **your own** conduct (shall).
  - You are **encouraged** to cooperate on investigations of **others**.
  - Giving confidential information to the PCP is not a violation; the PCP keeps it confidential.

### Recommended procedures
- Safest approach: share client information only with **authorized fellow employees who also work for that client** (curriculum L3395).
- Before sharing information outside the confidential relationship (and not about illegal acts), ask (curriculum L3395 to L3399):
  - In what context was it disclosed? Is it relevant to the work for the client?
  - Is it background that would help you serve the client better?
- Follow firm rules on electronic communication and storage; if none, encourage some (curriculum L3401).
- **Communicating with clients** (curriculum L3403 to L3407): make sure firm communication methods prevent accidental release; review privacy measures regularly; discuss with clients the right channels for sending confidential information, since not every firm tool is suitable.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Possessing Confidential Information (Connor asked for hospital building plans) | Must refuse | Information is clearly within the confidential relationship. |
| 2 | Disclosing Confidential Information (Moody tips a charity about a client's planned gift) | Violation | Good intentions do not excuse disclosure. |
| 3 | Disclosing Possible Illegal Activity (Samuel and Garcia's improper pension charges) | Steps, not a violation finding | Inform supervisor; employer works with client to fix it; if not, get legal counsel. She may well have a duty to disclose and to resign as manager. |
| 4 | Disclosing Possible Illegal Activity (Bradford suspects CFO embezzling) | Steps | Ask compliance or legal counsel whether rules require reporting. |
| 5 | Accidental Disclosure of Confidential Information (client posts personal details in group page) | No violation | Clear warnings were given and the post was removed quickly; more training or controls may help. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q23 (III(A) and III(E)): Bronson is asked by a big donor for the endowment's financial data while trustees are away. **Do not send it.** Benefit to the client does not override confidentiality. Get permission **before**, not notice after.
- LM2 Q24: Townsend mentions her charity board role and its campaign in her newsletter: **no violation**. Giving client contact details to the charity would be a violation. There is no rule requiring the charity's approval.
- LM1 PP4: III(E) has no "reasonable and adequate basis" test; that phrase belongs to V(A).

> **Traps: III(E)**
> - Covers former and prospective clients too.
> - Three exceptions only: client's illegal activity, required by law, client permits.
> - Illegal activity: you "may have an obligation" to report; consult counsel first. If law demands secrecy, keep it.
> - PCP: sharing client information with the PCP is not a violation (own conduct: treat PCP as yourself where law permits).
> - Benefit to the client or a good cause does not justify disclosure (Ex 2, Q23).
> - Wrong timing: telling the client afterwards is not permission (Q23).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Trap answer" in a Level II/III set: sharing client information with the CFA Institute PCP is NOT a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Matches curriculum L3389.
> - Advisers sharing client information with outside accountants without client consent IS a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Consistent with the three exceptions.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles and 300Hours say confidentiality also applies to "deceased" clients. (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf, https://300hours.com/cfa-level-1-ethics-cheat-sheet/) The word "deceased" does not appear in the 2026 curriculum; the curriculum says "former" clients.

## IV(A) Loyalty

### Standard text
> In matters related to their employment, Members and Candidates must act for the benefit of their employer and not deprive their employer of the advantage of their skills and abilities, divulge confidential information, or otherwise cause harm to their employer. (curriculum L1172 to L1174)

### What it requires
- Do nothing that would injure the firm, deprive it of profit, or deprive it of your skills (curriculum L3476).
- Order: clients' interests **always** come before the employer's (curriculum L3476).
- Follow employer policies only to the extent they do not conflict with law or the Code and Standards (curriculum L3476).
- **Not a blanket rule:** you need not put the employer ahead of important personal and family duties. If personal matters interfere on a "regular or significant basis", you SHOULD talk with the employer (curriculum L3478).

**Employer responsibilities** (curriculum L3480 to L3486)
- Duties run both ways.
- You are **encouraged** (not required) to give your employer a copy of the Code and Standards.
- Employers are **not** bound by the Code and Standards, but should not set conflicting policies.
- Senior management should design pay and incentives that do not encourage unethical behavior.

**Independent practice** (curriculum L3488 to L3494)
- Abstain from independent competitive activity that conflicts with the employer.
- You may still start an independent business while employed.
- If you plan independent practice for pay, you **MUST** notify the employer and describe: (1) the services, (2) expected duration, (3) compensation (curriculum L3490).
- SHOULD not render services until the employer consents to **all** terms (curriculum L3494).

**Leaving an employer** (curriculum L3496 to L3524)
- Keep acting in the employer's interest until the resignation is **effective** (curriculum L3498).
- No bright line between allowed preparation and breach; judge each case.
- Possible violations, especially in combination (curriculum L3498 to L3508):
  1. misappropriating trade secrets;
  2. misusing confidential information;
  3. soliciting the employer's clients before leaving;
  4. self-dealing (taking a business opportunity or information that belongs to the employer);
  5. misappropriating clients or client lists.
- Preparations to compete are generally allowed if they do not breach loyalty (curriculum L3510).
- Do not contact existing **or potential** clients before leaving to solicit them for the new firm (curriculum L3510).
- After giving notice, follow the employer's policy on telling clients about your departure (curriculum L3510).
- Do not take records or files without the previous employer's **written** permission (curriculum L3510).
- After leaving (curriculum L3512 to L3516):
  - skills and experience are not confidential;
  - simple knowledge of former clients' names is generally not confidential, unless an agreement or law says so;
  - convenience copies of firm records SHOULD be erased or returned unless the firm allows you to keep them;
  - contacting former clients is allowed if the contact details do not come from the former employer's records and no noncompete forbids it; public information may be used;
  - review any noncompete agreement before leaving.
- Industry agreements (for example the US "Protocol for Broker Recruiting"): protection applies only if you follow **all** its steps. Under it you may take some general client contact information, must give a copy to local management, and only you may use it at the new firm (curriculum L3518 to L3524).

**Social media** (curriculum L3526 to L3532)
- Follow firm policies, especially when leaving; messages that may reach current clients follow the firm's departure-notice rules.
- Accounts created only for professional reasons, including firm-approved client accounts, are **firm assets**; transfer or delete as directed.
- Best practice: keep separate personal and professional accounts. If one account mixes both, discuss with the employer how to treat it.

**Whistleblowing** (curriculum L3534 to L3536)
- Protecting market integrity and clients comes before your own and your employer's interests.
- If the employer acts illegally or unethically, acts that would normally breach loyalty (going against instructions, breaking policies, copying records) may be justified.
- Only if the intent is clearly to protect clients or the market, **not for personal gain**.

**Nature of employment** (curriculum L3538 to L3545)
- Employee or independent contractor? It depends on the employer's **control**: set hours and location, facilities provided, expenses paid, whether you seek other work, how many clients or employers you have.
- Contractors: duties are set by the oral or written agreement with the client; once agreed, you must follow it.

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Practice | Any service the employer **currently** offers for pay (curriculum L3494). |
| Undertaking independent practice | Engaging in competing business, as opposed to preparing to start it (curriculum L3494). |
| Self-dealing | Taking for yourself a business opportunity or information that belongs to the employer (curriculum L3506). |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L3547 to L3567)
Encourage the firm to adopt, if missing:
1. **Competition policy:** know the rules on offering similar services outside the firm. If asked to sign a noncompete, make sure the details are clear **before** signing.
2. **Termination policy:** how departures are disclosed to clients and staff, whether social media updates are allowed, handover of research and accounts, and any agreement on taking client information.
3. **Incident-reporting procedures:** know the whistleblowing policy; many firms must offer confidential, anonymous reporting.
4. **Employee classification:** know your status (part time, full time, contractor) and which policies apply.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soliciting Former Clients (Magee solicits clients and prospects before resigning) | Violation | Duty is to the current employer while employed; soliciting current and prospective clients first is a breach. |
| 2 | Former Employer's Documents and Files (Hightower plans to copy lists, models, and his own spreadsheets) | Violation | Taking **any** employer records without consent, even ones you prepared, in any medium, is a violation. |
| 3 | Addressing Rumors (Winston considers an employee buyout) | Consistent with IV(A) | A buyout rests on employer and client permission; if you doubt management's honesty, consult legal counsel. |
| 4 | Ownership of Completed Prior Work (Clay's study for Bradley) | Would violate if she gives the same ideas to her new firm first | Even on a handshake deal, Bradley gets to act first unless it waives its rights; take none of its materials. |
| 5 | Ownership of Completed Prior Work (unpaid intern Madeline copies software) | Violation | An unpaid intern is an employee (paid in experience); copying firm software is misappropriation. |
| 6 | Soliciting Former Clients (Chisolm contacts former clients at new firm) | No violation, with conditions | Fine if loyal before leaving, no solicitation until gone, and no former-employer materials used. |
| 7 | Starting a New Firm (Allen registers a competing company) | No violation | Registering on her own time, without soliciting or operating, is preparation, not independent practice. |
| 8 | Competing with Current Employer (departing staff want to answer an RFP) | Violation unless permitted | Needs permission from the employer **and** the RFP issuer. |
| 9 | Externally Compensated Assignments (Mota, paid part-time mayor) | No automatic violation | If duties may detract from his job, he should discuss and agree with the employer. |
| 10 | Soliciting Former Clients (McQuillen uses public phone numbers) | No violation | No employer records used and no noncompete. |
| 11 | Whistleblowing Actions (Rasmussen sees fund returns ignore a market drop) | No violation by whistleblowing | After supervisor and compliance fail, follow the firm's whistleblowing policy. See I(D), IV(C). |
| 12 | Soliciting Former Clients (Crome brings 70% of clients) | Contacting is OK; using employer client records is a violation | Only publicly available information may be used. |

| 15 | Confidential Firm Information (Gupta's personal blog counterpoints) | Violation | Indirectly reveals firm views, and uses firm resources for personal advantage without consent. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q25: Correct statement: independent practice that could compete with the employer needs the employer's consent. There is no total ban on independent practice, and preparations to compete are allowed if they do not interfere with current duties. (The option says "written consent"; the solution text says "consent".)
- LM2 Q26: Jackman offers US$10,000 to advisers who join with committed assets. An adviser still employed is not eligible, because soliciting the current employer's clients is prohibited. Disclosing the incentive does not cure it. The offer itself is not a violation.
- LM1 PP8: IV(A) = do not deprive the employer of your skills and abilities. Gifts that compromise independence are I(B). IV(B) needs consent from **all parties involved**, not the employer only.

> **Traps: IV(A)**
> - Clients first, then employer. Loyalty to the employer is not a blanket rule over family life.
> - Preparation is fine (registering a company on your own time, Ex 7); competing or soliciting before the resignation is effective is not (Ex 1, Ex 8).
> - The solicitation ban covers potential clients too.
> - "Practice" means services the employer **currently** offers for pay.
> - Taking any employer record is a violation, even your own spreadsheets, even on a home device (Ex 2). Written permission is needed.
> - After leaving, the test is the **source** of contact data (public versus employer records) and any noncompete (Ex 10, Ex 12).
> - Unpaid interns count as employees (Ex 5). No written contract is needed for the duty (Ex 4).
> - Whistleblowing excuses disloyal acts only if aimed at protecting clients or markets, not personal gain.
> - Firm-approved social media accounts belong to the firm.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - While employed you may not solicit clients using employer records; after leaving, a chance meeting is OK; work done for the employer belongs to the employer. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qd_2KJKO-8c)
> - A recalled practice question ruled a personal blog post ahead of the employer's downgrade a loyalty issue, not MNPI. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q) Compare curriculum Ex 15.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles: "Preparing to compete is not the same as competing; cannot take records; duty of loyalty applies to employees, not independent contractors." (https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf) Curriculum nuance: contractors are bound by their agreement with the client (curriculum L3544).
> - CFA Society Los Angeles caveat on "when in doubt, disclose": disclosure is not enough where a Standard requires **consent**, such as employer consent under Standard IV. (https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf)

## IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must not accept gifts, benefits, compensation, or consideration that competes with or might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with their employer's interest unless they obtain written consent from all parties involved. (curriculum L1176 to L1180)

### What it requires
- Get permission from the employer **before** accepting pay or benefits from third parties for (1) services to the employer, or (2) any services that might conflict with the employer's interest (curriculum L3694).
- Covers direct pay from the client and indirect benefits from third parties (curriculum L3694).
- Why: such deals can affect loyalty and objectivity. The employer is entitled to know all such arrangements so it can judge your motives and the **true cost** of your services (curriculum L3698).
- Part-time staff: discuss possible limits on competing services during **negotiation and hiring**; IV(B) applies to limits identified then (curriculum L3699).

### Key definitions
- **Written consent:** any communication that can be documented, such as a retrievable e-mail (curriculum L3694).
- Two triggers in the text: the benefit **competes with**, or **might reasonably be expected to create a conflict** with, the employer's interest.

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L3701 to L3703)
- Make an **immediate written report** to your supervisor **and** the compliance officer of any proposed extra compensation.
- The party offering it should **confirm** the details, including client performance incentives.
- The report should state the nature of the compensation, the approximate amount, and the duration.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notification of Client Bonus Compensation (Whitman's Monaco trip if returns reach 15%) | Violation | A contingent client bonus must be disclosed in writing and approved by the employer; it may bias him toward that account. |
| 2 | Notification of Outside Compensation (Jones's free club membership as director) | Violation | Nonmonetary benefits can create conflicts just like cash. |
| 3 | Prior Approval for Outside Compensation (Hollis meets ABC Oil CEO, declines travel, attends dinner) | No violation | Approval in advance, declined travel, and disclosure of an unplanned dinner afterward. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q27: A client offers Jurgen pay based on annual capital appreciation. Get employer permission (in writing) **before** accepting. Real concerns (other clients, short-term pressure) do not mean the arrangement is banned.
- LM1 PP8: consent must be from **all parties involved**, not the employer only.

> **Traps: IV(B)**
> - Standard text: written consent from all parties involved. Guidance stresses employer permission; recommended procedure adds confirmation by the offering party.
> - Consent comes **before** accepting.
> - Nonmonetary and performance-contingent benefits count (Ex 1, Ex 2).
> - IV(B) is about pay that competes or conflicts with the employer. Gifts that threaten independence and objectivity are I(B).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - Mnemonic from YouTubers: client gift for **past** performance: disclose to employer; tied to **future** performance: additional compensation needing written employer consent. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ) Consistent with Ex 1.
> - Conflicting claim: one YouTuber recalled a question where a trip for beating next year's benchmark needed only disclosure. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q) This conflicts with the curriculum (Ex 1 and Q27): follow the curriculum.

## IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that anyone subject to their supervision or authority complies with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and the Code and Standards. (curriculum L1182 to L1184)

### What it requires
- Promote compliance with laws, rules, regulations, **firm policies**, and the Code and Standards by everyone you supervise (curriculum L3745). (The guidance adds "firm policies"; the Standard text does not.)
- Applies to anyone under your control or influence, **whether or not** they are CFA members or candidates (curriculum L3747).
- Supervisors need in-depth knowledge of the Code and Standards (curriculum L3747).
- What is reasonable depends on how many people you supervise and what they do. You may **delegate**, but must teach delegates how to prevent and detect violations (curriculum L3749).
- **Minimum:** reasonable efforts to **prevent and detect** violations by making sure effective compliance systems exist. An effective system goes beyond writing a code and reviewing actions (curriculum L3751).
- SHOULD run regular education and training, and reward ethical behavior, not just business results (curriculum L3753).
- Limited authority (large firm): still work with superiors to build compliance tools: code of ethics, policies, training, ethical incentives, firm-wide standards such as GIPS or the Asset Manager Code (curriculum L3755 to L3767).
- Inadequate system: bring it to senior management and recommend fixes. If you clearly cannot supervise properly, SHOULD **decline in writing** to accept supervisory duties until reasonable procedures exist (curriculum L3769).

**System for supervision** (curriculum L3771 to L3777)
- Make reasonable efforts so that procedures are established, documented, communicated, **and followed**; then monitored and enforced.
- "Adequate" = meets industry standards, regulations, the Code and Standards, and the firm's circumstances.
- Procedures must exist **before** a violation and target the activities most likely to go wrong.
- When you learn of a possible violation, **MUST promptly** start an assessment of its extent. Not enough: relying on the employee's word, or only reporting up the chain and warning the employee. Meanwhile limit the person's activities or increase monitoring.

**Supervision includes detection** (curriculum L3779 to L3781)
- Make reasonable efforts to **detect** violations; use written procedures and periodic review.
- If you set reasonable procedures and a real compliance program, you **may not** be in violation when someone hides misconduct anyway.
- But you **may** violate IV(C) if you know or should know the procedures are not being followed.

### Key definitions
- **Code of ethics** = broad principles and fiduciary duties for all staff, in plain language. **Compliance procedures** = detailed rules that put the code into daily practice. Keep them **separate** (curriculum L3790 to L3792).

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L3785 to L3857)
**Codes of ethics:** encourage the employer to adopt one, keep it plain and free of detailed procedures, and give it to **clients** (curriculum L3789 to L3793).

**Adequate compliance procedures should** (curriculum L3799 to L3817):
- be in a clear, accessible written manual tailored to the firm, easy to understand;
- name a compliance officer with clear authority and resources;
- describe the supervision hierarchy and duties;
- set checks and balances;
- outline scope, permitted conduct, and how monitoring and testing are documented;
- set procedures for reporting violations and sanctions.

**Once a program exists, a supervisor should** (curriculum L3817 to L3830): send it to staff; update it; keep educating; issue reminders; include professional conduct in performance reviews; review employee actions; enforce after a violation.

**When a violation is found, a supervisor should** (curriculum L3832 to L3840): respond promptly; investigate fully; increase supervision or restrict the wrongdoer meanwhile; review procedures.

**Training** (curriculum L3842 to L3848): regular ethics training plus a code builds a culture of integrity and helps people spot pitfalls.

**Incentives** (curriculum L3852 to L3857): a "succeed at all costs" culture pushes even good people astray. Tie pay to client interests and **how** results are achieved, not **how much** is earned for the firm.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supervising Research Activities (Mattock; staff trade and tip before a downgrade is published) | Violation (Mattock) | Needed procedures to stop and detect trading or tipping on unpublished rating changes. |
| 2 | Supervising Research Activities (Miller passes on an unreviewed merger memo) | Violation (Miller) | Sending research without checking its basis or MNPI risk; a "not reviewed" note does not cure it. |
| 3 | Supervising Trading Activities (Mason ignores trainee's free-riding trades) | Violation (Mason) | Failed to investigate; a supervisor paid on commissions must be extra alert to his own conflict. |
| 4 | Supervising Trading Activities and Record Keeping (Tabbing allocates good fills to the profit-sharing plan) | Violation (Henry), twice | Failed to supervise her futures trading and to set record-keeping procedures; not knowing is no defense when no system exists. |
| 5 | Accepting Responsibility (Rasmussen's concerns dismissed) | Violation by supervisor and compliance officer | They must review concerns raised and build an ethical culture. |
| 6 | Inadequate Procedures (Witt's weak report gets posted) | Lack of compliance procedures (IV(C) issue) | The supervisor must set a review process for all contracted analysts. Witt himself breaches V(A). |
| 7 | Inadequate Supervision (CIO Papis moves allocation to a friend) | Need for review at all levels | Even the CIO needs oversight; firms need procedures to detect such actions. See V(A), V(B), VI(A). |
| 8 | Supervising Research Activities (Graf, junior analyst posts buy call on X) | Violation (Graf) | No procedures to stop research release via social media; train staff on business use of personal social media. |
| 9 | Supervising Research Activities (Chen skips review of a late-night report) | Violation (Chen) | Time pressure and a good track record do not excuse skipping the required review. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q7 (main answer III(A), with I(D) and IV(C)): Anderb got better personal prices from the broker and filed reports only in months with no trades. Supervisor Bates violated IV(C) by not enforcing reporting rules. Owning the same securities as clients, and using the same broker, are not prohibited in themselves.
- LM2 Q33 / VI(B) Ex 4: letting an employee skip personal trading reports is a supervisor violation.
- LM1 PP3: the supervisor standard is broader than detection and prevention alone.

> **Traps: IV(C)**
> - Covers non-members you supervise too.
> - Delegation does not remove your duty.
> - Minimum = reasonable efforts to prevent **and** detect. Training and ethical incentives are SHOULD items.
> - Reasonable procedures plus a real program: an undetected, hidden violation may not be your violation. Knowing or should-know that procedures are ignored: violation.
> - On learning of a problem: promptly investigate. Taking the employee's word, or only warning and reporting up, is not enough.
> - No system and inadequate authority: decline supervision in writing.
> - The same story often has two verdicts: the whistleblower complies (IV(A) Ex 11), the supervisors violate (IV(C) Ex 5).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "A supervisor with reasonable procedures is not in violation just because someone hid misconduct." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aT8sGNsyFo0) Matches curriculum L3781.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles: "Informing subordinates is not enough; monitoring and detection are required." (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf)

## V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must:
> 1. Exercise diligence, independence, and thoroughness in analyzing investments, making investment recommendations, and taking investment actions.
> 2. Have a reasonable and adequate basis, supported by appropriate research and investigation, for any investment analysis, recommendation, or action.
>
> (curriculum L1188 to L1192)

### What it requires
- How much diligence depends on: (1) your or your firm's investment philosophy, (2) your role in the decision, (3) the support and resources your employer gives (curriculum L3962).
- Make reasonable efforts to cover all relevant issues. Offering clients the supporting information **enhances transparency** (good practice) (curriculum L3964).
- A reasonable basis comes from a **balance** of sources suited to the decision: company reports, third-party research, model output (curriculum L3972).
- Factors to consider (not a full list) (curriculum L3974 to L3990): macro conditions; a company's history; industry and business cycle; a fund's fees and management history; model output and limits; quality of assets in a securitization; choice of peer groups.
- Diligence **minimizes**, it cannot remove, downside surprises. You are judged on information available at the time (curriculum L3992).

**Secondary and third-party research** (curriculum L3994 to L4012)
- Make reasonable, diligent efforts to check it is sound.
- If you suspect it lacks a sound basis, you **MUST NOT** rely on it.
- Check the source and accuracy of all data. Internet sources (blogs, aggregators, social media) need **more** review than established research firms.
- Criteria: assumptions; rigor; date and timeliness; objectivity and independence.
- You may rely in good faith on others in your firm who vetted the source (for example, a data vendor chosen by senior management), unless you have reason to doubt it.
- SHOULD check that the firm reviews approved research providers regularly; if not, encourage it.

**Quantitative research** (curriculum L4014 to L4026)
- Users need not be experts in every technical detail, but **MUST** understand a model's assumptions and limits and how its results are used.
- Make reasonable efforts to **test** model output; validate **before** using it.
- Use a broad range of assumptions, including negative and extreme scenarios; leaving out bad outcomes can misstate value.

**Developing quantitative techniques** (curriculum L4028 to L4032)
- Creators of models need a **higher** level of diligence than users.
- Understand the technical side; test fully **before** release.
- Databases may miss full market cycles; test with scenarios outside the data, including negative events.

**Selecting external advisers and subadvisers** (curriculum L4034 to L4050)
- Review managers as diligently as individual securities.
- SHOULD have standardized criteria, including: the adviser's code of ethics; its compliance and internal controls; quality of its published returns; its investment process and whether it sticks to its stated strategy.
- CFA Institute tools: Asset Manager Code, GIPS, Model RFP.

**Group research** (curriculum L4052 to L4054)
- A group report is the consensus, not necessarily your view, even with your name on it.
- If you disagree but believe the consensus has a reasonable and adequate basis and is independent and objective, you **need not** dissociate.

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Secondary research | Research done by someone else **inside** your firm (curriculum L3996). |
| Third-party research | Research done **outside** your firm, such as a broker, bank, or research firm (curriculum L3996). |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4056 to L4072)
Encourage the firm to have:
1. A policy that research, credit ratings, and recommendations have a reasonable and adequate basis, approved by a supervisory analyst or review committee **before external release**.
2. Written due diligence guidance for analysts, supervisors, and review committees.
3. Measurable criteria for research quality and accuracy over time (pay may be tied to them, applied consistently).
4. Written minimum scenario testing for all computer models (breadth of scenarios, accuracy over time, sensitivity of cash flows to inputs).
5. Measurable criteria for outside data and research providers, and how often they are reviewed.
6. Standard criteria for judging external advisers, including how often the allocation is reviewed.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Hawke prices IPOs only on company size) | Violation | Take on only work you can handle; "justify later" is no basis. |
| 2 | Sufficient Scenario Testing (Dhaliwal shows best case as base case) | Violation | Give a range of scenarios with probabilities. |
| 3 | Developing a Reasonable Basis (Witt uses a few articles and hype) | Violation | Articles and conventional wisdom alone are not a basis. See IV(C). |
| 4 | Timely Client Updates (Chandler ignores a manager's staff exodus) | Failed V(A) | Diligence continues until delivery; reflect new material facts. |
| 5 | Group Research Opinions (committee changes Mastakis's forecast) | Name removal not required | Fine if the group process has a reasonable basis; she may ask to remove her name. |
| 6 | Reliance on Third-Party Research (McDermott's two-person firm) | Permitted | Only with regular due diligence on the research's soundness. |
| 7 | Due Diligence in Submanager Selection (Ostrowski picks lowest fee) | May be violating | Review services, performance, and costs, not fee alone. See III(C). |
| 8 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Papis moves money to a friend) | Violation | Arbitrary choice; run a proper search. See IV(C), V(B), VI(A). |
| 9 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Shrub's fund of a friend's funds) | Violation | Friendship and past knowledge are no substitute for full review of operations and transparency. See III(C). |
| 10 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Thompson tests falling house prices) | Complies | Testing beyond historical data is good diligence. See I(B). |
| 11 | Use of Quantitatively Oriented Models (Liakos's untested combined strategy) | Violation | Test the strategy as a whole, not just its parts; be able to explain results. |
| 12 | Successful Due Diligence/Failed Investment (Newbury's hedge fund loses 60%) | Complies | A sound basis does not guarantee results; explain the process and that losses can happen. |
| 13 | Quantitative Model Diligence (Cannon adds "Expert CFA" blog factors) | Violation | A blog may give ideas, but test the effect yourself before use. See VII(B). |
| 14 | Selecting a Service Provider (Smith picks a "black box" for its looks) | Insufficient diligence | Review all options and check fit with the firm's process. |
| 15 | Subadviser Selection (Jackson picks highest return from a GIPS list) | Insufficient review | Returns and a GIPS claim alone are not enough. |
| 16 | Manager Selection (Green uses only RFP answers) | Likely not satisfied | Verify: interviews, regulatory filings, custodian or auditor talks. |
| 17 | Technical Model Requirements (Dupont's buggy fix; PM Smith follows blindly) | Both violate | Dupont violates though he never trades; Smith violates by not understanding the model he relies on. See V(B). |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q28: Willier changes a weak "hold" to a strong "buy" after overhearing another analyst. **Violation** of V(A): another person's opinion is not a reasonable basis. If it had prompted new research that justified the change, it would be fine. The distractor citing fact versus opinion names the wrong Standard.

> **Traps: V(A)**
> - "Reasonable and adequate basis" belongs to V(A), not to III(E) or V(B).
> - Reason to doubt a source: you must not rely on it. Good-faith reliance on firm-vetted sources is fine.
> - Blogs and social media need more checking; they can give ideas, not a basis (Ex 13).
> - Model users: understand assumptions and limits. Model builders: higher diligence. Both can violate (Ex 17).
> - Test combined strategies as a whole (Ex 11). Present ranges, not the best case (Ex 2).
> - Manager selection: not fee only, not returns plus GIPS only, not friendship, not unverified RFP data.
> - A loss after proper diligence is not a violation (Ex 12).
> - Group research: you need not dissociate if the process is sound.
> - Verdict wording varies: "may be violating" (Ex 7), "likely not satisfied" (Ex 16).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Don't change a recommendation just because a respected person disagrees; redo the research." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ar8-0Oogeuo) Matches Q28.
> - If a quant model was only a secondary basis and is dropped, the fundamental research still gives a reasonable basis. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc)
> - Keyword "immediately": updating materials with a new third-party rating immediately, without checking it, points to a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc)
> - Conflict to note: a CFA Society Los Angeles deck summarizes "dissociate if you disagree with group research". (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf) The curriculum says you need **not** dissociate if you are confident the process gave a reasonable and adequate basis (curriculum L4054, Ex 5). Follow the curriculum.

## V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must:
> 1. Disclose to clients and prospective clients the nature of the services provided, along with information about the costs to the client associated with those services.
> 2. Disclose to clients and prospective clients the basic format and general principles of the investment processes they use to analyze investments, select securities, and construct portfolios and must promptly disclose any changes that might materially affect those processes.
> 3. Disclose to clients and prospective clients significant limitations and risks associated with the investment process.
> 4. Use reasonable judgment in identifying which factors are important to their investment analyses, recommendations, or actions and include those factors in communications with clients and prospective clients.
> 5. Distinguish between fact and opinion in the presentation of investment analysis and recommendations.
>
> (curriculum L1194 to L1208; part 1 was added in the 2023 revision, effective 2024)

### What it requires
**Nature of services and costs** (curriculum L4247 to L4260)
- Disclose **all** professional services, including what will **not** be provided.
- **MUST** tell clients and prospects about services and the costs **to the client** (curriculum L4251).
- Give a reasonable amount of detail. **Not required:** exact dollar amounts (a fee based on future asset value cannot be known in advance), or the **firm's** own cost of providing the service (curriculum L4251).
- Best practice: describe services and costs in **writing at the outset**, in the client agreement (curriculum L4250 to L4251).
- Ongoing duty: if services or costs change, update **all** affected clients and prospects on a timely basis (curriculum L4251 to L4252).
- Disclose **all** costs, including those of affiliates and third parties whose products are used (curriculum L4254).
- Client sophistication is **not** a reason to skip disclosure, but you may tailor the detail to the client (curriculum L4254).
- Manner is up to you if it is appropriate, accurate, timely, and complete; written is best practice (curriculum L4255 to L4259).
- Firm-written disclosures can be used but do not remove your responsibility. If they are flawed: tell the firm; supplement them if possible; if not fixed, document objections and dissociate (curriculum L4259).
- Applies generally only to **client-facing** members (curriculum L4260).

**Investment process** (curriculum L4262 to L4266)
- Describe how decisions are made, the factors with positive and negative effects, and significant risks and limits.
- Keep clients informed of changes on an ongoing basis.
- Show how an investment affects the whole portfolio (a stock that is 90% of one client's assets versus 2% of a diversified one).
- If outside advisers manage part of the assets, SHOULD tell clients what expertise they add.

**Forms of communication** (curriculum L4270 to L4274)
- Includes in-person, phone, broadcast, internet, social media, from one word ("buy") to 100+ page reports.
- Social media may not reach all clients: take steps to treat all fairly.
- Capsule recommendations (a stock list): SHOULD tell clients more information is available.

**Risks and limitations** (curriculum L4276 to L4281)
- Outline significant risks and limits. Leverage is in general a significant risk to disclose.
- Disclose general market risks and significant risks of complex instruments. May also consider counterparty, country, sector, security, and credit risk.
- Report significant limits such as **liquidity** and **capacity**.
- Judged **ex ante**: disclose risks known at the time; you cannot be expected to disclose unknown risks.
- A one-time loss after disclosure does not prove poor disclosure. Not knowing a risk may be a V(A) problem, not necessarily V(B).

**Report presentation** (curriculum L4285 to L4288)
- Include what the reader needs to follow and challenge your reasoning.
- You may focus on some areas and skip others if you clearly state the limits of the report's scope.
- Quantitative advice: back it with available reference material, apply methods consistently, and highlight method changes.

**Fact versus opinion** (curriculum L4290 to L4294)
- Separate opinion from fact. Common failure: not marking earnings estimates, dividend outlooks, or price forecasts as opinions.
- Separate facts from statistical conjecture; failing to state limits of statistical projections may violate V(B).
- Discuss model assumptions with clients; do not oversell model accuracy.

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Ability to sell an investment in a timely way at a reasonable cost (curriculum L4280). |
| Capacity | The amount invested above which new money hurts returns (curriculum L4280). |
| Ex ante | Judged by what was known when the action was taken (curriculum L4281). |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4296 to L4300)
- Whether you included the right factors is judged case by case, not by checklist.
- Encourage a rigorous review process for research before release.
- To support later review, you **MUST** keep records of the nature of the research, and SHOULD be able to give clients more information on factors left out of the report.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Costs of Services to Clients (ABC keeps structuring fees when deals do well) | Violation (Alphonso) | Undisclosed compensation; sophistication of investors and "win-win" do not excuse it. |
| 2 | Costs of Services to Clients (James's manager search shows all fees) | No violation | Uniform comparison of performance and full fee schedules meets V(B). |
| 3 | Disclosure of Changed Fee Calculation Methodology (Maalouf's firm changes fee base) | Must disclose | Changing method without telling clients is improper, even if fees fall. |
| 4 | Sufficient Disclosure of Investment System (Williamson's top-five newsletter) | Violation | Need not give every detail, but must explain the basic process and logic. |
| 5 | Providing Opinions as Facts (Dox's "fact" of 500,000 ounces) | Violation if issued | A reserve estimate is an opinion. |
| 6 | Proper Description of a Security (Thomas hides how the structured product works) | Violation | Describe structure, risks, leverage, and both rising and falling rate scenarios; disclose liquidity terms. |
| 7 | Notification of Fund Mandate Change (May raises market-cap ceiling) | Must tell existing clients | Telling only prospects and consultants is not enough. |
| 8 | Notification of Fund Mandate Change (May adds non-US stocks) | Must tell clients | Other notifiable changes: adding derivatives, relaxing limits such as beta. |
| 9 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (RJZ switches P/E model to DDM) | Must disclose | Moving to a forecast-based model is a material change. |
| 10 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (RJZ quietly shifts to passive) | Violation | A substantial process change must be disclosed. |
| 11 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (stock picks move to a committee) | Must disclose | Clients may care about who decides. |
| 12 | Sufficient Disclosure of Investment System (Chinn explains new outside managers) | No violation | He disclosed the change, so the client can decide. |
| 13 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (Papis's quiet adviser switch) | Violation | Trustees and plan members must learn of the change. See IV(C), V(A), VI(A). |
| 14 | Notification of Errors (Dupont's model error; Smith rebalances silently) | Violation (Smith) | Material errors in the process and the fix must be told to clients. Dupont's issue is V(A). |
| 15 | Notification of Risks and Limitations (capacity limit of about US$100m omitted) | Violation (Yakovlev and Wellard) | Disclose capacity limits even when far below them. |
| 16 | Notification of Risks and Limitations (Ramon praises VaR) | Insufficient | Explain how inputs were chosen and the model's limits. |
| 17 | Notification of Risks and Limitations (Smith: "Our clients are sure to benefit") | Conflicts with V(B) | Opinion presented as fact; also check V(A) basis. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q29: Scott's report says the price will rise "because the dividend will increase" though it depends on uncertain legislation. **Violation** of V(B): opinion stated as fact. The legislative outlook is conjecture, not MNPI. Using uncertain future events is allowed if labeled as opinion.
- LM1 PP4: V(B) requires **prompt** disclosure of changes that might materially affect the investment process.
- LM1 PP9: fact versus opinion sits in V(B).
- LM1 PP3: the Standards address risks and limitations of recommendations.

> **Traps: V(B)**
> - Cost disclosure is required; exact dollar figures and the firm's own costs are not.
> - Written disclosure at the outset is best practice, not a must.
> - Ongoing duty: changed fees or methods need disclosure even if the change helps clients (Ex 3).
> - Sophisticated clients still get disclosure (Ex 1).
> - Applies mainly to client-facing members. In Ex 14 the client-facing PM Smith violates V(B); model builder Dupont violates V(A).
> - Risk disclosure is ex ante. Unknown risk later causing a loss: maybe V(A), not V(B).
> - Capacity and liquidity are named limits; disclose capacity even far from the threshold (Ex 15).
> - Existing clients must hear of mandate or process changes, not only prospects (Ex 7).
> - Estimates, forecasts, reserves, and "sure to benefit" claims are opinions.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - Keyword trap: "will" versus "may"; presenting an opinion as fact. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aT8sGNsyFo0)
> - Disclose model limitations and risks. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eyh4l5Y7iw)
> - Official fact confirmed by YouTube and web notes: the 2024 revision added the nature of services and costs to V(B). (https://www.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/support/ethics/summary-of-the-2023-revisions-to-the-cfa-institute-code-and-standards.pdf) Videos made before 2024 omit this part.

## V(C) Record Retention

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must develop and maintain appropriate records to support their investment analyses, recommendations, actions, and other investment-related communications with clients and prospective clients. (curriculum L1210 to L1212)

### What it requires
- Keep records that show the scope of research and the reasons for actions or conclusions (curriculum L4449).
- Applies to buy and sell decisions **and** to reviews that lead to **no change** (curriculum L4449).
- Which records depends on your role. Paper or electronic is fine (curriculum L4449).
- Examples of records (curriculum L4451 to L4467): meeting notes with the company; press releases and presentations; model output and analysis; model input parameters; risk analyses of portfolio impact; selection criteria for external advisers; notes from IPS review meetings with clients; outside research reports.
- **New media** (curriculum L4469 to L4483): format does not remove the duty. Keep e-mails, texts, blog posts, social media posts. Firm and regulator policies may lag, putting **more** duty on you.
- **Records are firm property** (curriculum L4485 to L4487):
  - You cannot take originals or **copies** to a new employer without the old employer's **express consent**.
  - You cannot reuse old recommendations or reports at the new firm, because the support is not available.
  - Re-create supporting records at the new firm from public sources or from the company, **not** from memory or old-employer sources.
- **Local requirements** (curriculum L4489 to L4491): follow regulator and firm rules; meeting them satisfies V(C). With no regulator or firm rule, CFA Institute **recommends at least seven years**.

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4493 to L4495)
- The duty to keep records generally falls on the **firm**.
- But you must archive research notes and documents (paper or electronic) that support your current investment communications, to help the firm comply.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record Retention and IPS Objectives and Recommendations (Lindstrom, tech-heavy benchmark, unhappy client) | Complies if records exist | Keep records of benchmark weights, the agreed IPS, explanations given, and regular IPS updates. |
| 2 | Record Retention and Research Process (Young's interviews, visits, surveys) | Must document; failure violates | Keep copies of all inputs, including secondary and third-party research. |
| 3 | Records as Firm, Not Employee, Property (Blank takes model records to a competitor) | Violation | Records belong to the old firm; re-create them at the new one. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q30: Cannan saved client files on her home computer working weekends, then used them after being laid off. **Violation:** no permission to keep or use them; she should have deleted them. Own time and own computer do not make the files hers. Using a personal computer for work is not itself banned.
- LM1 PP5: record maintenance is V(C), not II(A).

> **Traps: V(C)**
> - Reviews that change nothing still need records.
> - Seven years is only a CFA Institute recommendation for when no law or firm rule exists. Meeting the local rule is enough.
> - Records are firm property; even copies need express consent. Re-create from public or company sources, never from memory.
> - The firm carries the main duty, but you must still archive your support.
> - Social media and texts count as records.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - The web research could not confirm the "seven years" figure in the online Handbook (https://www.finquiz.com/blog/10-minute-review-cfa-ethics-standards/). The 2026 curriculum does state it (curriculum L4491), so it is official for the exam.

## VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must avoid or make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity and interfere with respective duties to their clients, prospective clients, and employer. Members and Candidates must ensure that such disclosures are prominent, are delivered in plain language, and communicate the relevant information effectively. (curriculum L1216 to L1218; renamed and revised in 2023, effective 2024)

### What it requires
- **Best practice:** avoid actual conflicts and even the appearance of conflicts (curriculum L4545).
- When avoiding is not reasonable, clear and complete disclosure is **necessary** (curriculum L4547).
- Avoid or fully disclose all **actual and potential** conflicts to clients, prospects, and employers (curriculum L4549).
- Disclosure **MUST** be prominent, in plain language, and effective. You decide how often and how. Best practice: update disclosures when a conflict changes materially (for example, bonuses move from annual to quarterly profits). Err on the side of caution (curriculum L4553).
- Common source: pay structures, especially bonuses that reward short-term results (curriculum L4545).

**To employers** (curriculum L4555 to L4563)
- Give enough information for the employer to judge the impact.
- Report **promptly**: owning stock you analyze or recommend, outside board seats, financial or other pressures that could affect decisions.
- If the employer bans certain activities (personal trading, outside boards), you **MUST** comply.
- Take reasonable steps to avoid conflicts; if not resolved, report them promptly.

**To clients** (curriculum L4565 to L4575)
- Disclosure lets clients judge your motives.
- Must **always** be avoided or disclosed: issuer relationships such as directorships or consulting; investment banking, underwriting, and financial relationships; broker-dealer market making; material beneficial ownership of stock.
- Take reasonable steps to find and disclose your **firm's** conflicts.
- Market making: tells clients trades may be with the firm's own account and the firm cares about the price.
- Disclose conflicts from fee arrangements, subadvisory deals, nonstandard fees, and arrangements where the firm benefits from recommendations (for example, fund fee rebates).

**Cross-departmental conflicts** (curriculum L4577 to L4580): pressure from investment banking, issuers, or marketing to cover or praise a stock; broker-sponsored limited partnerships. Resolve or disclose.

**Stock ownership** (curriculum L4582 to L4584): the **most common** conflict. Banning ownership is simplest but may be too restrictive. **MUST** disclose any beneficial ownership in what you recommend.

**Board member or director** (curriculum L4586 to L4592): three conflicts:
1. duties to clients versus duties to the company's shareholders;
2. director pay in shares or options may tempt trading that raises their value;
3. access to material nonpublic information, or the perception that it flows to your firm.
- Remedy: isolate the director from investment decision makers with **firewalls**.

### Key definitions
- **Conflict of interest:** any matter that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity, or raise doubt that your judgment is free of bias (curriculum L4545).
- **Beneficial ownership** (VI(A)): you have (1) a direct or indirect pecuniary interest, or (2) the power to vote or direct the vote, or (3) the power to sell or direct the sale (curriculum L4573).

### Recommended procedures
- The 2026 curriculum has **no** separate Recommended Procedures section for VI(A); it goes straight from guidance to examples. (Personal-trading procedures are under VI(B).)

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conflict of Interest and Business Relationships (Weiss; firm is Jimco's M&A adviser) | Must disclose in the report | Underwriting and advisory ties threaten objectivity. |
| 2 | Conflict of Interest and Business Stock Ownership (Hobbs upgrades the new 25% owner) | Best: stop coverage; minimum: disclose to all clients | Portfolio managers must also disclose the ownership when acting on it. |
| 3 | Conflict of Interest and Personal Stock Ownership (Fargmon's wife inherits shares) | Minimum: disclose to employer and in report | Best practice: ask for another analyst. A spouse's holding counts. |
| 4 | Conflict of Interest and Personal Stock Ownership (Roberts holds penny stock she would rate "buy") | Must disclose to employer; consider declining | If assigned anyway, disclose in the report. The conflict exists even if her view would be the same. |
| 5 | Conflict of Interest and Compensation Arrangements (Snead's quarterly bonus) | Violation | Must tell clients of a pay change that conflicts with their long-term IPSs. Performance pay itself is allowed. |
| 6 | Conflict of Interest and Compensation Arrangements (Carter paid by a stock promoter) | Violation | Minimum: disclose to clients. Best: decline the pay. |
| 7 | Conflict of Interest and Requested Favors (Papis and his friend Nagle) | Violation | He did not tell his **employer** about the personal relationship. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q31: The conflict that must be avoided or disclosed is XYZ's **substantial position in JNI for its own account**. Buying JNI's products, or a brother-in-law who is a JNI supplier, is too remote.
- LM2 Q32: Jamison's firm is Britland's long-time M&A adviser and two officers sit on Britland subsidiary boards. **Best:** do not write the report. If not avoided, disclose **both** the client and the director relationships. Disclosing only one is wrong; refusing to give an opinion does not remove the duty.

> **Traps: VI(A)**
> - "Best" stems: avoid beats disclose. "Minimum" stems: disclose.
> - Disclose to clients, prospects, **and** the employer. Papis (Ex 7) failed toward his employer.
> - Firm conflicts count: underwriting, market making, M&A advisory, board seats, ownership stakes.
> - Spouse and family holdings count as beneficial ownership.
> - The conflict exists even if your opinion would not change (Ex 4).
> - Remote links (using the company's products, distant relatives) usually need no disclosure (Q31).
> - Board seats: firewalls.
> - No Recommended Procedures section for VI(A) in 2026.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "When a question describes a conflict and asks what to do, the best answer is almost always to avoid the conflict." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g)
> - "When in doubt, disclose" is called the "golden rule" by 300Hours (https://300hours.com/cfa-ethics-tips/) and CFA Society Los Angeles (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf). Caveat from CFA Society Los Angeles: since 2024 VI(A) puts avoidance first, and disclosure does not help when a Standard prohibits the act or requires consent. (https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf)
> - Older videos call VI(A) "Disclosure of Conflicts"; that name is outdated since 2024. (YouTube notes, for example https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gsfdl2CzaSc)

## VI(B) Priority of Transactions

### Standard text
> Investment transactions for clients and employers must have priority over investment transactions in which a Member or Candidate is the beneficial owner. (curriculum L1220 to L1222)

### What it requires
- Clients' and employers' interests come before personal financial interests. Client trades also come before trades for the member's **firm** (curriculum L4660).
- Prevents conflicts and the **appearance** of conflicts.
- Personal investing is fine if all three hold (curriculum L4666):
  1. the client is not disadvantaged;
  2. you do not personally benefit from trades made for clients;
  3. you follow applicable regulations.
- A personal trade against current recommendations (for tuition, a home, a margin call) is allowed under the same three tests (curriculum L4666 to L4667).
- Owning the same securities as clients, or co-investing, does not always create a conflict; some clients want aligned interests. Personal or firm positions must **never** hurt client investments (curriculum L4671).
- **Nonpublic information** (curriculum L4675):
  - do not pass **any** nonpublic information to a person whose relationship makes you a beneficial owner of their securities;
  - do not pass nonpublic information to **anyone** if it is **material**.
- Personal trades in beneficially owned accounts only after clients and employers have had an **adequate opportunity** to act (curriculum L4679).
- Personal transactions include your own account, family accounts (spouse, children, other immediate family), and accounts with a direct or indirect pecuniary interest such as a trust or retirement account (curriculum L4679).
- Family accounts that are **client** accounts: treat like any other client, with no special treatment and no disadvantage. You may still face employer or legal preclearance and reporting rules (curriculum L4679).

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4683 to L4720)
Urge the firm to set personal trading policies fitted to its size and clients, and to disclose them prominently. Basic procedures:
1. **Limited participation in equity IPOs:**
   - Two conflicts: taking an attractive opportunity from clients, and the IPO acting as a reward or incentive from the provider.
   - Avoid by not taking part in IPOs. Supervisors should review IPO participation systematically.
   - Preclear IPO participation even when there is no conflict with clients.
   - Do not benefit from clients' market position (preferred trading, limited offerings, oversubscription).
2. **Restrictions on private placements:**
   - Strict limits and supervisory review.
   - Biggest conflict for those managing large pools or acting as plan sponsors, who may be offered placements as rewards.
   - Avoid deals that look like favors or gifts.
   - If the investment later goes public, you have an incentive to recommend it regardless of suitability.
3. **Blackout or restricted periods:**
   - Set before client trades, to prevent front-running.
   - At minimum, everyone involved in investment decisions has the **same** restricted period.
   - Large firm: effectively a total trading ban. Small firm: more likely to stop front-running.
4. **Reporting requirements** (best way to enforce):
   - Disclose beneficially owned holdings at the start of employment and **at least annually**, handled confidentially.
   - Duplicate confirmations of all personal trades and account statements sent to the firm. Two purposes: signals independent checking (deters misconduct), and shows the flow of trades, which holdings alone cannot.
   - Preclearance of all planned personal trades.
5. **Disclosure of policies:** tell investors your personal trading policies in a helpful way, not boilerplate such as "personnel are subject to policies regarding personal trading".

### Key definitions
- **Front-running:** trading personally before client accounts (curriculum L4702).
- **Beneficial ownership:** see VI(A) (pecuniary interest, voting power, or power to sell).

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal Trading (Long delays a buy call to trade first) | Violation | Using knowledge before the employer can benefit. |
| 2 | Trading for Family Member Account (Baker buys hot issues for husband's account) | Violation as described | Fill the fund first, husband's account only after; disclose the account to the employer. |
| 3 | Family Accounts as Equals (Toffler gives parents' client account IPOs last) | Violation | Family client accounts must be treated equally, not disadvantaged; preclearance and reporting may apply. |
| 4 | Personal Trading and Disclosure (Michaels buys ahead of the buy list, skips forms) | Violation (Michaels); IV(C) (supervisor) | Supervisor let him work without the form. Tipping others who trade would be II(A). |
| 5 | Trading Prior to Report Dissemination (Riley trades options 7 minutes after TV comments) | Violation (Riley) | Wait until clients can receive and absorb the recommendation; firm trades come after clients. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q33 (negative stem): the statement that clearly **conflicts** with recommended procedures is "report personal trades to employers only if regulators require it". Firms should compare employee and client trades regardless of regulation, confidentially. Disclosing policies to clients and having employees clear trades are consistent.
- LM2 Q34: Rose may sell stock his firm rates "strong buy" to pay tuition, once the firm is properly informed. No ban on trading against the firm's view if clients are not hurt; no need to match client trades.
- LM2 Q7: owning the same securities as clients is not prohibited; VI(B) only requires clients and employer to come first.

> **Traps: VI(B)**
> - Order: clients, then employer, then you. Client trades also come before **firm** trades (Ex 5).
> - Trading against a recommendation for personal needs is allowed if the three tests are met (Q34).
> - Same holdings as clients is not a violation in itself (Q7).
> - Family **client** accounts: equal treatment. Last place is a violation too (Ex 3).
> - Nonpublic information: to beneficially owned persons, none at all; to others, none if material.
> - "Adequate opportunity" means clients have received and absorbed the news (7 minutes after TV is too soon, Ex 5).
> - IPO abstention, preclearance, blackouts, duplicate confirms, annual holdings reports are **recommended procedures**, not the Standard itself.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Family members who are fee-paying clients must be treated like any other client; making the mother wait until all others traded was ruled a violation." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZeKYZYJQBGg, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-JAwzhU-A0o) Matches Ex 3.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles and FinQuiz: order is client, employer, self; family accounts are included. (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf, https://www.finquiz.com/blog/10-minute-review-cfa-ethics-standards/)

## VI(C) Referral Fees

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must disclose to their employer, clients, and prospective clients, as appropriate, any compensation, consideration, or benefit received from or paid to others for the recommendation of products or services. (curriculum L1224 to L1226)

### What it requires
- Tell the employer, clients, and prospects about any benefit for referrals (curriculum L4766).
- Purposes: let them judge (1) any **partiality** in the recommendation and (2) the **full cost** of the services (curriculum L4766).
- Works **both ways**: fees you receive **and** fees you pay to others who refer clients to you (curriculum L4766).
- **Timing:** before entering any formal agreement for services (curriculum L4767).
- **Content:** the nature of the consideration (flat fee or percentage, one-time or continuing, performance-based, research, other noncash benefit) **and** its estimated dollar value (curriculum L4767).
- Consideration includes all fees: cash, soft dollars, or in kind (curriculum L4767).
- It is a disclosure rule, not a ban.

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4769 to L4772)
- Encourage the employer to set referral fee procedures. The firm may ban referral fees entirely; if not, set steps for approval.
- Employees should tell clients about approved referral programs and give the employer regular updates, **at least quarterly**, on the amount and nature of compensation.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Outside Parties (Brady and Lewis Brothers; White takes Maxwell's plan) | Violation (White) | Referral fees paid in research and directed commissions, for an indefinite period, must be disclosed. Noncash counts. |
| 2 | Disclosure of Interdepartmental Referral Arrangements (Handley paid per internal referral) | Violation | Internal referral pay is treated the same as third-party pay; disclose at referral, in writing, with nature and value. |
| 3 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Informing Firm (Roberts directs commissions to classmate's broker) | Violation | The stated failure is not telling her **employer**. |
| 4 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Outside Organizations (Burl donates fees to child's school) | No violation | Employer and school approved first; client told while setting the IPS. |
| 5 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Outside Parties (consultant Arrow takes kickbacks) | Violation (Arrow) | Undisclosed payments violate even if the manager choice was objective; also follow local consultant rules. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q35: A stated purpose of disclosure is to help the client judge **partiality**. The other purpose is to judge full cost. There is no rule to discount fees. Disclose to prospects **before** the relationship starts, not after.

> **Traps: VI(C)**
> - Fees received **and** paid both count.
> - Disclose before the formal agreement, with nature **and** estimated dollar value.
> - Noncash (research, directed commissions, soft dollars) counts.
> - Internal referrals between departments count (Ex 2).
> - Employer must know too (Ex 3).
> - A good outcome does not excuse a hidden kickback (Ex 5).
> - Quarterly updates to the employer are a recommended procedure, not the Standard.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - In a case discussed on YouTube, disclosure sent only to new clients after they signed drew the comment that it "should have been told first". (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Matches the "before any formal agreement" rule.
> - "When in doubt, disclose and get consent." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA)

## VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must not engage in any conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the CFA designation or the integrity, validity, or security of CFA Institute programs. (curriculum L1230 to L1232)

### What it requires
- Prohibits conduct that weakens public trust that the charter reflects merit and ethics (curriculum L4836).
- Covers **all** CFA Institute programs, including CIPM and Investment Foundations (curriculum L4836).
- Covered conduct includes (curriculum L4838 to L4848):
  - giving or receiving help (cheating) on any exam;
  - breaking program rules and testing policies;
  - giving confidential program or exam information to candidates or the public;
  - ignoring or trying to get around exam security;
  - misusing a link with CFA Institute for personal or professional gain;
  - misrepresenting information on the Professional Conduct Statement or in the Continuing Education Program.

**Confidential program information** (curriculum L4852 to L4864)
- Candidates may not disclose: specific exam questions, and **broad topic areas and formulas tested or not tested**.
- All of it stays confidential until CFA Institute releases it.
- Allowed: discussing non-confidential information and curriculum with others or in study groups.
- CFA Institute polices blogs and forums; suspected violations should be reported.

**Other program rules** (curriculum L4866 to L4880)
- Breaking any testing policy (calculator, personal belongings, Candidate Pledge) violates VII(A).
- **Volunteers** must not disclose or **solicit** confidential material gained before or during exam development and grading: questions, deliberations, scoring information. Same for volunteers in other programs.

**Expressing an opinion** (curriculum L4882 to L4888)
- You may criticize CFA Institute, its programs, policies, and positions.
- But you may not reveal content: actual questions or subjects covered or not covered.

**Candidate Pledge** (curriculum L4890 to L4898): no giving or receiving exam content before or during the exam; after the exam, disclose no part of it and remove no exam materials; all materials are CFA Institute property. Breaking the rules voids results and may lead to suspension or end of candidacy.

### Recommended procedures
- The 2026 curriculum has **no** Recommended Procedures section for VII(A).

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sharing Exam Questions (proctor Nero tips two candidates) | Violation (Nero and both candidates) | Giving and receiving both breach VII(A). |
| 2 | Bringing Written Material into Exam Room (formula on Sullivan's palm) | Violation | Also breaks program rules and the Pledge. |
| 3 | Writing after Exam Period End (Davis keeps filling ovals) | Violation | Unfair advantage, even random answers; ignored the proctor. |
| 4 | Sharing Exam Content (Rossi's blog) | Partly | "Hard but fair" and self-assessment are fine; naming topics covered ("18 derivatives questions") and not covered ("no IPO questions") is a violation. |
| 5 | Sharing Exam Content (Gagne's forum discussion of hard questions) | Violation | Giving and asking for question details. |
| 6 | Sharing Exam Content (Pena answers a prep firm's request) | Violation (Pena) | Prep firm staff who asked also violate if they are members or candidates. |
| 7 | Discussion of Exam Grading Guidelines and Results (grader Whitcomb) | Violation | Broke the Grader Agreement; time passed does not matter. |
| 8 | Compromising CFA Institute Integrity as a Volunteer (Ramirez picks his own clients as speakers) | Violation | Using a volunteer role to benefit yourself and clients. |
| 9 | Compromising CFA Institute Integrity as a Volunteer (Warrenski's GIPS committee seat) | Violation | You may state the role factually, but not imply a client advantage. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q36: Park posts that inventories and taxes had several questions and the exam was very hard. **Violation** for revealing tested topics. Saying it was hard is allowed; using outside study materials is allowed.

> **Traps: VII(A)**
> - Topics and formulas tested **or not tested** are confidential, not only specific questions.
> - Opinions and criticism of CFA Institute are fine; content is not.
> - Asking for confidential information violates, not only giving it.
> - Testing policy breaches (calculator, belongings, stop-writing) are VII(A).
> - Applies to all CFA Institute programs and to volunteers.
> - Misrepresentation on the Professional Conduct Statement is VII(A).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - You may criticize CFA Institute and say the exam was hard. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ar8-0Oogeuo, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU)
> - Indirect hints also violate, for example "I expected X and it didn't come". (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU) Matches Ex 4.
> - Formula on your body, and writing after time is called, are violations. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7rq5_qdUfM) Match Ex 2 and Ex 3.

## VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program

### Standard text
> When referring to CFA Institute, CFA Institute membership, the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA Program, Members and Candidates must not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or implications of membership in CFA Institute, holding the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA Program. (curriculum L1234 to L1238)

### What it requires
- No promises or guarantees tied to the designation; no exaggeration (curriculum L4984).
- Factual statements about the benefits of the designation are allowed (curriculum L4986).
- Not allowed: overstating competence, or implying that **superior performance** can be expected from a charterholder (curriculum L4986).
- Allowed: stressing commitment to ethics and professional conduct, or the rigor of the CFA Program (curriculum L4986).
- Claims about the **relative merits** of CFA Institute, the Program, or the Code are allowed if stated as **opinion**; statements not framed as opinion need factual support (curriculum L4986 to L4988).
- Applies to every form: letterhead, business cards, bios, directory listings, ads, brochures, resumes, spoken statements (curriculum L4990).

**Membership** (curriculum L4992 to L5000)
- Members are "regular" and "affiliate" members who met the Bylaws.
- To stay active, **every year**: (1) file the Professional Conduct Statement, and (2) pay dues.
- Failing either: no longer an active member; may say you were a member in the past.

**Using the designation** (curriculum L5002 to L5014)
- Charterholders are encouraged to use it correctly, possibly with an accurate explanation of its requirements.
- A charterholder completed the Program and the required work experience, and must also keep up membership requirements.
- Failing a membership requirement forfeits the right to use "CFA" until reactivated; you may say you held it in the past.
- Pseudonyms or online names that hide your identity should not carry the CFA designation.
- Use is governed by the Professional Conduct Statement Agreement, signed before first use and reaffirmed each year.

**Candidacy** (curriculum L5016 to L5026)
- You may mention participation, but state clearly you are a **candidate**; never imply a partial designation.
- You are a candidate if (1) your registration is accepted and you are enrolled for a specified exam, or (2) you sat a specified exam and results are not yet out.
- Registered but not sitting: not an active candidate until you enroll again.
- Never state or imply a partial designation or an **expected completion date**.
- The charter is awarded only after program requirements are met and the CFA Institute **Board of Governors** approves.
- Saying you passed each level in consecutive years is a fact; claiming superior ability from it is a violation.

**Proper and improper references** (curriculum L5028 to L5064)
| Proper | Improper |
|---|---|
| "Completion of the CFA Program has enhanced my portfolio management skills." | "CFA charterholders achieve better performance results." |
| "John Smith passed all three CFA Program exams in three consecutive years." | "John Smith is among the elite, having passed all three CFA exams in three consecutive attempts." |
| "The CFA designation is globally recognized and attests to a charterholder's success in a rigorous and comprehensive study program..." | "As a CFA charterholder, I am the most qualified to manage client investments." |
| "The credibility that the CFA designation affords and the skills the CFA Program cultivates are key assets for my future career development." | "As a CFA charterholder, Jane White provides the best value in trade execution." |
| "I enrolled in the CFA Program to obtain the highest set of credentials in the global investment management industry." | "Enrolling as a candidate in the CFA Program ensures one of becoming better at valuing debt securities." |
| "I passed Level I of the CFA Program." | "CFA, Level II" |
| "I am a 2010 Level III candidate in the CFA Program." | "CFA, Expected 2011" |
| "I passed all three levels of the CFA Program and may be eligible for the CFA charter upon completion of the required work experience." | "John Smith, Charter Pending" |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L5066 to L5069)
- Give written VII(B) guidance to the firm's legal, compliance, public relations, and marketing staff, since others often misuse the designation.
- Encourage templates approved by a central authority (for example compliance) for references to membership, the designation, and candidacy.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q37: "Judy Albert, CFA Level II" on a card is a **violation** (partial designation). Tye saying he takes part in the Program and completed Levels I and II is **fine**.
- LM2 Q38: Rule may say he passed in three consecutive years (a fact), but "highest possible scores" is a **violation**: candidates do not receive actual scores; the exam is pass or fail and topic results are banded.
- LM2 Q39: Quinn, waiting for Level III results, wrote that he finished the Program and will be eligible for the charter: **violation**. Until results arrive he is still a candidate. Saying the Program improved his skills is fine.
- LM1 PP10: VII(B) is the explicit Standard VII rule about referring to the CFA Program. Loyalty belongs to III(A) and IV(A); supervision to IV(C).

> **Traps: VII(B)**
> - Facts are fine (consecutive years, levels passed, the Program improved my skills). Superior performance or ability claims are not.
> - Relative-merit claims only as opinion.
> - Active status needs **both** annual PCS **and** dues. Retirement needs the formal retired status.
> - No "CFA, Level II", "CFA, Expected [year]", "Charter Pending", no expected completion date.
> - No completion or eligibility claims before results are released (Q39). No "highest score" claims (Q38).
> - Pseudonyms should not carry "CFA" (Ex 6).
> - Covers every format, including personal business cards (Ex 3).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "CFA is an adjective, not a noun or a degree"; write it without periods and never as part of a firm name. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VqwxCgjPkTU, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7rq5_qdUfM) These rules are not in the 2026 curriculum text; treat them as Handbook or provider guidance.

# Part 4: Level III application cases

Source: 2026 curriculum, Learning Module 3 "Application of the Code and Standards: Level III" (curriculum L5617 to L6965). Cases are taken from CFA Institute casebooks and are stated to be consistent with the 11th edition of the Standards of Practice Handbook (curriculum L5654). Line tags such as "L5720" point to the official curriculum file.

## 4.1 What this module tests

**Learning outcomes (curriculum L5619 to L5622):** at mastery level you must (1) evaluate practices, policies, and conduct against the Code and Standards, and (2) explain how the conduct does or does not violate them.

**Setting:** private wealth and institutional asset management. The book asks whether actions "taken or not taken" violate the Code and Standards, so omissions (silence, nondisclosure, failure to investigate) count (curriculum L5638).

### The 4-step framework (Exhibit 1, curriculum L5628 to L5636)

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify | Relevant facts; stakeholders and duties owed; ethical principles; conflicts of interest |
| 2. Consider | Situational influences; additional guidance; alternative actions |
| 3. Decide and act | Choose and carry out the action |
| 4. Reflect | Was the outcome as anticipated? Why or why not? |

- The steps "do not need to be addressed in the sequence shown," but a review of the outcome should conclude the process, because it improves future decisions (curriculum L5626).
- Referencing the Code and Standards helps identify the ethical principles on which sound decisions are based (curriculum L5642).

### Principles that run through all the cases

| Principle | Where shown |
|---|---|
| Situational influences are real and testable: political pressure, bonus schemes, supervisor pressure, deference to a boss | SIC L5660; Hockett L5744, L5760; Plume L5798; Benning L5833; Mehrotra L5696; Yousoff L6097 |
| Following an employer policy is not a defense | Benning IPO policy L5878; Graeme L5981 |
| Disclosure in another document (prospectus) is "necessary but not sufficient"; VI(A) disclosure must be prominent, plain language, effective | Plume L5807 to L5811; Graeme L5985 |
| Disclosure does not replace independent and objective judgment | SIC L5665 |
| Silence or passive acquiescence can be a violation | Mehrotra L5703 to L5707 |
| A client request does not override the IPS; balance the request with the IPS | Madam Tan L6110, L6126 |
| Supervisors must actively investigate; escalating or relying on assurances is not enough | Balmer L5999 vs L6011; Yousoff L6084 |
| When in doubt, go to compliance or legal counsel | Sim L6101 |

### Recommended fixes and policies proposed in the case solutions

These are the book's corrective actions. The module has no separate policy section; they sit inside the solutions.

- Build a rigorous **governance framework** for investment decisions so political bias is resisted (I(B); curriculum L5661).
- Develop an **IPS before recommending** any investment (Kochanskis, curriculum L5750). Update the IPS **at least annually and before material changes** (Milgram, curriculum L6010). Update it when a client-requested trade may materially affect the portfolio (Madam Tan, curriculum L6114).
- Firms can **remove the conflicting incentive**: drop Plume's IPO-proceeds bonus (L5818); prohibit employees from trading against their own recommendations (L5820); do not pay Benning a signing bonus tied to client moves or a bonus tied to client IPO participation (L5843, L5847); Graeme can refuse new-issue commissions or else tell **all** clients (L5981).
- Do not link analyst pay directly to investment banking deals; require public disclosure of actual conflicts; give analysts an environment where they are neither coerced nor enticed (curriculum L5818).
- Personal trades: wait until the client or fund trade is done (Corrales, L5682). Best practice: disclose co-investing (L5686).
- Supervisors: on learning of a possible violation, **promptly initiate an investigation** (L5999, L6011).
- Escalate concerns to compliance or legal counsel. Reporting to regulators is not compelled unless law requires it, but may be prudent (L6101).
- Get the client's own written signature on account documents; use a cosigner or written power of attorney for third-party instructions (Deacon, L5897).
- Keep records, including notes from client meetings (Sim, L6065 to L6069).

## 4.2 Case-by-case verdicts

### Case 1: Sovereign Investment Corporation (curriculum L5644 to L5709)

**Facts in brief.** Victoria Adebayo (CFO and CIO of a developing country's Sovereign Investment Corporation, SIC) runs National Investments (NI), which has a **dual mandate**: finance local projects with positive returns that attract co-investors, plus develop local capital markets. She "quickly decides" to fund the ruling party's urban project ahead of the opposition governor's mining project. Her ex-colleague Anthony Corrales (hedge fund HFI) gets the standard disclosure package, invests 25% of his personal portfolio in NI before HFI invests; Adebayo assures HFI that NI is committed to high returns. HFI pays local subadvisers "deal fees" to win government support and books them as operating expenses. Adebayo's CFA dues are unpaid but she still uses "CFA"; she tells a prospect that all senior staff are charterholders while her report Ani Mehrotra stays silent. Mehrotra asks exam-prep students which questions were hardest.

| Issue | Standard | Verdict | Lesson / fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adebayo's quick, political choice of project | V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis | Violation | Must investigate each project independently; not III(C), because both projects suit NI's goals (L5660) |
| Best way to fight political bias | I(B) Independence and Objectivity | Fix | Rigorous governance framework. Wrong: approval by higher government (adds political influence); wrong: just disclose (disclosure does not replace objective judgment) (L5661 to L5665) |
| Assuring HFI that NI is committed to high returns | III(C) Suitability (managing to a mandate) | Violation | Dual mandate; promising return maximization breaks it (L5676 to L5680) |
| Giving Corrales the standard package | III(B) / II(A) | No violation | Same information other investors get; no MNPI (L5676) |
| Regular direct updates to Corrales | III(B) Fair Dealing | No violation | Fine if all investors are treated fairly and get the same disclosures (L5680) |
| Corrales buys NI personally before HFI | VI(B) Priority of Transactions | Violation | Front-running even if HFI is not harmed; fix: wait until HFI completes its investment. Co-investing alone is not a conflict; best practice is to disclose it (L5682 to L5686) |
| Booking subadviser "deal fees" as operating expenses | I(C) Misrepresentation (primary) | Violation | Hides bribery; also "very likely" I(A). Hiring and relying on legitimate subadvisers is fine, so not V(A) or I(B) (L5688) |
| Adebayo uses "CFA" with lapsed membership | VII(B) | Violation | Right to use the designation needs dues plus annual Professional Conduct Statement; she may say she was previously a member (L5703) |
| Mehrotra stays silent | I(A) Knowledge of the Law | Violation | Silence "at minimum, assists." Fix: talk to Adebayo, then SIC compliance, then dissociate from her sales meetings (L5703 to L5707) |
| Exam prep: asking students about hardest questions | VII(A) | Violation | Soliciting specific question content violates. Sharing that the exam was "difficult" is the least likely violation; saying "no questions on insider information" violates (L5709) |

### Case 2: Marcia Lopez, David Hockett, and the Kochanskis at BankGlobal (curriculum L5713 to L5775)

**Facts in brief.** Lopez (passed Level I) asks for a card saying "CFA, Level I"; supervisor Hockett, CFA, tells her to add the expected charter year. Hockett's team gets analyst calls about 45 minutes before research changes are published and trades at once for its discretionary accounts. For the Kochanskis (both 61, USD 7.4 million), Lopez builds a model portfolio (simple average of four funds' returns) and a composite of the team's similar discretionary accounts that **excludes terminated accounts**; Hockett makes her use only proprietary funds. They recommend a balanced portfolio knowing only age, wealth, and employment. Later a new benchmark bonus leads the team to put 15% to 20% of conservative accounts into high-beta stocks; Lopez posts "Just opened an account for Mr. Kochanski and his wife."

| Issue | Standard | Verdict | Lesson / fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| "CFA, Level I" plus expected year | VII(B) | Violation | Say "candidate"; no partial designation; never an expected completion date (L5720) |
| Trading early on analyst calls for discretionary accounts | III(B) Fair Dealing | Violation | Harms non-discretionary accounts and other teams' clients. Not II(A): clients pay for the research and there is no sign the public relies on it (L5732 to L5736) |
| Recommending a balanced portfolio with no client profile | III(C) | Violation (Hockett and Lopez) | Fix: build an IPS before recommending; age and wealth are not enough (L5750) |
| Excluding terminated accounts from the composite | III(D) Performance Presentation | Violation | Include terminated and current similar discretionary accounts; non-discretionary accounts may be left out (L5751 to L5755) |
| Simple average of four funds | III(D) | Acceptable | Fit to the audience's knowledge and sophistication (L5755) |
| Using a composite of similar accounts | III(D) | Acceptable | Weighted composite preferred over a single representative account (L5755) |
| High-beta "alpha" in conservative accounts | III(C) (mandate) | Violation | High-beta stocks have risk by nature; unchanged IPS is irrelevant; strategy changed for the bonus (L5765 to L5769) |
| Social media post about the new account | III(E) Confidentiality | Violation | Even the existence of an account is confidential, and the duty extends to the general population (L5771 to L5775) |

Not ruled on by the book (do not assume an answer): White's referral gift certificates, nondisclosure of Hockett's bonus scheme, and Hockett's proprietary-funds-only order (L5759 to L5765).

### Case 3: Castle Biotechnology (1), David Plume, PhD, CFA (curriculum L5778 to L5824)

**Facts in brief.** Castle Biotechnology (CB) controls nine biopharma companies and bought control of broker Global. Global took STRX and APBX public; CB keeps voting control. Plume, ex-CB biochemist with close ties to CB management, is now Global's analyst; his bonus is 0.10% of gross proceeds of each IPO he works on. After the IPOs he rates both "buy" and predicts prices will double (no revenues). His reports do not disclose CB's ownership of Global (only in the prospectus; generic "may be a director" text) or his CB preferred shares and options. He shorts APBX three months after his "buy," claiming he "was never a beneficial owner."

| Issue | Standard | Verdict | Lesson / fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not disclosing that CB owns Global | VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts | Violation | Disclosure must be prominent, plain language, effective; prospectus is "necessary but not sufficient" (L5807 to L5811) |
| Not disclosing CB shares and options | VI(A) | Violation | Beneficial ownership = direct or indirect pecuniary interest, voting power, or disposition power; owning the controlling parent counts (L5813) |
| IPO-proceeds bonus not disclosed | I(B) (cited); also "a conflict that must be disclosed" | Violation | Analyst pay must not be linked to IB deals; fix: remove the incentive (L5814 to L5818) |
| Shorting APBX while rated "buy" | VI(A) | Violation | Must be disclosed; fix: firm prohibits trading against own ratings (L5820 to L5824) |

### Case 4: Castle Biotechnology (2), Sandra Benning, CFA, and Claris Deacon (curriculum L5827 to L5897)

**Facts in brief.** Benning moved from Kodiak to Global. After resigning, she used her personal social media and email to invite Kodiak clients to follow. Global pays her a USD 250,000 signing bonus based on the share of clients who move and a year-end bonus based on clients' IPO participation; she discloses neither. For the oversubscribed FTSX IPO, Global's policy allocates to institutions that will buy more on day one; she follows it. She initials changed option-agreement language and signs a linking form for client Deacon after phone approval, and processes redemptions on the instructions of Deacon's husband.

| Issue | Standard | Verdict | Lesson / fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soliciting Kodiak clients via personal social media after resigning | IV(A) Loyalty | No violation | No noncompete; solicited only after leaving; used no Kodiak material; no confidential data (L5837 to L5841) |
| Signing bonus tied to client moves, undisclosed | VI(A) (book also says it could impair I(B)) | Violation | Not VI(C) (employer pay, not a referral fee); not IV(B) (no conflict with the employer). Fix: do not pay her this way (L5843) |
| IPO-participation bonus, undisclosed | VI(A) | Violation | Conflict is with clients, not the employer, so not IV(B) (L5847) |
| Allocating only to institutions that promise to buy more | II(B) Market Manipulation and III(B) Fair Dealing | Violation | This is a **tie-in agreement**; Global's allocation criteria themselves violate III(B); employer policy is no defense (L5874 to L5878) |

Not ruled on: her warning to individual clients not to flip while telling corporate clients they could sell (L5870).

### Case 5: Lionsgate Limited and Bank of Australia (1), Tony Hill (curriculum L5900 to L5945)

**Facts in brief.** Hill runs the Victory Capital Fund (VCF). Marketing states the fund is best performing over 10 years, 28.7% **gross of fees** over one year. ## 4.3 Level III item-set practice: 47 questions (curriculum L6131 to L6963)

### Format of the official practice sets

| Feature | What the curriculum practice shows |
|---|---|
| Sets and questions | 8 vignettes, 47 questions (seven sets of 6, one set of 5) |
| Vignette length | About 490 to 990 words, mostly 500 to 760 |
| Characters | 2 to 6 named people; CFA status is always stated (it decides who is bound) |
| Build | Chronological chunks; each question tied to one chunk; embedded tables, dialogues, policy drafts, numbered statements or procedures |
| Numbers | Light math planted (fee vs commissions; USD 25.00 vs 25.01; share counts and dates) |
| Decoys | Some facts have no question; some are tempting wrong reasons (politics, "distorted" price) |
| Options | Always three (A/B/C) |
| "No violation" answers | 13 of 47 (about 28%); 12 of those are option A |
| Answer key | A 18, B 16, C 13 |

Official exam facts (from WEB research, not the curriculum): each Level III item set has a vignette with **4** multiple-choice questions, 3 points each; 11 item sets and 11 essay sets; there is no penalty for wrong answers ([CFA Institute Level III page](https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa/exam/level-iii)). The official page does not confirm three answer choices per item-set question.

### Stem types to recognize

| Stem type | Count | How to attack it |
|---|---|---|
| "Did X violate any of the Standards?" A No / B Yes, because... / C Yes, because... | about 22 | Decide the verdict, then pick the correct reason; two "Yes" options often both name real standards |
| Two-item grid (No/No, No/Yes, Yes/No) | 6 | Judge each item separately |
| Compound "are both consistent?" | about 5 | One half is often the subtle flaw |

### Lessons from all 47 questions, grouped by standard

"[label inferred]" means the solution does not print the standard number.

**Who the Code applies to**
- Q8 (Nano): Code and Standards bind individuals but firms may adopt them; the Asset Manager Code is written for firms. Answer B (curriculum L6861).

**I(B) Independence and Objectivity / VI(A) Conflicts (issuer pay, gifts)**
- Q15 (Dean): issuer-paid research is allowed; violation is only presenting it as independent without prominent disclosure. Answer B (L6877).
- Q17 (seminar): legal is not the same as ethical; a weak research/banking barrier is a conflict source. Answer B (L6881).
- Q18 (seminar): disclose conflicts regardless of the member's own materiality view; disclosing all client gifts to the employer complies. Answer B (L6883).

**I(C) Misrepresentation and I(D) Misconduct**
- Q16 (Jollie): written permission does not remove the duty to cite Dean; citing only the original study is fine when using its results. Answer B (L6879).
- Q29 (Khadri): blaming a human typo on "computer programming" is knowing dishonesty, I(C) and I(D); III(D) is least likely because corrected data was given. Answer C (L6915).
- Q34 (Garcia): eavesdropping on the squawk box is I(D); not II(A) because possession alone is not a violation. Answer B (L6931).

**II(A) Material Nonpublic Information**

# Part 5: Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct

Source: 2026 curriculum, Learning Module 4 (curriculum L6965 to L7758). Labels: **REQUIRED** = the book says "must" (in the Code provision or its guidance). **RECOMMENDED** = "should," "should consider," "best practice," "ideally," "could," "we recommend."

## 5.1 Learning outcomes (curriculum L6971 to L6974)

1. Explain the purpose of the Asset Manager Code (AMC) and the benefits to a firm that adopts it.
2. Explain the responsibilities in the six General Principles of Conduct.
3. Determine whether a manager's practices are consistent with the AMC.
4. Recommend practices and procedures that prevent violations.

## 5.2 Who it applies to and what it is

- **Firms, not individuals.** The Code and Standards address individual conduct; the AMC applies, globally, to **firms that manage client assets** as separate accounts or pooled funds (including collective investment schemes, mutual funds, funds of funds). These firms are called **"Managers"** (curriculum L6985).
- It extends the Code and Standards "to the firm level," partly at managers' request (L6985).
- **Voluntary** code, meant as a template and guidepost for investors seeking ethical managers (L6980, L6984).
- Firms with an existing code (especially in well-regulated markets) should use the AMC to **evaluate their own code**. It is also aimed at managers with no code, including **hedge fund managers** (L6985). **Unregistered hedge fund managers** are encouraged to adopt it to promote self-regulation (L6987).
- **Second edition** added **risk management** provisions and guidance on **claiming compliance** (L6985).
- Adopted by **senior management, the board, and similar oversight bodies**; covers **all employees**, not just investment staff (L6986).
- Benefits: shows commitment to ethics and investor protection; **protects and enhances the firm's reputation** (L6986).
- Sets **minimum** ethical standards; general and flexible for firms of any size; applies whether or not a manager must register or comply with securities law (L6987).
- Adopting a code is **not enough** by itself; it must be backed by compliance procedures sized to the firm's business, clients, AUM, staff, and regulatory regime (L6988).
- Managers **must** follow all applicable laws; the AMC may need supplementing for local rules (L6989).
- Clients must monitor their assets, but they must be able to count on full and fair disclosure (L6994).

## 5.3 Adopting the Code and claiming compliance (curriculum L6996 to L7008)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| All or nothing (REQUIRED) | Must adhere to **all** principles and provisions; otherwise the firm **cannot** claim compliance (L6998 to L6999) |
| No partial claims (REQUIRED) | Prohibited: "complies with the Asset Manager Code **except for** ..." or "complies with parts A, B, and C" (L6999 to L7000) |
| Existing code | No need to amend it if it is **at least consistent** with the AMC (L6998) |
| Appendix guidance | Firms are **strongly encouraged** to consider it, but compliance **does not require strict adherence** to it (L6998) |
| Compliance statement (REQUIRED, whenever compliance is claimed) | See exact wording below (L7004) |
| Notify CFA Institute (REQUIRED) | Online notification at www.cfainstitute.org/assetcode (L7008) |
| No verification | Notification is for communication and information only; CFA Institute does **not** verify the claim or actual compliance and does no enforcement or quality control (L7008) |

**Exact compliance statement (curriculum L7004):**

> "[Insert name of Firm] claims compliance with the CFA Institute Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct. This claim has not been verified by CFA Institute."

## 5.4 The six General Principles of Conduct (verbatim, curriculum L7016 to L7028)

Managers must:
1. Act in a professional and ethical manner at all times.
2. Act for the benefit of clients.
3. Act with independence and objectivity.
4. Act with skill, competence, and diligence.
5. Communicate with clients in a timely and accurate manner.
6. Uphold the applicable rules governing capital markets.

## 5.5 The provisions and their guidance

The provision text itself is always REQUIRED ("Managers must"). The Appendix (Recommendations and Guidance) mixes required and recommended points; it is **not exhaustive** and adoption alone is insufficient: "Managers must adopt detailed policies and procedures to effectively implement the Code" (curriculum L7156).

Count of provisions: A = 3, B = 6 (B.5 and B.6 have two parts each), C = 5, D = 7, E = 2, F = 4 (F.4 has 12 items, a to l) (L7030 to L7144).

### A. Loyalty to Clients (curriculum L7032 to L7040; guidance L7160 to L7182)

| Provision (Managers must) | REQUIRED in guidance | RECOMMENDED in guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **A.1** Place client interests before their own | "Client interests are paramount" | Policies so client interests come first in all aspects (selection, transactions, monitoring, custody); avoid conflicts; operational safeguards; pay that aligns manager and client interests (L7164 to L7166) |
| **A.2** Preserve confidentiality of client information within the manager-client relationship | Keep information from **clients or other sources** strictly confidential; take all reasonable measures (L7168 to L7172) | Privacy policy (how data is collected, stored, protected, used); consider a written **anti-money-laundering** policy (L7172 to L7174) |
| | Exception: confidentiality does **not** override a duty (sometimes legal) to **report suspected illegal activity** to authorities (L7174) | |
| **A.3** Refuse any business relationship or gift that could reasonably be expected to affect independence, objectivity, or loyalty | Establish gift and entertainment policies; **refuse** gifts or entertainment of **more than minimal value** from service providers, potential investment targets, or other business partners (L7176 to L7178) | Firm defines "minimal value" and checks local rules; written policy; specific limits (amount per period per vendor); **prohibit cash gifts**; employees document and disclose **any** gift to supervisor, compliance, or senior management (L7178 to L7180) |
| | Multiple business relationships with a client are allowed if conflicts are managed and disclosed (L7182) | |

### B. Investment Process and Actions (curriculum L7042 to L7066; guidance L7184 to L7258)

| Provision (Managers must) | REQUIRED in guidance | RECOMMENDED in guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **B.1** Use reasonable care and prudent judgment | Care and prudence needed to meet client obligations. Prudence = care, skill, diligence of a person in a like capacity familiar with such matters; follow client parameters; balance risk and return; avoid harm (L7192 to L7196) | |
| **B.2** No practices designed to distort prices or inflate volume with intent to mislead | Manipulation = distorting prices or volume **with intent to deceive** (L7200 to L7204) | Examples: transaction-based (end-of-period trading in illiquid stocks to lift performance; building a large position to manipulate an asset or its derivative); information-based (spreading knowingly false rumors; **pressuring sell-side analysts**) (L7204 to L7206) |
| **B.3** Deal fairly and objectively with all clients | No preferential treatment of favored clients to others' detriment (L7208) | Allowed: higher service tiers and products for qualifying clients **if disclosed and made available to all clients**; side letters, sidecars, tag-alongs **if fairly allocated among similarly situated clients for whom suitable** (L7210 to L7212) |
| **B.4** Reasonable and adequate basis for decisions | Must analyze opportunities before acting (L7216) | Due diligence first; basis depends on style (passive vs active); third-party research OK after reasonable and diligent checks of **assumptions, thoroughness, timeliness, objectivity and independence**; understand structure, trading, liquidity, counterparty risk; for complex strategies explain vulnerabilities clearly and **stress test** (L7216 to L7226) |
| **B.5.a** Mandate, strategy, or style: take only actions consistent with stated objectives and constraints | Applies to pooled funds or mandates where client details are unknown; must stay within mandate (no style drift) (L7230 to L7232) | Flexibility for market events is fine if expressly agreed; best practice: disclose such events when they happen or at least in normal reporting (L7234) |
| **B.5.b** Disclose so investors can judge proposed changes in style or strategy | Give adequate information on proposed **material changes well in advance** (L7236) | Give enough time to act; let clients **redeem without undue penalties** after a material change (L7238) |
| **B.6.a** Separate accounts: before advising or acting, evaluate objectives, risk tolerance, horizon, liquidity, financial constraints, unique circumstances (tax, legal, regulatory) | Understand and evaluate the client's situation before acting (L7242) | "Ideally" an IPS for each client: risk tolerance (**ability and willingness**), return, horizon, liquidity, liabilities, tax, legal/regulatory/unique; review the IPS with the client **at least annually** and when circumstances change; IPS states manager's role and review schedule; agree on benchmark(s) and when measurement begins (L7246 to L7254) |
| **B.6.b** Determine that an investment is suitable to the client's financial situation | Evaluate actions in light of each client's circumstances; only appropriate investments (L7256) | "Ideally" judge in context of total assets and liabilities, including outside assets **if the information is provided and included in the IPS** (L7258) |

Note: the IPS is the basis for strategic asset allocation; the client may set the SAA or delegate it to the manager (L7254).

### C. Trading (curriculum L7068 to L7080; guidance L7260 to L7318)

| Provision (Managers must) | REQUIRED in guidance | RECOMMENDED in guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **C.1** Not act, or cause others to act, on MNPI that could affect a publicly traded investment | Adopt compliance procedures such as **information barriers (firewalls)**; keep **pending trades and client or fund holdings** confidential, since they may be MNPI (L7276) | Evaluate company information against the MNPI definition (L7278) |
| | Material = reasonable investor would consider it important and it significantly alters the **total mix**. Nonpublic = until **widely disseminated** (not to a select group). M&A information before disclosure is generally MNPI. **Mosaic theory** is permitted (L7274 to L7280) | |
| **C.2** Give priority to client investments over the manager's own | Do not trade own account before client trades in the same security; no front-running; co-investment only if clients are not disadvantaged (L7284 to L7286) | Monitor and limit personal trading; **prior approval for IPOs and private placements**; restricted list and/or watch list with preclearance; optional ("could"): quarterly trade confirmations and annual holdings statements to compliance (L7288 to L7292) |
| **C.3** Use client commissions only for investment-related products or services that directly assist investment decision making, not firm management | Commissions and benefits are **client property**; test: does it directly assist the investment decision-making process (financial analysis, trading and risk analysis, securities selection, broker selection, asset allocation, suitability analysis) (L7296 to L7300) | Some firms eliminated soft dollars; disclose the policy and any soft or bundled arrangements; consider following the **CFA Institute Soft Dollar Standards** (L7302 to L7304) |
| **C.4** Maximize portfolio value by seeking best execution | Seek best possible overall result given trade size, market, liquidity, security type; choose brokers by commission rates, timeliness, anonymity, fewer incomplete trades, low market impact (L7308) | Directed brokerage: warn the client it may not get best execution and get **written acknowledgment** (L7310) |
| **C.5** Policies for fair and equitable trade allocation | Accounts must not be **routinely traded first**; if a new issue is too small for all, no preferential treatment (L7314, L7318) | Block trades, **pro rata**, **average price**; give all clients for whom suitable a chance at IPOs and secondaries; policy should specifically address IPOs and private placements (L7314 to L7318) |

### D. Risk Management, Compliance, and Support (curriculum L7082 to L7098; guidance L7320 to L7413)

| Provision (Managers must) | REQUIRED in guidance | RECOMMENDED in guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **D.1** Policies and procedures to comply with the AMC and all laws | Detailed firmwide policies put the principles into operation (L7330) | Compliance programs, controls, self-assessment tools sized to the firm (L7332) |
| **D.2** Appoint a compliance officer who administers policies and **investigates complaints** | Officer must be competent, knowledgeable, credible, and empowered; may be an existing employee, a separate hire, or a whole department, depending on size (L7336) | Where possible **independent of investment and operations** staff and **reporting to the CEO or board**; staff acknowledge receipt of the Code, agree to comply, and report violations; training; self-evaluation; review firm and employee trades; act quickly on breaches and work on discipline (L7338 to L7348) |
| **D.3** Ensure portfolio information to clients is accurate and complete and arrange **independent third-party** confirmation or review | Accuracy duty (L7352) | Forms: audit or review (most pooled funds), or custodian account statements and trade confirmations (L7354) |
| **D.4** Maintain records for an appropriate period in an easily accessible format | Keep records that support investment activity, research scope, basis for conclusions, reasons for actions; absent regulation, set a minimum period (L7358 to L7360) | Also keep compliance records and records of violations and actions; hard copy or electronic both fine; **at least seven years** unless local law says otherwise (L7358 to L7360) |
| **D.5** Qualified staff and sufficient human and technological resources | Assets invested, administered, protected by **qualified, experienced** staff; deliver the services claimed; resources to monitor holdings (L7372 to L7375) | Adequate back-office and compliance support and internal controls against fraud (L7366 to L7370) |
| | **Outsourcing allowed, but the manager keeps liability and responsibility** (L7372) | |
| **D.6** Business-continuity plan for disaster recovery or market disruption | Plan scaled to size, nature, complexity (L7381) | **At a minimum, should consider:** (1) backup of account information, preferably off-site; (2) **alternative plans for monitoring, analyzing, and trading** if systems fail; (3) communication with critical vendors and suppliers; (4) employee communication and coverage of critical functions; (5) contacting clients during an extended disruption. Also backup office space and key-person loss; train staff; **test the plan firmwide** at intervals (L7381 to L7397) |
| **D.7** Firmwide risk management process that identifies, measures, and manages risk | Must complement (not compete with) investment management; must be **objective, independent, insulated from portfolio managers** (L7405 to L7409) | Risks include market, credit, liquidity, counterparty, concentration, operational; stress tests, scenario tests, backtests; continuously challenge models and be ready to describe them to clients; small firms should consider **outsourcing** risk management; risk work ultimately depends on judgment (L7407 to L7413) |

### E. Performance and Valuation (curriculum L7102 to L7108; guidance L7415 to L7439)

| Provision (Managers must) | REQUIRED in guidance | RECOMMENDED in guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **E.1** Present performance that is fair, accurate, relevant, timely, complete; do not misrepresent individual portfolio or firm performance | Fair representation including all relevant factors; **hypothetical or backtested results clearly identified**; forward-looking information also fair, accurate, complete (L7427) | Do not claim others' performance or cherry pick periods or investments; give extra portfolio transparency; **GIPS is "a model"**, not an AMC requirement (L7427 to L7429) |
| **E.2** Use fair-market prices; in good faith, fair-value securities with no ready independent quote | Good-faith methods where no third-party quote exists (L7102 to L7108) | Conflict when the PM sets valuations that drive fees: move valuation to an **independent third party**; pooled funds with independent board members: they approve policies and review valuations; without independent directors, use independent valuation experts; use widely accepted methods consistently (L7433 to L7439) |

## 5.8 AMC vs the Code and Standards

| Point | Code and Standards | Asset Manager Code |
|---|---|---|
| Who is bound | Individual members and candidates | Firms ("Managers"); firm may adopt even with no charterholders (L6985, L7555) |
| How it applies | Through membership and candidacy | Voluntary adoption by senior management and board; covers all employees (L6984, L6986) |
| Firm adoption | Firms are encouraged to adopt the Code and Standards as part of a firm code (Q8, curriculum L6861) | Written specifically for firms |
| Compliance claim | Not a firm claim | Prescribed statement, notification to CFA Institute, no verification or enforcement; no partial claims (L7004, L7008, L6999) |
| Guidance status | Standards text binding; recommended procedures optional | Provisions binding; Appendix guidance not strictly required (L6998) |
| Firm-level operations spelled out | Not as provisions | Compliance officer (D.2), third-party confirmation (D.3), BCP (D.6), firmwide risk management (D.7), 12 disclosure items (F.4) |

# Part 6: Master list of traps and distinctions

Merged and de-duplicated from the trap sections of all curriculum notes (Code and Standards, Guidance I to VII, Level III cases, item sets, Asset Manager Code) plus the YouTube and web research. "L" numbers are curriculum lines. Points marked **[YouTube]** or **[Web]** come from prep providers or video presenters; they are opinions unless the curriculum line is also given.

Reference key: "Qn" = Level III item-set question from Part 4.3 (Asset Manager Code questions carry L77xx tags); "LM2 Qn" = Learning Module 2 practice problem; "Ex n" = the Standard's own application example in the guidance.

## A. Required vs recommended

1. "Must" in the Standard or guidance is required; "Recommended procedures" are optional. A stem asking what is **not** required usually points to a recommended procedure (all Standards). [YouTube] repeated by several presenters: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9_As0t5P4fU , https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qd_2KJKO-8c
2. Members are **encouraged** (not required) to tell their employer about their Code duties and **strongly urged** to discuss the Code with supervisors and compliance (Preamble; L1074, L915 to L919).
3. GIPS is recommended, not required, by III(D); but any GIPS claim must meet all requirements, and a partial claim by a firm or a fund violates III(D) (L3272, L3302, L6905).
4. Seven-year record retention is a CFA Institute recommendation that applies only when no regulatory or firm rule exists; meeting local or firm rules satisfies V(C) (L4491). The AMC says the same with "should" (D.4; L7360). [Web] could not confirm the figure; the curriculum confirms it.
5. V(B): cost disclosure is required, but not specific dollar amounts and not the firm's own costs; written disclosure at the outset is best practice only (L4250 to L4255).
6. VI(A): avoiding the conflict is best practice; full and fair disclosure is the minimum (L4545 to L4553).
7. VI(B) procedures (blackouts, preclearance, IPO bans, private-placement limits, duplicate confirms, holdings reports) are recommendations; the Standards do not set a blackout length (L4688 to L4720; Q47, L6963).
8. IV(C) minimum is reasonable efforts to prevent and detect violations; training and ethical incentives are "should" items (L3751).
9. I(E) Competence does not require any specific continuing education program (L2228).
10. Even a sole proprietor with only discretionary accounts should have written fair-dealing procedures (III(B); L2953).

## B. Disclosure vs prohibition (when disclosure cures and when it does not)

11. Disclosure cures: issuer-paid research (I(B), VI(A); L1745, Q15 L6877); premium service tiers (III(B); L2923); referral fees (VI(C) is a disclosure rule, not a ban; L1226); a liquidity-boosting arrangement that is fully disclosed (II(B) ACME example).
12. Disclosure does **not** cure: a patently unfair allocation even with client consent (III(B); L2945); lack of independent judgment (I(B); SIC L5665); soliciting your current employer's clients (IV(A); LM2 Q26); manipulation that benefits clients (II(B)).
13. Where local law **prohibits** something the Code only requires to be disclosed (e.g., IPO participation), the prohibition wins (I(A); L1621 to L1623).
14. Prospectus disclosure is "necessary but not sufficient"; VI(A) disclosure must be prominent, plain language, and effective (VI(A); Plume L5811, Graeme L5985; CFA fees in a prospectus, LM2 Q17).
15. Disclose conflicts to **all** clients, not only to those who ask (VI(A); Graeme L5981).
16. Disclose every conflict regardless of your own view of its materiality, and not only conflicts tied to current holdings (VI(A); Q11 L6867, Q18 L6883).
17. Generic boilerplate ("employees may be directors of the issuer") is not adequate; disclosures must be specific (VI(A), I(B); L5802, L1710).
18. On "best action" stems, prefer **avoidance** over disclosure: pay your own way, put the stock on a restricted list, do not write the report (I(B), VI(A); LM2 Q2, Q3, Q32).
19. If the firm will not allow a negative opinion, put the company on a **restricted list** and publish only factual information (I(B); L1763). The restricted list is also a right answer for MNPI (LM2 Q10).
20. Firms can remove the conflict: drop deal-linked analyst bonuses, ban trading against one's own rating, refuse issuer commissions (VI(A), I(B); L5818, L5820, L5981).
21. No disclosure needed: media gift bags with no conflict (IV(B), I(B); Hill L5925); internal fee sharing that is not a referral fee (VI(C); Sim L6030); board-seat pay to clients (Q41 L6951); using a company's products or a remote in-law supplier link (VI(A); LM2 Q31); risks that were unknown (V(B); L4281).
22. Prohibiting all personal ownership of covered stocks is "simplest" but may be overly burdensome; not required (VI(A); L4584).

## C. Who is the client, and to whom is the duty owed

23. Pension or trust: the client is the **beneficiaries**, not the trustees, sponsor, or company management (III(A); L2753; Q1 L6847; LM2 Q13).
24. Fund run to a mandate or index: the duty is to the stated mandate, not to judge suitability for each fund investor (III(A), III(C)(2); L2754, L1152).
25. Execution-only relationship: duty is best execution within client instructions, not fiduciary advice (III(A); L2741).
26. III(A) does not by itself make members fiduciaries; it is a minimum, and any legal fiduciary duty must also be met (III(A); L2737 to L2739).
27. Non-investment roles: the "client" may be public shareholders or the investing public, so independence outranks loyalty to one organization (III(A); L2756).
28. Priority order: clients, then employer, then self (III(A) L1136; VI(B) L1222). Code component 2 puts the integrity of the profession and clients above personal interest (L1084). [YouTube] rankings such as "markets before clients" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ar8-0Oogeuo) or "profession first" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo) are not curriculum wording.
29. Fee-paying family accounts are ordinary clients: excluding or disadvantaging them violates (III(A) Ex 7; III(B) L2940; VI(B) Toffler L4740).
30. A non-fee-paying account in which the member has a beneficial interest is **excluded** from an oversubscribed IPO (VI(B); Q46 L6961).
31. III(E) protects current, **former**, and prospective clients (L1162, L3377). [Web] adds "deceased clients"; that wording is not in the curriculum file.
32. III(C)(1) applies only in an **advisory** relationship (L1144, L3112). V(B) applies mainly to client-facing members (L4260).
33. Unpaid interns are employees (IV(A) Ex 5; L3609). Employee vs contractor turns on degree of **control** (IV(A); L3540 to L3544).
34. IV(C) covers anyone under the supervisor's control or authority, member or not (L3747).

## D. Consent: written vs verbal, and whose consent

35. IV(B): **written** consent from **all parties involved**, obtained **before** accepting; e-mail counts (L1180, L3694). Verbal consent fails (Q36 L6935); a "discussion" with a supervisor fails (Q12 L6871).
36. IV(B) recommended written report goes to supervisor and compliance and gives nature, approximate amount, and duration (L3703). Client consent is not needed for an outside board seat (Q36).
37. Independent practice: notify the employer **and** get consent to all terms before rendering services (IV(A); L3490 to L3494).
38. Taking employer records needs the employer's **written** permission (IV(A); L3510); even copies need express consent (V(C); L4487).
39. Never sign or initial for a client, even after phone approval (I(D); Deacon L5897). Third-party instructions need a cosigner or written power of attorney (III(A); L5897).
40. Directed brokerage: warn of possible loss of best execution and get **written** acknowledgment (AMC C.4 L7310; III(A) L2772). A new client may waive best execution only in writing (III(A) Ex 3).
41. Confidential information: get permission **first**; notice afterward does not work (III(E); LM2 Q23). Specific client permission is a valid exception (Sim L6090).
42. Agency cross-transactions need disclosure and advance client consent (III(B); L3038).
43. Answering an RFP before your resignation is effective needs permission from both employer and the RFP issuer (IV(A) Ex 8; L3632).
44. Client gift for **past** service: disclose to the employer, before accepting if possible, otherwise after (I(B); L1679). Client bonus **contingent on future** results: IV(B), employer's written consent (Whitman, L3711). [YouTube] one presenter recalled a future-performance gift needing only disclosure (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q); this conflicts with the curriculum.
45. Donating referral fees to charity is fine with approval of the employer and the charity and disclosure to the client (VI(C); L4806).

## E. Timing: before vs after

46. Soliciting clients (existing **and** prospective) **before** leaving is prohibited; **after** leaving it is allowed absent a noncompete (IV(A); L3510; Q7 L6859; Benning L5837; Hill L5945).
47. Preparing to compete on your own time is allowed (registering a company, leasing space, inviting colleagues) (IV(A); L3494; Hill L5938 to L5943).
48. Honor an employer's confidentiality request: telling the team **after** the board asked for secrecy violated; telling them **before** would have been fine (IV(A); Hill L5932 to L5936).
49. VI(C) disclosure must come **before** any formal agreement, ideally in writing at the time of referral (L4767, L4792). [YouTube] disclosure sent only after clients signed "should have been told first" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc).
50. III(C): gather client information at the start and build the IPS **before** recommending (L3116; Kochanskis L5750).
51. Information is public once disseminated; you need not wait for the slowest channel (II(A); L2368; Q13 L6873). Waiting a day after a private tip does not make it public (LM2 Q10).
52. VI(B): let clients have an adequate chance to receive and act; trading seven minutes after a broadcast but before written dissemination violated (L4679, L4754). Wait until the client or fund trade is done (Corrales L5682).
53. III(B): disseminate to everyone first, then follow up with select clients (Ex 6); a client ordering against a changed recommendation should be told of the change before the order is accepted (L2936).
54. Allocate trades before execution or systematically right after; allocating after seeing price moves violates (III(B) Ex 4; L3038). Review suitability before allocating, not at month end (Q5 L6855).
55. IV(C): procedures must exist **before** a violation; on learning of one, start an assessment **promptly** (L3775, L3777).
56. Keeping an external manager during an investigation is fine; after the evaluation shows breaches, fire it (III(A); Q4 L6853, Q6 L6857).
57. V(B): disclose material changes to the process **promptly**, including who actually makes decisions (L1198; Hill and Martin L5931). AMC: disclose style changes **well in advance** (B.5.b; L7236; Q1 L7725).
58. V(A): diligence continues until delivery, so known material changes must be reflected (Ex 4 L4105); research done after the trade does not cure it (Q45 L6959).
59. An unknowing error is not an I(C) violation, but once found it must be corrected (I(C); L2034).

## F. Materiality and public vs nonpublic

60. Material has two alternative tests: probable price effect **or** reasonable investors would want to know (II(A); L2328).
61. Reliability of the source decides materiality: insider fact vs speculation (II(A); L2362). Ambiguous price effect or the passage of time lowers it (L2364).
62. Material information need not come from the issuer: large pending orders and third-party rating changes qualify (II(A); L2358 to L2360). AMC: pending trades and client or fund holdings may be MNPI (C.1; L7276).
63. Not public: a room of analysts, the 10 largest shareholders, a members-only social media group, or company "guidance" on filings given selectively (II(A); L2372, L2392, L2506).
64. Firm research that clients pay for is not MNPI unless the general public relies on it; trading early on it for some clients is III(B), not II(A) (Hockett L5732 to L5736; L2403 to L2407). Advance notice to a **non-client** (a journalist) is MNPI (Ex 7).
65. Rumors plus a publicly broadcast report of insider sales are public information (II(A); Portman L5968). Political conjecture is not MNPI (LM2 Q29).
66. Mosaic theory: material public plus **nonmaterial** nonpublic is fine; adding one material nonpublic piece breaks it (II(A); L2380; AMC C.1 L7280). Documenting the mosaic is recommended evidence, not a condition (L2385).
67. Possessing MNPI is not a violation; acting is (Q34 vs Q35, L6931 to L6933). Communicating it is a violation even if the recipient never trades (Ex 7, L2536). Letting colleagues overhear is a failure to control it (Ex 2, L2500).
68. Trading a related company on the same nonpublic news is still acting on MNPI (II(A); Q35). [YouTube] a commenter argued intent, not correlation, decides (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q); the curriculum answer stands.
69. MNPI traded through a fund is still MNPI trading; sharing it casually also violates (II(A); LM2 Q11). II(A) covers all instruments, including derivatives and funds (L2322).
70. Due-diligence use of insider information under a business agreement is allowed; trading or tipping on it is not (II(A); L2376).
71. MNPI received selectively from a broker: keep it confidential and do not use it; publishing it is not your job (II(A); Q44 L6957). If public dissemination cannot be achieved, tell only compliance and do not trade or change recommendations (L2413).
72. Expert networks may be paid, but the member makes his own materiality judgment (II(A); L2398 to L2399).
73. Market makers should stay passive, not stop trading, when the firm holds MNPI; risk arbitrage is best suspended (II(A); L2474 to L2479). Restricted lists can tip the market; watch lists are seen only by compliance (L2466).
74. Two "material" definitions: MNPI (would change the total mix about a security; L7274) vs AMC disclosure (what investors want to know to choose or keep the manager; L7469).

## G. Intent, knowledge, and defenses that fail

75. "Knowingly" is in I(A) (participate or assist) and I(C); it is not in I(D) or II(A) (L1102, L1110). In I(C), "knowingly" includes "should have known" (L1929).
76. I(A): members are presumed to know the law ("know or should know") but need not be compliance experts (L1425, L1455).
77. II(B) needs **intent to mislead**; a legitimate trade that moves a thin market is not manipulation (L2611; Q14 L6875). Transaction-based manipulation uses "knew or should have known" the actions could affect pricing (L2605; Benning L5874).
78. II(B): no personal profit is needed, and benefit to clients is no excuse (Ex 3, Ex 4, Ex 9).
79. Following an employer policy is no defense (Benning L5878; Graeme L5981); employer policies bind only if consistent with law and the Code (IV(A); L3476).
80. A lawyer's opinion that the regulator could not prove wrongdoing is not a safe harbor (I(A); L1593).
81. Good intentions or "client service" do not excuse a confidentiality breach or signing for a client (III(E) Ex 2; I(D) Deacon L5897).
82. A client's instruction is not a full defense: balance it with the IPS and explain costs and risks (III(A), III(C); Madam Tan L6126). You cannot simply refuse a client's direct order, but must discuss the risks (III(C) Ex 8).
83. Bad outcomes do not prove a violation: no I(E) breach from a bad result (L2224), no V(A) breach from a loss after proper diligence (L4172), and suitability does not prevent losses (L3106).
84. A dishonest excuse for an error (blaming a computer for a human typo) violates I(C) and I(D) even when correct data is supplied (Q29 L6915).
85. "It is not illegal here" and "I am a citizen of a looser country" are no defense; follow the stricter rule (I(A); L1431 to L1433, L1621). [YouTube] "Legal is the floor, not the ceiling" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LalyW-mSiU4).

## H. Supervision and compliance

86. On learning of a possible violation, the supervisor must promptly start an investigation; relying on assurances, warning the employee, or reporting up the chain is **not** enough (IV(C); L3777; Balmer L5999; Q12 L6871).
87. Not reporting to compliance is not itself the violation if an investigation was started (IV(C); Balmer L6011 to L6015).
88. Sending a subordinate to people with no firsthand knowledge is not a reasonable effort (IV(C); Yousoff L6084 to L6088).
89. Writing good procedures is not enough; they must be enforced (IV(C); Q23 L6899). Once warned of problems, verify the fix (Q31 L6919).
90. Delegation is allowed, but delegates must be told how to prevent and detect violations (IV(C); L3749; Q23).
91. If the compliance system is inadequate, decline supervisory responsibility **in writing** until it is fixed; not resign or go to the board (IV(C); L3769; Q9 L6863).
92. Not detecting a violation despite reasonable procedures may not be a violation; knowing that procedures are not followed is (IV(C); L3781). Ignorance because no procedures exist is a violation (Ex 4, L3889).
93. A "not reviewed" disclaimer, time pressure, or a good track record do not excuse skipping review (IV(C) Ex 2, Ex 9). Review applies at all levels, including the CIO (L3913).
94. Incentives should reward **how** results are achieved, not how much is generated (IV(C); L3855 to L3857). A supervisor's own pay incentive heightens the duty (Ex 3, L3881).
95. Keep the code of ethics (principles, given to clients) separate from detailed compliance procedures (IV(C); L3790 to L3793).
96. Letting an employee skip personal-trading forms is a IV(C) violation (L4748).
97. Whistleblower Rasmussen did not violate IV(A); the supervisor and compliance officer who dismissed her violated IV(C) (L3652, L3895). Whistleblowing justifies disloyal acts only if aimed at protecting clients or markets, not personal gain (L3536).

## I. Which standard? Classic confusions

98. Conflict standards: **VI(A)** = conflicts with clients (bonus tied to client moves or IPO uptake, issuer links, beneficial ownership, shorting your own "buy," issuer commissions); **IV(B)** = pay that competes with the employer's interest; **VI(C)** = pay from or to others for referrals; **I(B)** = threats to objectivity (Benning L5843, L5847; Sim L6071; Yousoff L6036; Plume L5818).
99. Employer-paid bonuses are not IV(B) and are not referral fees; they are VI(A) issues when they create client conflicts (Benning L5843 to L5847).
100. Gifts that may sway judgment: I(B). Outside pay that competes with the employer: IV(B) (L1376).
101. Acting early on in-house research for favored accounts: III(B), not II(A) (Hockett L5732).
102. A politically driven but suitable investment: V(A), not III(C) (SIC L5660). Promising return maximization under a dual mandate: III(C) (L5680).
103. Hiding bribes as operating expenses: I(C) (plus likely I(A)); hiring legitimate subadvisers is fine under V(A) and I(B) (L5688).
104. Misportraying who manages the fund: I(C) for the salesman (Portman L5961); failure to disclose who actually decides: V(B) for the managers (L5931).
105. Rating changed on an overheard opinion: V(A); a contingent dividend stated as certain: V(B) (LM2 Q28, Q29). Fact vs opinion is in V(B), not V(A) (L1208).
106. Model builder with poor model: V(A) (Dupont); client-facing PM who does not disclose it: V(B) (L4404 to L4406, L4216).
107. Same Papis and Nagle facts: V(B) for hiding the change from plan recipients, VI(A) for not telling the employer about the relationship (L4392, L4640).
108. I(A) = compliance with law; I(D) = any conduct reflecting on integrity, legal or not (L2151). Personal-life dishonesty can violate I(D); civil disobedience and bankruptcy alone generally do not; intoxication at work does (L2152 to L2194).
109. Avoiding products you cannot understand is compliant under I(C); investing in them is an I(E) violation (L2113, L2292).
110. Eavesdropping on a squawk box: I(D); trading on what was heard: II(A) (Q34, Q35).
111. Speakerphone exposing block trades: III(A) (clients) **and** IV(A) (employer) (Q33 L6925).
112. V(A) does not apply to personal trades (Q35 L6933). Suitability is the least likely violation for a personal purchase (Q40 L6949).
113. Chuong hiding her son's hiring: VI(A), not I(C) (L6038). CIO selling before the resignation news: II(A), not IV(A) (L5967).
114. Redemption executed without explaining costs: III(A) primarily, III(C) arguably (Madam Tan L6122 to L6126). Illiquid lock-up in a liquidity IPS: III(A) and III(C) (III(C) Ex 5).
115. Suitability and Performance Presentation sit in Standard III, not V (L1382 to L1384). "Loyalty" appears in III(A) and IV(A), never in VII (L1388).
116. Plagiarism is I(C) Misrepresentation (L1971 to L1980).
117. [YouTube] a personal blog post ahead of the employer's downgrade was ruled loyalty, not MNPI, in a recalled question (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q). Curriculum: a personal blog revealing firm views discloses confidential information and uses firm resources (IV(A) Ex 15, L3686 to L3688).

## J. Gifts, compensation, and incentives

118. Client gifts are acceptable with disclosure to the employer; gifts from others seeking influence must be token items (I(B); L1677 to L1679, L1767). Disclosure is to the **employer**, not the client.
119. Customary business entertainment is fine unless meant to influence or reward; firm limits may be per gift or annual total (I(B); L1767).
120. I(B) covers **offering** as well as accepting; lavish entertainment of a consultant violates, and internal disclosure and expensing do not cure it (L1106, L1734, L1879).
121. Travel: best practice is commercial transport at own or firm expense; modest issuer arrangements are acceptable when commercial travel is unavailable; no issuer should pay airfare (I(B); L1751 to L1765). A conference host paying a speaker's own costs may be fine; extras are inducements (LM2 Q2 vs Q4).
122. Promise coverage, never a favorable rating (I(B); L1795). Softening wording to cope with pressure is not acceptable (L1683 to L1685).
123. Issuer-paid research: best practice is a flat fee agreed before writing; pay tied to the rating or to investor purchases, or in warrants, impairs independence (I(B); L1745 to L1747).
124. Analyst pay must not be linked directly to investment banking deals; IB has no say over research (I(B); L1710, L5818).
125. IV(B) covers nonmonetary, indirect, and contingent benefits (Ex 1 L3711, Ex 2 L3719); its purpose includes letting the employer see the true cost of your services (L3698).
126. Undisclosed benefits violate; prior approval of a meeting plus disclosure of an unplanned dinner did not (IV(B); Hollis Ex 3, L3727).
127. Incentive pay is allowed, but a change that conflicts with client objectives must be disclosed (VI(A); L4626); a strategy shifted to earn a bonus violates III(C) (Hockett L5769).
128. Third-party pay for selling a stock: disclose to clients at minimum; best practice decline (VI(A); L4632).
129. VI(C) applies to fees **received and paid**, including internal interdepartmental payments, and must also be disclosed to the employer (L4766, L4792, L4798).
130. VI(C) disclosure must give the **nature** (flat or percentage, one-time or ongoing) and **estimated dollar value**; "I get a referral fee" fails (Q22 L6897; L4767). Consideration includes soft dollars and in-kind benefits (L4786).
131. Undisclosed consultant kickbacks violate even when the resulting selection was objective (VI(C); L4811 to L4813).
132. Soliciting corporate sponsorship for CFA society events can threaten independence (I(B); L1687 to L1691).

## K. Fair dealing, allocations, and trade errors

133. III(B) means **fairly, not equally**; "approximately the same time" is the target (L2923, L2967). [YouTube] "Netflix tiers" analogy (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo).
134. Different service levels are allowed if disclosed, not harmful, and available to all (III(B); L2923; Q30 L6917; AMC B.3 L7210).
135. Not every client must get every recommendation; selection must rest on suitability and interest, never preferred status (III(B); L2954).
136. Oversubscribed issues: prorate on a round-lot basis; member and family forgo purchases, except family accounts run like client accounts (III(B); L2940). Departing from strict pro rata to meet minimum lot sizes is fine (Ex 7, L2993).
137. Allocate new issues by **client**, not by portfolio manager; get indications of interest first (III(B); L2995).
138. Block trades: same price and same commission for every participating account (III(B); L2991).
139. Giving discretionary accounts priority over non-discretionary ones is unfair (III(B) Ex 2; Hockett L5732).
140. Posting a recommendation on social media before clients receive it is selective disclosure (III(B) Ex 9).
141. Tie-in agreements (IPO shares only for buyers who promise to buy more) violate II(B) and III(B) (Benning L5874).
142. Buying in one account while selling in another is not unfair per se if documented (III(B); L3003). Employee trading limits stay until the recommendation reaches all clients (L2967).
143. Shifting soft-dollar costs by churning other clients violates III(B); do not add trades just to equalize commissions (Ex 8).
144. Trade errors: the client never bears the loss; pay interest to wrongly allocated accounts and never debit those that should have got shares (III(B); Q3 L6851, Q5 L6855). [YouTube] a broker absorbing its own error at no client cost is not a violation (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OsF5hFos0FU).
145. Breaking a disclosed promise (no markup on retail trades) violates I(C) and III(B) (Q32 L6921 to L6923).

## L. Suitability and the IPS

146. Managing to a mandate: no action outside the IPS "under no circumstances," even if profitable or later disclosed (III(C); Q2 L6849; L1152).
147. Judge suitability in the context of the **total portfolio**; a risky security can be suitable within it (III(C); L1150, L3136).
148. Suitability is judged on the information the client actually provided (III(C); L3131).
149. IPS review **at least annually** and **before material changes**; a five-year-old IPS violates even if updated after a complaint (III(C); L3126; Milgram L6010).
150. Risk tolerance drives suitability: low tolerance, not long horizon, made short-held new issues unsuitable (III(C); Delaneys L5995). Age alone does not set risk tolerance (Ex 1).
151. Unsolicited unsuitable trade: discuss first; minor impact: educate and get acknowledgment; material impact: update the IPS; if the client refuses, a separate unmanaged account or ending the relationship (III(C); L3141 to L3147).
152. An investment within an asset-class limit can still be unsuitable if illiquid (III(C), III(A); Ex 5).
153. Picking a subadviser on fees alone may violate III(C); due diligence is V(A) (Ex 6).
154. A wealth increase raises ability to bear risk; update the IPS (III(C) Ex 3).
155. Forcing every client into one of two model portfolios ignores individual needs (III(C); Q11 L6867).
156. Fee-based account suitability depends on computing fee vs commissions saved (III(C), V(A); Q19, Q20 L6885 to L6891); later life changes trigger review (Q23).
157. A plan to buy "risk-free" bills does not replace an IPS (III(C); Sim L6072 to L6076).

## M. Performance presentation and GIPS

158. Include terminated accounts; non-discretionary accounts may be left out of the composite (III(D); L3280 to L3282; Lopez L5755; Q10 L6865).
159. Use a weighted composite of similar portfolios, not a single representative account; no cherry-picking or rotating membership (III(D); L3280, Ex 5).
160. A simple average can be acceptable for an unsophisticated audience (III(D); Lopez L5755).
161. State gross of fees, net of fees, or after tax; "available upon request" does not cure a missing fee basis (III(D); L3286; Q28 L6913). Gross-of-fees results are fine when disclosed (Hill L5924).
162. Simulated or backtested results are fine when clearly labeled and not guaranteed (III(D), V(B); L3286; Q24 L6901; AMC E.1 L7427).
163. Brief presentations need supporting detail available on request (required); noting the limited nature is best practice (III(D); L3265 to L3266).
164. Prior-employer performance can be shown with disclosure of where it was earned and the person's role (III(D) Ex 3, L3312). Team-managed results must be disclosed as such (LM2 Q22).
165. Methodology changes need disclosure; show both methods and the reasons (III(D) Ex 6).
166. Guarantee language ("steady 25%") also violates I(C) (III(D) Ex 1). Guaranteeing a volatile return is always misrepresentation, but structurally guaranteed products may be described as such (I(C); L1933, L2056 to L2062).
167. Switching pricing sources only because they report higher values is a misrepresentation risk (I(C); L1951 to L1953).

## N. Confidentiality and record retention

168. III(E) exceptions: illegal client activity, disclosure required by law, client permission (L1164 to L1168).
169. Even the **existence** of an account is confidential, including to friends outside the industry (III(E); Lopez L5771 to L5775).
170. Law can cut both ways: if law requires confidentiality even about illegal acts, do not disclose; illegal activity creates a possible duty to report, so consult compliance or counsel first (III(E); L3371, L3381).
171. Scope: information received because of your special ability to handle part of the client's affairs and relating to that part (III(E); L3371).
172. Cooperating with the CFA Institute Professional Conduct Program is not a breach (III(E); L3389). [YouTube] this is a common trap answer (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc).
173. A client's own accidental post on a properly warned platform is not the member's violation (III(E) Ex 5). Tipping a charity about a client's planned gift violates (Ex 2).
174. Keeping records does not breach confidentiality; destroying meeting notes at the client's request violates V(C) (Sim L6065 to L6069).
175. Records may be hard copy or electronic (V(C); Q25 L6903; AMC D.4 L7358).
176. Records belong to the firm; re-creating them after leaving must use public sources or the company, not memory or old files (V(C); L4487, L4517 to L4519).
177. Keep records of reviews that do not change a position, third-party research used, and new media (e-mail, texts, posts) (V(C); L4449, L4477 to L4483, L4511).
178. AMC confidentiality does not override a duty to report suspected illegal activity; consider an AML policy (A.2; L7174).

## O. Loyalty to the employer and leaving

179. Taking any employer record is a violation, even one you created yourself (IV(A) Ex 2; L3588). Convenience copies must be returned or erased (L3512).
180. After leaving, your skills, experience, and knowledge of former clients' names are not confidential; contacting them from public sources is fine absent a noncompete (IV(A); L3512 to L3514). The test is the **source** of the contact information (Ex 10, Ex 12).
181. "Practice" means services the employer currently offers for pay (IV(A); L3494).
182. Recruiting protocols protect only if every procedure is followed (IV(A); L3522 to L3524).
183. Firm-approved professional social media accounts are firm assets; best practice is separate personal and professional accounts (IV(A); L3532). Updating LinkedIn after the employer notified clients is fine (Ex 14).
184. No written contract is needed for loyalty duties (IV(A) Ex 4). An employee buyout with employer permission is consistent (Ex 3).
185. No breach on resigning when nothing conflicting happened before it took effect; no need to resign an independent board seat (IV(A); Q39 L6943).
186. Personal and family obligations are not overridden by employer loyalty; dialogue with the employer is a "should" (IV(A); L3476 to L3478).

## P. Research, reasonable basis, and communication

187. Third-party research may be used after checking assumptions, rigor, timeliness, and objectivity; do not rely on a source you have reason to doubt (V(A); L3996 to L4008; AMC B.4 L7220).
188. Internet sources need **greater** review; a blog can give ideas but not a reasonable basis by itself (V(A); L3998, Ex 13).
189. Model users must understand assumptions and limits; model developers need a higher level of diligence (V(A); L4022, L4030). Test strategies as a whole and beyond historical data; a best case shown as the base case violates (L4024 to L4030; Ex 2, Ex 11).
190. External managers must be reviewed as carefully as securities: fees, performance plus a GIPS claim, past relationship, or RFP answers alone are insufficient (V(A) Ex 7, Ex 9, Ex 15, Ex 16).
191. Group research: no need to dissociate if the process is sound; you may ask for your name to be removed (V(A); L4054).
192. Buying on third-party "strong buy" signals or speed alone fails V(A) (Q45 L6959).
193. Forecasts stated with "will" present opinion as fact; "should" signals opinion; estimates and price targets are opinions (V(B); Q27 L6907 to L6911; L4292).
194. Describe the basic process and logic, not the full system; omitting topics is fine if the report states its scope (V(B); L4330, L4287).
195. Disclose all costs, including affiliates' and third parties'; changing fee methods without notice is improper even if fees fall (V(B); L4254, L4324).
196. Disclose capacity limits even far below the threshold; leverage is generally a significant risk (V(B); L4278, L4415 to L4417).
197. Changes needing notice to **existing** clients include new asset types, derivatives, relaxed constraints, new models, passive shifts, committee decision making, external managers, and material model errors (V(B); L4352 to L4404).
198. Firm-generated disclosures may be relied on but do not relieve the client-facing member (V(B); L4259). Client sophistication never excuses nondisclosure (L4254).
199. Plagiarism includes internal memos, oral remarks, forecasts stripped of caveats, unnamed "leading analysts," and copied charts or algorithms; recognized statistical services need no attribution (I(C); L1971 to L1980, L2016).
200. Cite the original author; the intermediary need not be cited if merely a conduit and you checked the original (I(C); L2090). Written permission does not remove the duty to attribute (Q16 L6879).
201. Research done for the employer belongs to it; the analyst cannot reissue it under his own name after leaving (I(C); L1986).
202. Misstatements in outside materials you use become your responsibility; disclose use of external managers (I(C); L1941, L1943, L2004).
203. Model outputs are not facts; no benchmark is required, but a non-opportunity-set index must be explained (I(C); L1947, L1966).
204. Credit rating users may need to validate ratings independently (I(B); L1729).

## Q. Market manipulation

205. Two types: information-based (false rumors, pump and dump, pressuring analysts) and transaction-based (artificial price or volume, a dominant position to move a related derivative) (II(B); L2593, L2601 to L2609; AMC B.2 L7204 to L7206).
206. Manipulating model inputs to change a rating counts (II(B), Mandeville). Splitting holdings across accounts and wash-like trading plus rumors is both types (Gray).
207. A press release announcing a strategic review or reaffirming guidance is not manipulation; unusual personal volume to move the price is (II(B); LM2 Q12).
208. End-of-period trading in illiquid stocks to lift reported performance is manipulation (AMC B.2; L7204).

## R. Priority of transactions, soft dollars, best execution, proxies

209. Front-running ahead of the fund violates VI(B) even with no harm to the fund; co-investing alone is not a conflict but should be disclosed (Corrales L5682 to L5686; L4671).
210. Permissible personal trading: clients not disadvantaged, no personal benefit from client trades, regulatory compliance; contrary trades for liquidity needs are fine (VI(B); L4666). Selling against the firm's view is fine if clients are not disadvantaged and the firm is told (LM2 Q34).
211. Nonpublic information may not go to anyone whose securities you beneficially own, whatever its materiality; to others only if not material (VI(B); L4675).
212. A blackout in a large firm can act as a total ban; in a small firm it more likely prevents front-running (VI(B); L4704).
213. Beneficial ownership = direct or indirect pecuniary interest, voting power, or disposition power; owning the controlling parent counts, and a spouse's shares count (VI(A); L4573, L4612; Plume L5813).
214. Firm conflicts to disclose: underwriting, market making, M&A advisory, board seats, ownership stakes (VI(A); L4569 to L4606). A conflict exists even if the recommendation would be the same (L4618).
215. Soft dollars violate only if higher commissions buy no client benefit; paying firm overhead (rent) or vacations violates (III(A); L2771). Research that also helps other accounts is fine with best execution (LM2 Q14). CFA Program fees are education, not research (LM2 Q17).
216. Directed brokerage is the client's asset; still seek best price and disclose possible loss of best execution (III(A); L2772). Research for order flow is fine with best execution and no minimum volume (Q43 L6955).
217. Proxies have economic value; blindly voting with management on non-routine issues may violate, but cost-benefit may justify not voting all; a vote matching management is fine if researched (III(A); L2777 to L2778; LM2 Q14).
218. Churning violates even with suitable securities (III(A) Ex 6). Personal benefit from a broker taints broker selection (Ex 4).
219. Recommended statements: at least quarterly, itemized, showing where assets are held; segregate client assets (III(A); L2784). Custody brings heightened responsibility (L2731).

## S. CFA designation wording and CFA Institute programs

220. Use of "CFA" requires **both** annual dues and the annual Professional Conduct Statement; a lapsed member may say she **was** a member (VII(B); L4994 to L5010; Adebayo L5703).
221. Retirement needs the formal "retired" status; cards handed to friends still count (VII(B); L5089 to L5095).
222. Never "CFA, Level I/II," "CFA expected [year]," "charter pending," or any partial designation or expected completion date; "passed Level I" and "Level III candidate" are fine (VII(B); L5026, L5054 to L5064; Lopez L5720; Q42 L6953).
223. Candidate status requires current enrollment or awaiting results; skipping the exam ends candidacy until re-enrolling (VII(B); L5018 to L5024). Do not claim completion before results (LM2 Q39).
224. Factual statements (passed three levels consecutively, program enhanced my skills) are fine; "I am the best qualified as a charterholder" or "highest scores" is not (VII(B); L4986, L5026; Q21 L6895; LM2 Q38).
225. Online authorship needs full name with any CFA reference; pseudonyms should not carry "CFA"; order of CFA and other designations is personal choice (VII(B); L5012, L5107, L5113).
226. [YouTube] "CFA is an adjective, not a noun or a degree," never part of a firm name (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU , https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA). This wording is not in the 2026 curriculum file; treat it as a prep tip.
227. VII(A) confidentiality covers specific questions, broad topics, and formulas, tested **or not tested**; "there were no ethics questions on insider trading" violates; "the exam was hard" is fine (L4860 to L4862, L4928; Mehrotra L5709).
228. Both giving and soliciting exam information violate VII(A) (L4908, L4936; Mehrotra L5709). Prep staff violate only if they are members or candidates (L4944).
229. VII(A) covers all CFA Institute programs, testing rules (calculators, stop-writing), volunteers (graders, proctors), and false statements on the PCS (L4836, L4848, L4868 to L4878).
230. A volunteer may state the role factually but not imply a client advantage (VII(A); L4958).

## T. Law, dissociation, and reporting

231. Stricter rule in two steps: first find the applicable law, then follow the stricter of that law and the Code (I(A); L1431 to L1433). "Stricter" means more restriction **or** more investor protection (L1433).
232. Dissociation is **required**; reporting to regulators or CFA Institute is **encouraged**, not compelled unless law requires it (I(A); L1457 to L1459; Sim L6101).
233. Dissociation ladder: speak to the wrongdoer, then supervisor or compliance, then step away (remove name, decline the client), document, and resign in extreme cases (I(A); L1457, L1573, L1607).
234. Silence while a superior misrepresents "at minimum, assists" the violation (I(A); Mehrotra L5703 to L5707).
235. First step on suspected wrongdoing by a colleague: compliance or legal counsel (I(A); Sim L6101).
236. Non-member candidates are bound by exam rules, the Candidate Responsibility Statement, and the Candidate Pledge, not the Membership Agreement (I(A); L1447 to L1451).

## U. Code basics, the Professional Conduct Program, and the 2023 revisions

237. Board of Governors oversees the PCP; PCP with the Disciplinary Review Committee enforces; Standards of Practice Council interprets (L963, L987). Do not swap DRC and SPC.
238. A DRC panel is used only if the member rejects the charges and proposed sanction (L969). A cautionary letter is not a sanction (L968 to L970).
239. Inquiry sources include self-disclosure on the PCS, complaints, media, exam proctor reports, and score analysis or social-media monitoring (L964).
240. 2023 revisions: new I(E); revised V(B) (nature of services and costs) and VI(A) (renamed "Avoid or Disclose Conflicts") (L925 to L959). [YouTube] videos listing "22 sub-standards" or four under Standard I predate I(E).
241. Code component 6 says "maintain and improve" the competence of others; I(E) says "act with and maintain" competence (L1092; [YouTube] https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo).
242. Senior management's culture may be the single most important factor; a code alone is not enough (L1053). Universal ethics should rank above local custom (L1023).
243. Credentials or a passed exam do not by themselves show competence; new duties require new competence (I(E); L2226, L2228).

## V. Asset Manager Code (firm level)

244. AMC binds the **firm**, whether or not anyone holds the charter; firms may also adopt the Code and Standards (L6985, L7555; Q8 L6861).
245. All or nothing: no "except for" claims and no "parts A, B, and C" claims; an existing consistent code need not be rewritten (L6998 to L7000).
246. The compliance statement must say "This claim has not been verified by CFA Institute." Notify CFA Institute online; it does not verify or enforce (L7004, L7008).
247. Code provisions are required; the Appendix guidance is not strictly required. Read the stem: "required standards" vs "required and recommended standards" (L6998; Q10 L7745).
248. Gifts of more than minimal value from service providers or targets must be refused; the firm defines minimal value; cash gifts should be banned; all gifts reported (A.3; L7178 to L7180).
249. Board seat plus options in a company the fund can buy: the book says terminate it, not just disclose (A.1, A.3; L7727).
250. Revealing that a client hired the firm, or its fee discount, breaches A.2 (L7733).
251. Style drift: disclose well in advance, keep managing to the mandate until then, allow redemption without undue penalty; better returns do not cure it (B.5; L7238, L7725).
252. B.5 covers pooled funds and mandates where client details are unknown; B.6 (know your client, IPS) covers separate accounts (L7054 to L7062).
253. Accepting a client's directed brokerage with written acknowledgment is fine; routing other clients' trades behind it breaks best execution and fair allocation (C.4, C.5; Q3 L7729, Q4 L7731).
254. Soft dollars must directly aid investment decisions, not firm management; Soft Dollar Standards are only "should consider" (C.3; L7296 to L7304).
255. Compliance officer should be independent and report to the CEO or board, not the CIO; an unqualified relative fails D.2 and D.5 (L7338; Q6 L7735, Q7 L7739).
256. BCP minimum list (five items) is most often missing **alternative monitoring, analysis, and trading** plans; test the plan firmwide (D.6; L7385, L7397; Q8 L7741).
257. Outsourcing is allowed, but the manager keeps responsibility (D.5, D.7; L7372, L7409). Risk management must be independent of portfolio managers (D.7; L7409).
258. GIPS is a model, not an AMC requirement; hypothetical results must be labeled (E.1; L7427 to L7429).
259. Internal valuation models are fine in good faith for hard-to-value assets; valuation disclosure must be by asset class and not boilerplate (E.2, F.4.g; L7521; Q12).
260. Fees: give prospects average or expected fees (required); give itemized actual fees when asked, including incentive fee and commissions (F.4.d; Q9 L7743, Q11 L7751).
261. Performance reporting: requirement is "regular and timely"; recommendation is at least quarterly within 30 days; there is no monthly requirement, and lock-ups do not excuse semiannual reporting (F.4.f; Q10 L7745, Q12 L7753).
262. Audit results must be disclosed **if** the manager submits to an audit; significant changes include staff turnover and the manager's M&A (F.4.j, F.4.k; L7539, L7543).
263. Disciplinary disclosure covers actions by any regulator **or other organization** (F.4.b; L7487). Proxy policy disclosure must include how clients can learn how their shares were voted (F.4.h; L7531).

## W. Exam-format and answer-pattern traps

264. In the curriculum practice, 13 of 47 answers were "no violation"; do not assume a violation just because conduct looks questionable (curriculum L6847 to L6963).
265. Right verdict, wrong reason: "Yes, because he was an unpaid intern" gives the right verdict for the wrong reason (Q7). Always check the reason clause.
266. Watch the time point: the same act can be right at one stage and wrong at another (keep the manager during review, fire after; Q4 vs Q6).
267. Half-measures and overreactions are common distractors: reducing an allocation instead of firing (Q6), resigning instead of declining supervision in writing (Q9), terminating a broker instead of not using MNPI (Q44).
268. "Least likely violated" items list three standards; two are probably violated (Q29, Q37, Q40).
269. [Web] Official exam: 4 questions per item set, no penalty for wrong answers, so answer every question (https://www.cfainstitute.org/programs/cfa/exam/level-iii). The number of answer choices per item-set question is not stated on the official page.
270. [YouTube] Presenters say ethics appears in item sets, rarely if ever in essays (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lAk6orRwPV8 , https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc). Not confirmed officially; the official page says topics are placed randomly.
271. [YouTube] The most common wrong framework answers skip "Consider" or "Reflect"; memory aid "I Can Decide, Right?" for Identify, Consider, Decide and act, Reflect (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LalyW-mSiU4). Curriculum: reflection should conclude the process (L5626).
272. [YouTube] "When in doubt, disclose and get consent" is a prep shortcut (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA); remember the curriculum exceptions where disclosure is not enough (items 12 to 14).
