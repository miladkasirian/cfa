## V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must:
> 1. Exercise diligence, independence, and thoroughness in analyzing investments, making investment recommendations, and taking investment actions.
> 2. Have a reasonable and adequate basis, supported by appropriate research and investigation, for any investment analysis, recommendation, or action.
>
> (curriculum L1188 to L1192)

### What it requires
- How much diligence depends on: (1) your or your firm's investment philosophy, (2) your role in the decision, (3) the support and resources your employer gives (curriculum L3962).
- Make reasonable efforts to cover all relevant issues. Offering clients the supporting information **enhances transparency** (good practice) (curriculum L3964).
- A reasonable basis comes from a **balance** of sources suited to the decision: company reports, third-party research, model output (curriculum L3972).
- Factors to consider (not a full list) (curriculum L3974 to L3990): macro conditions; a company's history; industry and business cycle; a fund's fees and management history; model output and limits; quality of assets in a securitization; choice of peer groups.
- Diligence **minimizes**, it cannot remove, downside surprises. You are judged on information available at the time (curriculum L3992).

**Secondary and third-party research** (curriculum L3994 to L4012)
- Make reasonable, diligent efforts to check it is sound.
- If you suspect it lacks a sound basis, you **MUST NOT** rely on it.
- Check the source and accuracy of all data. Internet sources (blogs, aggregators, social media) need **more** review than established research firms.
- Criteria: assumptions; rigor; date and timeliness; objectivity and independence.
- You may rely in good faith on others in your firm who vetted the source (for example, a data vendor chosen by senior management), unless you have reason to doubt it.
- SHOULD check that the firm reviews approved research providers regularly; if not, encourage it.

**Quantitative research** (curriculum L4014 to L4026)
- Users need not be experts in every technical detail, but **MUST** understand a model's assumptions and limits and how its results are used.
- Make reasonable efforts to **test** model output; validate **before** using it.
- Use a broad range of assumptions, including negative and extreme scenarios; leaving out bad outcomes can misstate value.

**Developing quantitative techniques** (curriculum L4028 to L4032)
- Creators of models need a **higher** level of diligence than users.
- Understand the technical side; test fully **before** release.
- Databases may miss full market cycles; test with scenarios outside the data, including negative events.

**Selecting external advisers and subadvisers** (curriculum L4034 to L4050)
- Review managers as diligently as individual securities.
- SHOULD have standardized criteria, including: the adviser's code of ethics; its compliance and internal controls; quality of its published returns; its investment process and whether it sticks to its stated strategy.
- CFA Institute tools: Asset Manager Code, GIPS, Model RFP.

**Group research** (curriculum L4052 to L4054)
- A group report is the consensus, not necessarily your view, even with your name on it.
- If you disagree but believe the consensus has a reasonable and adequate basis and is independent and objective, you **need not** dissociate.

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Secondary research | Research done by someone else **inside** your firm (curriculum L3996). |
| Third-party research | Research done **outside** your firm, such as a broker, bank, or research firm (curriculum L3996). |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4056 to L4072)
Encourage the firm to have:
1. A policy that research, credit ratings, and recommendations have a reasonable and adequate basis, approved by a supervisory analyst or review committee **before external release**.
2. Written due diligence guidance for analysts, supervisors, and review committees.
3. Measurable criteria for research quality and accuracy over time (pay may be tied to them, applied consistently).
4. Written minimum scenario testing for all computer models (breadth of scenarios, accuracy over time, sensitivity of cash flows to inputs).
5. Measurable criteria for outside data and research providers, and how often they are reviewed.
6. Standard criteria for judging external advisers, including how often the allocation is reviewed.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Hawke prices IPOs only on company size) | Violation | Take on only work you can handle; "justify later" is no basis. |
| 2 | Sufficient Scenario Testing (Dhaliwal shows best case as base case) | Violation | Give a range of scenarios with probabilities. |
| 3 | Developing a Reasonable Basis (Witt uses a few articles and hype) | Violation | Articles and conventional wisdom alone are not a basis. See IV(C). |
| 4 | Timely Client Updates (Chandler ignores a manager's staff exodus) | Failed V(A) | Diligence continues until delivery; reflect new material facts. |
| 5 | Group Research Opinions (committee changes Mastakis's forecast) | Name removal not required | Fine if the group process has a reasonable basis; she may ask to remove her name. |
| 6 | Reliance on Third-Party Research (McDermott's two-person firm) | Permitted | Only with regular due diligence on the research's soundness. |
| 7 | Due Diligence in Submanager Selection (Ostrowski picks lowest fee) | May be violating | Review services, performance, and costs, not fee alone. See III(C). |
| 8 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Papis moves money to a friend) | Violation | Arbitrary choice; run a proper search. See IV(C), V(B), VI(A). |
| 9 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Shrub's fund of a friend's funds) | Violation | Friendship and past knowledge are no substitute for full review of operations and transparency. See III(C). |
| 10 | Sufficient Due Diligence (Thompson tests falling house prices) | Complies | Testing beyond historical data is good diligence. See I(B). |
| 11 | Use of Quantitatively Oriented Models (Liakos's untested combined strategy) | Violation | Test the strategy as a whole, not just its parts; be able to explain results. |
| 12 | Successful Due Diligence/Failed Investment (Newbury's hedge fund loses 60%) | Complies | A sound basis does not guarantee results; explain the process and that losses can happen. |
| 13 | Quantitative Model Diligence (Cannon adds "Expert CFA" blog factors) | Violation | A blog may give ideas, but test the effect yourself before use. See VII(B). |
| 14 | Selecting a Service Provider (Smith picks a "black box" for its looks) | Insufficient diligence | Review all options and check fit with the firm's process. |
| 15 | Subadviser Selection (Jackson picks highest return from a GIPS list) | Insufficient review | Returns and a GIPS claim alone are not enough. |
| 16 | Manager Selection (Green uses only RFP answers) | Likely not satisfied | Verify: interviews, regulatory filings, custodian or auditor talks. |
| 17 | Technical Model Requirements (Dupont's buggy fix; PM Smith follows blindly) | Both violate | Dupont violates though he never trades; Smith violates by not understanding the model he relies on. See V(B). |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q28: Willier changes a weak "hold" to a strong "buy" after overhearing another analyst. **Violation** of V(A): another person's opinion is not a reasonable basis. If it had prompted new research that justified the change, it would be fine. The distractor citing fact versus opinion names the wrong Standard.

> **Traps: V(A)**
> - "Reasonable and adequate basis" belongs to V(A), not to III(E) or V(B).
> - Reason to doubt a source: you must not rely on it. Good-faith reliance on firm-vetted sources is fine.
> - Blogs and social media need more checking; they can give ideas, not a basis (Ex 13).
> - Model users: understand assumptions and limits. Model builders: higher diligence. Both can violate (Ex 17).
> - Test combined strategies as a whole (Ex 11). Present ranges, not the best case (Ex 2).
> - Manager selection: not fee only, not returns plus GIPS only, not friendship, not unverified RFP data.
> - A loss after proper diligence is not a violation (Ex 12).
> - Group research: you need not dissociate if the process is sound.
> - Verdict wording varies: "may be violating" (Ex 7), "likely not satisfied" (Ex 16).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Don't change a recommendation just because a respected person disagrees; redo the research." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ar8-0Oogeuo) Matches Q28.
> - If a quant model was only a secondary basis and is dropped, the fundamental research still gives a reasonable basis. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc)
> - Keyword "immediately": updating materials with a new third-party rating immediately, without checking it, points to a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc)
> - Conflict to note: a CFA Society Los Angeles deck summarizes "dissociate if you disagree with group research". (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf) The curriculum says you need **not** dissociate if you are confident the process gave a reasonable and adequate basis (curriculum L4054, Ex 5). Follow the curriculum.

## V(B) Communication with Clients and Prospective Clients

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must:
> 1. Disclose to clients and prospective clients the nature of the services provided, along with information about the costs to the client associated with those services.
> 2. Disclose to clients and prospective clients the basic format and general principles of the investment processes they use to analyze investments, select securities, and construct portfolios and must promptly disclose any changes that might materially affect those processes.
> 3. Disclose to clients and prospective clients significant limitations and risks associated with the investment process.
> 4. Use reasonable judgment in identifying which factors are important to their investment analyses, recommendations, or actions and include those factors in communications with clients and prospective clients.
> 5. Distinguish between fact and opinion in the presentation of investment analysis and recommendations.
>
> (curriculum L1194 to L1208; part 1 was added in the 2023 revision, effective 2024)

### What it requires
**Nature of services and costs** (curriculum L4247 to L4260)
- Disclose **all** professional services, including what will **not** be provided.
- **MUST** tell clients and prospects about services and the costs **to the client** (curriculum L4251).
- Give a reasonable amount of detail. **Not required:** exact dollar amounts (a fee based on future asset value cannot be known in advance), or the **firm's** own cost of providing the service (curriculum L4251).
- Best practice: describe services and costs in **writing at the outset**, in the client agreement (curriculum L4250 to L4251).
- Ongoing duty: if services or costs change, update **all** affected clients and prospects on a timely basis (curriculum L4251 to L4252).
- Disclose **all** costs, including those of affiliates and third parties whose products are used (curriculum L4254).
- Client sophistication is **not** a reason to skip disclosure, but you may tailor the detail to the client (curriculum L4254).
- Manner is up to you if it is appropriate, accurate, timely, and complete; written is best practice (curriculum L4255 to L4259).
- Firm-written disclosures can be used but do not remove your responsibility. If they are flawed: tell the firm; supplement them if possible; if not fixed, document objections and dissociate (curriculum L4259).
- Applies generally only to **client-facing** members (curriculum L4260).

**Investment process** (curriculum L4262 to L4266)
- Describe how decisions are made, the factors with positive and negative effects, and significant risks and limits.
- Keep clients informed of changes on an ongoing basis.
- Show how an investment affects the whole portfolio (a stock that is 90% of one client's assets versus 2% of a diversified one).
- If outside advisers manage part of the assets, SHOULD tell clients what expertise they add.

**Forms of communication** (curriculum L4270 to L4274)
- Includes in-person, phone, broadcast, internet, social media, from one word ("buy") to 100+ page reports.
- Social media may not reach all clients: take steps to treat all fairly.
- Capsule recommendations (a stock list): SHOULD tell clients more information is available.

**Risks and limitations** (curriculum L4276 to L4281)
- Outline significant risks and limits. Leverage is in general a significant risk to disclose.
- Disclose general market risks and significant risks of complex instruments. May also consider counterparty, country, sector, security, and credit risk.
- Report significant limits such as **liquidity** and **capacity**.
- Judged **ex ante**: disclose risks known at the time; you cannot be expected to disclose unknown risks.
- A one-time loss after disclosure does not prove poor disclosure. Not knowing a risk may be a V(A) problem, not necessarily V(B).

**Report presentation** (curriculum L4285 to L4288)
- Include what the reader needs to follow and challenge your reasoning.
- You may focus on some areas and skip others if you clearly state the limits of the report's scope.
- Quantitative advice: back it with available reference material, apply methods consistently, and highlight method changes.

**Fact versus opinion** (curriculum L4290 to L4294)
- Separate opinion from fact. Common failure: not marking earnings estimates, dividend outlooks, or price forecasts as opinions.
- Separate facts from statistical conjecture; failing to state limits of statistical projections may violate V(B).
- Discuss model assumptions with clients; do not oversell model accuracy.

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Ability to sell an investment in a timely way at a reasonable cost (curriculum L4280). |
| Capacity | The amount invested above which new money hurts returns (curriculum L4280). |
| Ex ante | Judged by what was known when the action was taken (curriculum L4281). |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4296 to L4300)
- Whether you included the right factors is judged case by case, not by checklist.
- Encourage a rigorous review process for research before release.
- To support later review, you **MUST** keep records of the nature of the research, and SHOULD be able to give clients more information on factors left out of the report.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Costs of Services to Clients (ABC keeps structuring fees when deals do well) | Violation (Alphonso) | Undisclosed compensation; sophistication of investors and "win-win" do not excuse it. |
| 2 | Costs of Services to Clients (James's manager search shows all fees) | No violation | Uniform comparison of performance and full fee schedules meets V(B). |
| 3 | Disclosure of Changed Fee Calculation Methodology (Maalouf's firm changes fee base) | Must disclose | Changing method without telling clients is improper, even if fees fall. |
| 4 | Sufficient Disclosure of Investment System (Williamson's top-five newsletter) | Violation | Need not give every detail, but must explain the basic process and logic. |
| 5 | Providing Opinions as Facts (Dox's "fact" of 500,000 ounces) | Violation if issued | A reserve estimate is an opinion. |
| 6 | Proper Description of a Security (Thomas hides how the structured product works) | Violation | Describe structure, risks, leverage, and both rising and falling rate scenarios; disclose liquidity terms. |
| 7 | Notification of Fund Mandate Change (May raises market-cap ceiling) | Must tell existing clients | Telling only prospects and consultants is not enough. |
| 8 | Notification of Fund Mandate Change (May adds non-US stocks) | Must tell clients | Other notifiable changes: adding derivatives, relaxing limits such as beta. |
| 9 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (RJZ switches P/E model to DDM) | Must disclose | Moving to a forecast-based model is a material change. |
| 10 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (RJZ quietly shifts to passive) | Violation | A substantial process change must be disclosed. |
| 11 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (stock picks move to a committee) | Must disclose | Clients may care about who decides. |
| 12 | Sufficient Disclosure of Investment System (Chinn explains new outside managers) | No violation | He disclosed the change, so the client can decide. |
| 13 | Notification of Changes to the Investment Process (Papis's quiet adviser switch) | Violation | Trustees and plan members must learn of the change. See IV(C), V(A), VI(A). |
| 14 | Notification of Errors (Dupont's model error; Smith rebalances silently) | Violation (Smith) | Material errors in the process and the fix must be told to clients. Dupont's issue is V(A). |
| 15 | Notification of Risks and Limitations (capacity limit of about US$100m omitted) | Violation (Yakovlev and Wellard) | Disclose capacity limits even when far below them. |
| 16 | Notification of Risks and Limitations (Ramon praises VaR) | Insufficient | Explain how inputs were chosen and the model's limits. |
| 17 | Notification of Risks and Limitations (Smith: "Our clients are sure to benefit") | Conflicts with V(B) | Opinion presented as fact; also check V(A) basis. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q29: Scott's report says the price will rise "because the dividend will increase" though it depends on uncertain legislation. **Violation** of V(B): opinion stated as fact. The legislative outlook is conjecture, not MNPI. Using uncertain future events is allowed if labeled as opinion.
- LM1 PP4: V(B) requires **prompt** disclosure of changes that might materially affect the investment process.
- LM1 PP9: fact versus opinion sits in V(B).
- LM1 PP3: the Standards address risks and limitations of recommendations.

> **Traps: V(B)**
> - Cost disclosure is required; exact dollar figures and the firm's own costs are not.
> - Written disclosure at the outset is best practice, not a must.
> - Ongoing duty: changed fees or methods need disclosure even if the change helps clients (Ex 3).
> - Sophisticated clients still get disclosure (Ex 1).
> - Applies mainly to client-facing members. In Ex 14 the client-facing PM Smith violates V(B); model builder Dupont violates V(A).
> - Risk disclosure is ex ante. Unknown risk later causing a loss: maybe V(A), not V(B).
> - Capacity and liquidity are named limits; disclose capacity even far from the threshold (Ex 15).
> - Existing clients must hear of mandate or process changes, not only prospects (Ex 7).
> - Estimates, forecasts, reserves, and "sure to benefit" claims are opinions.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - Keyword trap: "will" versus "may"; presenting an opinion as fact. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aT8sGNsyFo0)
> - Disclose model limitations and risks. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eyh4l5Y7iw)
> - Official fact confirmed by YouTube and web notes: the 2024 revision added the nature of services and costs to V(B). (https://www.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/support/ethics/summary-of-the-2023-revisions-to-the-cfa-institute-code-and-standards.pdf) Videos made before 2024 omit this part.

## V(C) Record Retention

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must develop and maintain appropriate records to support their investment analyses, recommendations, actions, and other investment-related communications with clients and prospective clients. (curriculum L1210 to L1212)

### What it requires
- Keep records that show the scope of research and the reasons for actions or conclusions (curriculum L4449).
- Applies to buy and sell decisions **and** to reviews that lead to **no change** (curriculum L4449).
- Which records depends on your role. Paper or electronic is fine (curriculum L4449).
- Examples of records (curriculum L4451 to L4467): meeting notes with the company; press releases and presentations; model output and analysis; model input parameters; risk analyses of portfolio impact; selection criteria for external advisers; notes from IPS review meetings with clients; outside research reports.
- **New media** (curriculum L4469 to L4483): format does not remove the duty. Keep e-mails, texts, blog posts, social media posts. Firm and regulator policies may lag, putting **more** duty on you.
- **Records are firm property** (curriculum L4485 to L4487):
  - You cannot take originals or **copies** to a new employer without the old employer's **express consent**.
  - You cannot reuse old recommendations or reports at the new firm, because the support is not available.
  - Re-create supporting records at the new firm from public sources or from the company, **not** from memory or old-employer sources.
- **Local requirements** (curriculum L4489 to L4491): follow regulator and firm rules; meeting them satisfies V(C). With no regulator or firm rule, CFA Institute **recommends at least seven years**.

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4493 to L4495)
- The duty to keep records generally falls on the **firm**.
- But you must archive research notes and documents (paper or electronic) that support your current investment communications, to help the firm comply.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record Retention and IPS Objectives and Recommendations (Lindstrom, tech-heavy benchmark, unhappy client) | Complies if records exist | Keep records of benchmark weights, the agreed IPS, explanations given, and regular IPS updates. |
| 2 | Record Retention and Research Process (Young's interviews, visits, surveys) | Must document; failure violates | Keep copies of all inputs, including secondary and third-party research. |
| 3 | Records as Firm, Not Employee, Property (Blank takes model records to a competitor) | Violation | Records belong to the old firm; re-create them at the new one. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q30: Cannan saved client files on her home computer working weekends, then used them after being laid off. **Violation:** no permission to keep or use them; she should have deleted them. Own time and own computer do not make the files hers. Using a personal computer for work is not itself banned.
- LM1 PP5: record maintenance is V(C), not II(A).

> **Traps: V(C)**
> - Reviews that change nothing still need records.
> - Seven years is only a CFA Institute recommendation for when no law or firm rule exists. Meeting the local rule is enough.
> - Records are firm property; even copies need express consent. Re-create from public or company sources, never from memory.
> - The firm carries the main duty, but you must still archive your support.
> - Social media and texts count as records.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - The web research could not confirm the "seven years" figure in the online Handbook (https://www.finquiz.com/blog/10-minute-review-cfa-ethics-standards/). The 2026 curriculum does state it (curriculum L4491), so it is official for the exam.

## VI(A) Avoid or Disclose Conflicts

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must avoid or make full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair their independence and objectivity and interfere with respective duties to their clients, prospective clients, and employer. Members and Candidates must ensure that such disclosures are prominent, are delivered in plain language, and communicate the relevant information effectively. (curriculum L1216 to L1218; renamed and revised in 2023, effective 2024)

### What it requires
- **Best practice:** avoid actual conflicts and even the appearance of conflicts (curriculum L4545).
- When avoiding is not reasonable, clear and complete disclosure is **necessary** (curriculum L4547).
- Avoid or fully disclose all **actual and potential** conflicts to clients, prospects, and employers (curriculum L4549).
- Disclosure **MUST** be prominent, in plain language, and effective. You decide how often and how. Best practice: update disclosures when a conflict changes materially (for example, bonuses move from annual to quarterly profits). Err on the side of caution (curriculum L4553).
- Common source: pay structures, especially bonuses that reward short-term results (curriculum L4545).

**To employers** (curriculum L4555 to L4563)
- Give enough information for the employer to judge the impact.
- Report **promptly**: owning stock you analyze or recommend, outside board seats, financial or other pressures that could affect decisions.
- If the employer bans certain activities (personal trading, outside boards), you **MUST** comply.
- Take reasonable steps to avoid conflicts; if not resolved, report them promptly.

**To clients** (curriculum L4565 to L4575)
- Disclosure lets clients judge your motives.
- Must **always** be avoided or disclosed: issuer relationships such as directorships or consulting; investment banking, underwriting, and financial relationships; broker-dealer market making; material beneficial ownership of stock.
- Take reasonable steps to find and disclose your **firm's** conflicts.
- Market making: tells clients trades may be with the firm's own account and the firm cares about the price.
- Disclose conflicts from fee arrangements, subadvisory deals, nonstandard fees, and arrangements where the firm benefits from recommendations (for example, fund fee rebates).

**Cross-departmental conflicts** (curriculum L4577 to L4580): pressure from investment banking, issuers, or marketing to cover or praise a stock; broker-sponsored limited partnerships. Resolve or disclose.

**Stock ownership** (curriculum L4582 to L4584): the **most common** conflict. Banning ownership is simplest but may be too restrictive. **MUST** disclose any beneficial ownership in what you recommend.

**Board member or director** (curriculum L4586 to L4592): three conflicts:
1. duties to clients versus duties to the company's shareholders;
2. director pay in shares or options may tempt trading that raises their value;
3. access to material nonpublic information, or the perception that it flows to your firm.
- Remedy: isolate the director from investment decision makers with **firewalls**.

### Key definitions
- **Conflict of interest:** any matter that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity, or raise doubt that your judgment is free of bias (curriculum L4545).
- **Beneficial ownership** (VI(A)): you have (1) a direct or indirect pecuniary interest, or (2) the power to vote or direct the vote, or (3) the power to sell or direct the sale (curriculum L4573).

### Recommended procedures
- The 2026 curriculum has **no** separate Recommended Procedures section for VI(A); it goes straight from guidance to examples. (Personal-trading procedures are under VI(B).)

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conflict of Interest and Business Relationships (Weiss; firm is Jimco's M&A adviser) | Must disclose in the report | Underwriting and advisory ties threaten objectivity. |
| 2 | Conflict of Interest and Business Stock Ownership (Hobbs upgrades the new 25% owner) | Best: stop coverage; minimum: disclose to all clients | Portfolio managers must also disclose the ownership when acting on it. |
| 3 | Conflict of Interest and Personal Stock Ownership (Fargmon's wife inherits shares) | Minimum: disclose to employer and in report | Best practice: ask for another analyst. A spouse's holding counts. |
| 4 | Conflict of Interest and Personal Stock Ownership (Roberts holds penny stock she would rate "buy") | Must disclose to employer; consider declining | If assigned anyway, disclose in the report. The conflict exists even if her view would be the same. |
| 5 | Conflict of Interest and Compensation Arrangements (Snead's quarterly bonus) | Violation | Must tell clients of a pay change that conflicts with their long-term IPSs. Performance pay itself is allowed. |
| 6 | Conflict of Interest and Compensation Arrangements (Carter paid by a stock promoter) | Violation | Minimum: disclose to clients. Best: decline the pay. |
| 7 | Conflict of Interest and Requested Favors (Papis and his friend Nagle) | Violation | He did not tell his **employer** about the personal relationship. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q31: The conflict that must be avoided or disclosed is XYZ's **substantial position in JNI for its own account**. Buying JNI's products, or a brother-in-law who is a JNI supplier, is too remote.
- LM2 Q32: Jamison's firm is Britland's long-time M&A adviser and two officers sit on Britland subsidiary boards. **Best:** do not write the report. If not avoided, disclose **both** the client and the director relationships. Disclosing only one is wrong; refusing to give an opinion does not remove the duty.

> **Traps: VI(A)**
> - "Best" stems: avoid beats disclose. "Minimum" stems: disclose.
> - Disclose to clients, prospects, **and** the employer. Papis (Ex 7) failed toward his employer.
> - Firm conflicts count: underwriting, market making, M&A advisory, board seats, ownership stakes.
> - Spouse and family holdings count as beneficial ownership.
> - The conflict exists even if your opinion would not change (Ex 4).
> - Remote links (using the company's products, distant relatives) usually need no disclosure (Q31).
> - Board seats: firewalls.
> - No Recommended Procedures section for VI(A) in 2026.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "When a question describes a conflict and asks what to do, the best answer is almost always to avoid the conflict." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=blRxYLK7K6g)
> - "When in doubt, disclose" is called the "golden rule" by 300Hours (https://300hours.com/cfa-ethics-tips/) and CFA Society Los Angeles (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf). Caveat from CFA Society Los Angeles: since 2024 VI(A) puts avoidance first, and disclosure does not help when a Standard prohibits the act or requires consent. (https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf)
> - Older videos call VI(A) "Disclosure of Conflicts"; that name is outdated since 2024. (YouTube notes, for example https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gsfdl2CzaSc)

## VI(B) Priority of Transactions

### Standard text
> Investment transactions for clients and employers must have priority over investment transactions in which a Member or Candidate is the beneficial owner. (curriculum L1220 to L1222)

### What it requires
- Clients' and employers' interests come before personal financial interests. Client trades also come before trades for the member's **firm** (curriculum L4660).
- Prevents conflicts and the **appearance** of conflicts.
- Personal investing is fine if all three hold (curriculum L4666):
  1. the client is not disadvantaged;
  2. you do not personally benefit from trades made for clients;
  3. you follow applicable regulations.
- A personal trade against current recommendations (for tuition, a home, a margin call) is allowed under the same three tests (curriculum L4666 to L4667).
- Owning the same securities as clients, or co-investing, does not always create a conflict; some clients want aligned interests. Personal or firm positions must **never** hurt client investments (curriculum L4671).
- **Nonpublic information** (curriculum L4675):
  - do not pass **any** nonpublic information to a person whose relationship makes you a beneficial owner of their securities;
  - do not pass nonpublic information to **anyone** if it is **material**.
- Personal trades in beneficially owned accounts only after clients and employers have had an **adequate opportunity** to act (curriculum L4679).
- Personal transactions include your own account, family accounts (spouse, children, other immediate family), and accounts with a direct or indirect pecuniary interest such as a trust or retirement account (curriculum L4679).
- Family accounts that are **client** accounts: treat like any other client, with no special treatment and no disadvantage. You may still face employer or legal preclearance and reporting rules (curriculum L4679).

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4683 to L4720)
Urge the firm to set personal trading policies fitted to its size and clients, and to disclose them prominently. Basic procedures:
1. **Limited participation in equity IPOs:**
   - Two conflicts: taking an attractive opportunity from clients, and the IPO acting as a reward or incentive from the provider.
   - Avoid by not taking part in IPOs. Supervisors should review IPO participation systematically.
   - Preclear IPO participation even when there is no conflict with clients.
   - Do not benefit from clients' market position (preferred trading, limited offerings, oversubscription).
2. **Restrictions on private placements:**
   - Strict limits and supervisory review.
   - Biggest conflict for those managing large pools or acting as plan sponsors, who may be offered placements as rewards.
   - Avoid deals that look like favors or gifts.
   - If the investment later goes public, you have an incentive to recommend it regardless of suitability.
3. **Blackout or restricted periods:**
   - Set before client trades, to prevent front-running.
   - At minimum, everyone involved in investment decisions has the **same** restricted period.
   - Large firm: effectively a total trading ban. Small firm: more likely to stop front-running.
4. **Reporting requirements** (best way to enforce):
   - Disclose beneficially owned holdings at the start of employment and **at least annually**, handled confidentially.
   - Duplicate confirmations of all personal trades and account statements sent to the firm. Two purposes: signals independent checking (deters misconduct), and shows the flow of trades, which holdings alone cannot.
   - Preclearance of all planned personal trades.
5. **Disclosure of policies:** tell investors your personal trading policies in a helpful way, not boilerplate such as "personnel are subject to policies regarding personal trading".

### Key definitions
- **Front-running:** trading personally before client accounts (curriculum L4702).
- **Beneficial ownership:** see VI(A) (pecuniary interest, voting power, or power to sell).

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Personal Trading (Long delays a buy call to trade first) | Violation | Using knowledge before the employer can benefit. |
| 2 | Trading for Family Member Account (Baker buys hot issues for husband's account) | Violation as described | Fill the fund first, husband's account only after; disclose the account to the employer. |
| 3 | Family Accounts as Equals (Toffler gives parents' client account IPOs last) | Violation | Family client accounts must be treated equally, not disadvantaged; preclearance and reporting may apply. |
| 4 | Personal Trading and Disclosure (Michaels buys ahead of the buy list, skips forms) | Violation (Michaels); IV(C) (supervisor) | Supervisor let him work without the form. Tipping others who trade would be II(A). |
| 5 | Trading Prior to Report Dissemination (Riley trades options 7 minutes after TV comments) | Violation (Riley) | Wait until clients can receive and absorb the recommendation; firm trades come after clients. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q33 (negative stem): the statement that clearly **conflicts** with recommended procedures is "report personal trades to employers only if regulators require it". Firms should compare employee and client trades regardless of regulation, confidentially. Disclosing policies to clients and having employees clear trades are consistent.
- LM2 Q34: Rose may sell stock his firm rates "strong buy" to pay tuition, once the firm is properly informed. No ban on trading against the firm's view if clients are not hurt; no need to match client trades.
- LM2 Q7: owning the same securities as clients is not prohibited; VI(B) only requires clients and employer to come first.

> **Traps: VI(B)**
> - Order: clients, then employer, then you. Client trades also come before **firm** trades (Ex 5).
> - Trading against a recommendation for personal needs is allowed if the three tests are met (Q34).
> - Same holdings as clients is not a violation in itself (Q7).
> - Family **client** accounts: equal treatment. Last place is a violation too (Ex 3).
> - Nonpublic information: to beneficially owned persons, none at all; to others, none if material.
> - "Adequate opportunity" means clients have received and absorbed the news (7 minutes after TV is too soon, Ex 5).
> - IPO abstention, preclearance, blackouts, duplicate confirms, annual holdings reports are **recommended procedures**, not the Standard itself.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Family members who are fee-paying clients must be treated like any other client; making the mother wait until all others traded was ruled a violation." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZeKYZYJQBGg, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-JAwzhU-A0o) Matches Ex 3.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles and FinQuiz: order is client, employer, self; family accounts are included. (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf, https://www.finquiz.com/blog/10-minute-review-cfa-ethics-standards/)

## VI(C) Referral Fees

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must disclose to their employer, clients, and prospective clients, as appropriate, any compensation, consideration, or benefit received from or paid to others for the recommendation of products or services. (curriculum L1224 to L1226)

### What it requires
- Tell the employer, clients, and prospects about any benefit for referrals (curriculum L4766).
- Purposes: let them judge (1) any **partiality** in the recommendation and (2) the **full cost** of the services (curriculum L4766).
- Works **both ways**: fees you receive **and** fees you pay to others who refer clients to you (curriculum L4766).
- **Timing:** before entering any formal agreement for services (curriculum L4767).
- **Content:** the nature of the consideration (flat fee or percentage, one-time or continuing, performance-based, research, other noncash benefit) **and** its estimated dollar value (curriculum L4767).
- Consideration includes all fees: cash, soft dollars, or in kind (curriculum L4767).
- It is a disclosure rule, not a ban.

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L4769 to L4772)
- Encourage the employer to set referral fee procedures. The firm may ban referral fees entirely; if not, set steps for approval.
- Employees should tell clients about approved referral programs and give the employer regular updates, **at least quarterly**, on the amount and nature of compensation.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Outside Parties (Brady and Lewis Brothers; White takes Maxwell's plan) | Violation (White) | Referral fees paid in research and directed commissions, for an indefinite period, must be disclosed. Noncash counts. |
| 2 | Disclosure of Interdepartmental Referral Arrangements (Handley paid per internal referral) | Violation | Internal referral pay is treated the same as third-party pay; disclose at referral, in writing, with nature and value. |
| 3 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Informing Firm (Roberts directs commissions to classmate's broker) | Violation | The stated failure is not telling her **employer**. |
| 4 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Outside Organizations (Burl donates fees to child's school) | No violation | Employer and school approved first; client told while setting the IPS. |
| 5 | Disclosure of Referral Arrangements and Outside Parties (consultant Arrow takes kickbacks) | Violation (Arrow) | Undisclosed payments violate even if the manager choice was objective; also follow local consultant rules. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q35: A stated purpose of disclosure is to help the client judge **partiality**. The other purpose is to judge full cost. There is no rule to discount fees. Disclose to prospects **before** the relationship starts, not after.

> **Traps: VI(C)**
> - Fees received **and** paid both count.
> - Disclose before the formal agreement, with nature **and** estimated dollar value.
> - Noncash (research, directed commissions, soft dollars) counts.
> - Internal referrals between departments count (Ex 2).
> - Employer must know too (Ex 3).
> - A good outcome does not excuse a hidden kickback (Ex 5).
> - Quarterly updates to the employer are a recommended procedure, not the Standard.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - In a case discussed on YouTube, disclosure sent only to new clients after they signed drew the comment that it "should have been told first". (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Matches the "before any formal agreement" rule.
> - "When in doubt, disclose and get consent." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA)

## VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must not engage in any conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the CFA designation or the integrity, validity, or security of CFA Institute programs. (curriculum L1230 to L1232)

### What it requires
- Prohibits conduct that weakens public trust that the charter reflects merit and ethics (curriculum L4836).
- Covers **all** CFA Institute programs, including CIPM and Investment Foundations (curriculum L4836).
- Covered conduct includes (curriculum L4838 to L4848):
  - giving or receiving help (cheating) on any exam;
  - breaking program rules and testing policies;
  - giving confidential program or exam information to candidates or the public;
  - ignoring or trying to get around exam security;
  - misusing a link with CFA Institute for personal or professional gain;
  - misrepresenting information on the Professional Conduct Statement or in the Continuing Education Program.

**Confidential program information** (curriculum L4852 to L4864)
- Candidates may not disclose: specific exam questions, and **broad topic areas and formulas tested or not tested**.
- All of it stays confidential until CFA Institute releases it.
- Allowed: discussing non-confidential information and curriculum with others or in study groups.
- CFA Institute polices blogs and forums; suspected violations should be reported.

**Other program rules** (curriculum L4866 to L4880)
- Breaking any testing policy (calculator, personal belongings, Candidate Pledge) violates VII(A).
- **Volunteers** must not disclose or **solicit** confidential material gained before or during exam development and grading: questions, deliberations, scoring information. Same for volunteers in other programs.

**Expressing an opinion** (curriculum L4882 to L4888)
- You may criticize CFA Institute, its programs, policies, and positions.
- But you may not reveal content: actual questions or subjects covered or not covered.

**Candidate Pledge** (curriculum L4890 to L4898): no giving or receiving exam content before or during the exam; after the exam, disclose no part of it and remove no exam materials; all materials are CFA Institute property. Breaking the rules voids results and may lead to suspension or end of candidacy.

### Recommended procedures
- The 2026 curriculum has **no** Recommended Procedures section for VII(A).

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sharing Exam Questions (proctor Nero tips two candidates) | Violation (Nero and both candidates) | Giving and receiving both breach VII(A). |
| 2 | Bringing Written Material into Exam Room (formula on Sullivan's palm) | Violation | Also breaks program rules and the Pledge. |
| 3 | Writing after Exam Period End (Davis keeps filling ovals) | Violation | Unfair advantage, even random answers; ignored the proctor. |
| 4 | Sharing Exam Content (Rossi's blog) | Partly | "Hard but fair" and self-assessment are fine; naming topics covered ("18 derivatives questions") and not covered ("no IPO questions") is a violation. |
| 5 | Sharing Exam Content (Gagne's forum discussion of hard questions) | Violation | Giving and asking for question details. |
| 6 | Sharing Exam Content (Pena answers a prep firm's request) | Violation (Pena) | Prep firm staff who asked also violate if they are members or candidates. |
| 7 | Discussion of Exam Grading Guidelines and Results (grader Whitcomb) | Violation | Broke the Grader Agreement; time passed does not matter. |
| 8 | Compromising CFA Institute Integrity as a Volunteer (Ramirez picks his own clients as speakers) | Violation | Using a volunteer role to benefit yourself and clients. |
| 9 | Compromising CFA Institute Integrity as a Volunteer (Warrenski's GIPS committee seat) | Violation | You may state the role factually, but not imply a client advantage. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q36: Park posts that inventories and taxes had several questions and the exam was very hard. **Violation** for revealing tested topics. Saying it was hard is allowed; using outside study materials is allowed.

> **Traps: VII(A)**
> - Topics and formulas tested **or not tested** are confidential, not only specific questions.
> - Opinions and criticism of CFA Institute are fine; content is not.
> - Asking for confidential information violates, not only giving it.
> - Testing policy breaches (calculator, belongings, stop-writing) are VII(A).
> - Applies to all CFA Institute programs and to volunteers.
> - Misrepresentation on the Professional Conduct Statement is VII(A).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - You may criticize CFA Institute and say the exam was hard. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ar8-0Oogeuo, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU)
> - Indirect hints also violate, for example "I expected X and it didn't come". (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU) Matches Ex 4.
> - Formula on your body, and writing after time is called, are violations. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7rq5_qdUfM) Match Ex 2 and Ex 3.

## VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation, and the CFA Program

### Standard text
> When referring to CFA Institute, CFA Institute membership, the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA Program, Members and Candidates must not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or implications of membership in CFA Institute, holding the CFA designation, or candidacy in the CFA Program. (curriculum L1234 to L1238)

### What it requires
- No promises or guarantees tied to the designation; no exaggeration (curriculum L4984).
- Factual statements about the benefits of the designation are allowed (curriculum L4986).
- Not allowed: overstating competence, or implying that **superior performance** can be expected from a charterholder (curriculum L4986).
- Allowed: stressing commitment to ethics and professional conduct, or the rigor of the CFA Program (curriculum L4986).
- Claims about the **relative merits** of CFA Institute, the Program, or the Code are allowed if stated as **opinion**; statements not framed as opinion need factual support (curriculum L4986 to L4988).
- Applies to every form: letterhead, business cards, bios, directory listings, ads, brochures, resumes, spoken statements (curriculum L4990).

**Membership** (curriculum L4992 to L5000)
- Members are "regular" and "affiliate" members who met the Bylaws.
- To stay active, **every year**: (1) file the Professional Conduct Statement, and (2) pay dues.
- Failing either: no longer an active member; may say you were a member in the past.

**Using the designation** (curriculum L5002 to L5014)
- Charterholders are encouraged to use it correctly, possibly with an accurate explanation of its requirements.
- A charterholder completed the Program and the required work experience, and must also keep up membership requirements.
- Failing a membership requirement forfeits the right to use "CFA" until reactivated; you may say you held it in the past.
- Pseudonyms or online names that hide your identity should not carry the CFA designation.
- Use is governed by the Professional Conduct Statement Agreement, signed before first use and reaffirmed each year.

**Candidacy** (curriculum L5016 to L5026)
- You may mention participation, but state clearly you are a **candidate**; never imply a partial designation.
- You are a candidate if (1) your registration is accepted and you are enrolled for a specified exam, or (2) you sat a specified exam and results are not yet out.
- Registered but not sitting: not an active candidate until you enroll again.
- Never state or imply a partial designation or an **expected completion date**.
- The charter is awarded only after program requirements are met and the CFA Institute **Board of Governors** approves.
- Saying you passed each level in consecutive years is a fact; claiming superior ability from it is a violation.

**Proper and improper references** (curriculum L5028 to L5064)
| Proper | Improper |
|---|---|
| "Completion of the CFA Program has enhanced my portfolio management skills." | "CFA charterholders achieve better performance results." |
| "John Smith passed all three CFA Program exams in three consecutive years." | "John Smith is among the elite, having passed all three CFA exams in three consecutive attempts." |
| "The CFA designation is globally recognized and attests to a charterholder's success in a rigorous and comprehensive study program..." | "As a CFA charterholder, I am the most qualified to manage client investments." |
| "The credibility that the CFA designation affords and the skills the CFA Program cultivates are key assets for my future career development." | "As a CFA charterholder, Jane White provides the best value in trade execution." |
| "I enrolled in the CFA Program to obtain the highest set of credentials in the global investment management industry." | "Enrolling as a candidate in the CFA Program ensures one of becoming better at valuing debt securities." |
| "I passed Level I of the CFA Program." | "CFA, Level II" |
| "I am a 2010 Level III candidate in the CFA Program." | "CFA, Expected 2011" |
| "I passed all three levels of the CFA Program and may be eligible for the CFA charter upon completion of the required work experience." | "John Smith, Charter Pending" |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L5066 to L5069)
- Give written VII(B) guidance to the firm's legal, compliance, public relations, and marketing staff, since others often misuse the designation.
- Encourage templates approved by a central authority (for example compliance) for references to membership, the designation, and candidacy.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Passing Exams in Consecutive Years (AZ ad links first-try passes to fund performance) | Violation | First-try passing is a fact if true; linking it to performance is not allowed. |
| 2 | Right to Use CFA Designation (Vasseur stops dues and PCS, still prints "CFA") | Violation | Right suspended until PCS, dues, and reinstatement are done. |
| 3 | "Retired" CFA Institute Membership Status (Simpson's personal card) | Violation | Retired status with reduced dues exists but must be applied for; personal cards count. |
| 4 | Stating Facts about CFA Designation and Program (Reese to a friend) | No violation | Factual statements and a personal recommendation are fine. |
| 5 | Order of Professional and Academic Designations (CFA and PhD) | No violation either way | Order is personal choice; separate designations with a comma. |
| 6 | Use of Fictitious Name ("Expert CFA" blog) | Violation by "Expert CFA"; user Glass violates V(A) | Online writing with a CFA reference must show the charterholder's full name. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q37: "Judy Albert, CFA Level II" on a card is a **violation** (partial designation). Tye saying he takes part in the Program and completed Levels I and II is **fine**.
- LM2 Q38: Rule may say he passed in three consecutive years (a fact), but "highest possible scores" is a **violation**: candidates do not receive actual scores; the exam is pass or fail and topic results are banded.
- LM2 Q39: Quinn, waiting for Level III results, wrote that he finished the Program and will be eligible for the charter: **violation**. Until results arrive he is still a candidate. Saying the Program improved his skills is fine.
- LM1 PP10: VII(B) is the explicit Standard VII rule about referring to the CFA Program. Loyalty belongs to III(A) and IV(A); supervision to IV(C).

> **Traps: VII(B)**
> - Facts are fine (consecutive years, levels passed, the Program improved my skills). Superior performance or ability claims are not.
> - Relative-merit claims only as opinion.
> - Active status needs **both** annual PCS **and** dues. Retirement needs the formal retired status.
> - No "CFA, Level II", "CFA, Expected [year]", "Charter Pending", no expected completion date.
> - No completion or eligibility claims before results are released (Q39). No "highest score" claims (Q38).
> - Pseudonyms should not carry "CFA" (Ex 6).
> - Covers every format, including personal business cards (Ex 3).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "CFA is an adjective, not a noun or a degree"; write it without periods and never as part of a firm name. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulQvcJsfieA, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VqwxCgjPkTU, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7rq5_qdUfM) These rules are not in the 2026 curriculum text; treat them as Handbook or provider guidance.
> - Introducing someone as "the firm's newest CFA" before the charter is awarded is a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Consistent with curriculum L5026.
> - "Preparing for the CFA designation" versus "CFA Level I candidate": both may be true, but the exam can ask for the "most correct" wording. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo)
> - Schweser cites a real case of a LinkedIn profile claiming "CFA Level II charterholder", which ended in suspension (for non-cooperation). (https://www.schweser.com/cfa/blog/become-a-cfa-charterholder/cfa-code-of-ethics)
> - Commenters' notes show VII(B) wording is heavily drilled. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nYIQmXyINfU comments)
