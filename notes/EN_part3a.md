# Part 3: Standards III(B) to VII(B)

How to read this part:
- **MUST** = the Standard or its guidance uses "must" (a requirement). **SHOULD** = the guidance says "should" or "best practice" (expected conduct, but not the hard rule). **RECOMMENDED PROCEDURE** = listed under the curriculum's "Recommended Procedures" heading (good practice for firms and members, never a requirement in itself).
- "(curriculum L1234)" points to the line in the 2026 Level III Volume 5 curriculum file so you can check it.
- Every application example is one line: number, title, verdict, lesson.
- Official curriculum content comes first. Points from prep providers, YouTubers, and forums are kept apart under "Research points (opinion, not curriculum)", each with its source link.

## III(B) Fair Dealing

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must deal fairly and objectively with all clients when providing investment analysis, making investment recommendations, taking investment action, or engaging in other professional activities. (curriculum L1140)

### What it requires
- **MUST** treat all clients fairly when you (a) send out investment recommendations, (b) make material changes to earlier recommendations, and (c) take investment action (general purchases, new issues, secondary offerings). (curriculum L2921)
- The word is "fairly", not "equally" (curriculum L2923). Reasons:
  - You cannot reach every client at exactly the same moment.
  - Not every opportunity suits every client.
  - Clients who pay more (higher fees or brokerage) may get more personal or in-depth service.
- Premium service is allowed only if (curriculum L2923 to L2925):
  - it does not disadvantage other clients;
  - it is disclosed to clients and prospects (SHOULD);
  - it is available to everyone, not offered selectively (SHOULD).
- Two kinds of conduct are covered: investment recommendations and investment action (curriculum L2927).

**Investment recommendations**
- Covers people whose primary job is preparing recommendations that others act on, inside or outside the firm (curriculum L2933).
- **MUST** spread recommendations so all clients have a fair opportunity to act on every recommendation (curriculum L2935).
- SHOULD encourage the firm to design an equitable system that prevents selective disclosure, and SHOULD tell clients what kinds of communications they will receive (curriculum L2935).
- Changed recommendations: the duty may be **more** critical than for first recommendations (curriculum L2936).
  - Material changes SHOULD go to all current clients.
  - Take extra care to reach clients you know acted on the earlier advice.
  - A client who places an order against a changed recommendation, not knowing of the change, SHOULD be told of the change **before** the order is accepted.

**Investment action**
- Covers portfolio managers acting on internal or external recommendations (curriculum L2940).
- New issues and secondary financings go to all clients for whom they are appropriate, consistent with the firm's block-allocation policy (curriculum L2940).
- Oversubscribed issues (curriculum L2940):
  - prorate to all subscribers, in round lots (no odd lots);
  - members SHOULD forgo purchases for themselves and immediate family to free shares for clients;
  - exception: a family account managed like other client accounts should **not** be excluded.
- Treat individual and institutional clients fairly. One institution may have several relationships with the firm (trustee, pension manager, lender), and each must be treated fairly (curriculum L2944).
- SHOULD disclose documented allocation procedures to clients and prospects, clearly and completely (curriculum L2945).
- **Key rule:** even with full disclosure, client interests come before your own. Client consent to a patently unfair allocation procedure never overrides the duty of fairness and loyalty (curriculum L2945).
- **MUST NOT** withhold hot issues for your own benefit or use them as a reward or incentive. Hot issues need a bona fide public distribution (curriculum L2946).
- Trade allocation procedures **must** be fair and equitable. Disclosing an inequitable method does not relieve you of this duty (curriculum L2999).

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Investment recommendation | Any opinion on buying, selling, or holding a security or other investment. It can be a full report, a short update, adding to or removing from a recommended list, or a spoken comment (curriculum L2934). |
| Communication for general distribution | A recommendation sent to anyone outside the organization (curriculum L2934). |
| Hot issue | Securities of a public offering that trade at a premium in the secondary market as soon as trading starts, because of high demand (curriculum L2946). |

### Recommended procedures
**Develop firm policies** (curriculum L2950 to L2955)
- Encourage the firm to set compliance procedures for fair treatment. At minimum, recommend procedures to management if none exist, and alert management to possible violations you see.
- How formal depends on firm size and the securities involved. Even a sole proprietor with only discretionary accounts SHOULD have formal written procedures.
- You need not send a recommendation to every customer. But choose recipients by **suitability and known interest**, never by favored status.
- Common practice: release recommendations inside the firm and to customers at the same time.

**Points for fair-dealing procedures** (curriculum L2955 to L2995)
1. Limit the number of people who know a recommendation is coming.
2. Shorten the time between the decision and its release:
   - publish a short summary before a long report;
   - hold research and policy committee meetings on the same day if possible;
   - use a "flash" report (e-mail, wire, short note).
3. Publish rules for pre-release behavior: people who know must not discuss or act on it.
4. Release at approximately the same time to all clients:
   - send to all branch offices at the time of the general announcement;
   - employee trading restrictions stay in effect until the recommendation is widely distributed;
   - after distribution you may follow up with individual clients, but give no favored client advance information.
5. Keep a list of clients and their holdings, so changed recommendations reach holders and liquidations are fair.
6. Develop and document trade allocation procedures. Principles: fairness in priority of execution and in allocation of block prices; timely and efficient execution; accurate records. Procedures to consider:
   - time-stamp orders, changes, and cancellations;
   - first-in, first-out execution, with bundling where efficient;
   - a policy for execution prices and partial fills in block trades;
   - all accounts in a block get the **same price** and the **same commission**;
   - partial fills allocated pro rata by order size, but not below a minimum lot size for some securities (for example, bonds);
   - new issues: get advance indications of interest, allocate **by client** (not by portfolio manager), and have a method for calculating allocations.

**Disclose trade allocation procedures** (curriculum L2997 to L2999): how accounts are chosen for an order and how amounts are set. Disclosure does not cure an unfair method.

**Systematic account review** (curriculum L3001 to L3003): supervisors review accounts regularly for favoritism and suitability. Buying a security in one account while selling it in another is allowed if the reasons for **both** sides are documented.

**Disclose levels of service** (curriculum L3005 to L3007): tell all clients whether different service levels exist for the same or different fees. Do not offer levels selectively.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Selective Disclosure (Ames tells best clients at lunch) | Violation | Telling select clients a week before all clients get the report is unfair. |
| 2 | Fair Dealing between Funds (commingled fund first, discretionary before nondiscretionary) | Violation (Jackson) | Priority to one fund or to discretionary accounts is unfair; disclosure would not cure an unfair policy. |
| 3 | Fair Dealing and IPO Distribution (Morris takes hot-issue shares) | Violation; also VI(A) | Take no shares yourself; prorate among clients; disclose the options to firm and clients. |
| 4 | Fair Dealing and Transaction Allocation (Preston gives winners to big client Colby) | Violation | Allocate before execution, or systematically (pro rata) right after. Agency cross trades need disclosure and advance client consent. |
| 5 | Selective Disclosure (Roberts tells large clients EPS will beat guidance) | Violation | His view of a likely upside surprise had to go to all clients. |
| 6 | Additional Services for Select Clients (Weng e-mails all, then calls top 3) | No violation | Release to all first, then extra service is fine. Calling the top 3 first would be a violation. |
| 7 | Minimum Lot Allocations (Hampton's bond allocation) | No violation | Not strictly pro rata, but giving each client at least the minimum lot is fair and keeps positions saleable. |
| 8 | Excessive Trading (Chan trades other plans more to pay for research for his father's plan) | Violation | All clients who use the research share its cost; but never add trades just to equalize commissions. |
| 9 | Limited Social Media Disclosures (Burdette posts buy call on X first) | Violation (Burdette); supervisor see IV(C) | A social media post before client release is selective disclosure. |
| 10 | Fair Dealing between Clients (Rove's holdings and performance comparison reports) | No violation | Monitoring reports help check fair treatment after the fact. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q18: Fair treatment of a new recommendation means **minimize the time** between decision and release. Telling everyone in the firm early raises leak risk. Sending to institutional clients before individuals discriminates and violates III(B).
- LM2 Q19: A client calls to buy a stock you just changed to "sell". Tell the client about the change **before** accepting the order. If the client still wants to buy, you must follow the order.
- LM1 PP7: The III(B) wording is "deal fairly and objectively with all clients". Distractors twist III(C) timing ("after" action) and III(A) priority ("equal balance").

> **Traps: III(B)**
> - Fair is not equal. An option that says the Standard requires "equal" treatment is wrong. The target is "approximately the same time" (curriculum L2967).
> - Order matters: all clients first, then follow-up with big clients is fine (Ex 6). Select clients first is a violation (Ex 1, 5, 9).
> - Disclosure never cures an unfair allocation method, and client consent cannot approve a patently unfair one (curriculum L2945, L2999).
> - Family accounts managed like client accounts are not excluded from hot issues (curriculum L2940). See VI(B) Ex 3: putting parents last is also a violation.
> - New issues are allocated by client, not by portfolio manager. Block trades: same price and same commission.
> - Discretionary accounts do not get priority over nondiscretionary ones (Ex 2).
> - Allocating after seeing which trades went up is a violation (Ex 4).
> - Changed recommendations matter even more than first ones (curriculum L2936).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Fair is not equal": think of Netflix price tiers where every tier still gets the new release. (YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo)
> - Trade allocations: same price for all participating clients. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ)
> - CFA Society Los Angeles deck: "'Fairly' does not mean 'equally'"; IPO allocation is a common exam issue. (https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf)
> - Candidates' own notes often list "fair dealing is not equal dealing" and family accounts treated like other clients as heavily tested. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZeKYZYJQBGg comments)

## III(C) Suitability

### Standard text
> 1. When Members and Candidates are in an advisory relationship with a client, they must:
>    a. Make a reasonable inquiry into a client's or prospective client's investment experience, risk and return objectives, and financial constraints prior to making any investment recommendation or taking investment action and must reassess and update this information regularly.
>    b. Determine that an investment is suitable to the client's financial situation and consistent with the client's written objectives, mandates, and constraints before making an investment recommendation or taking investment action.
>    c. Judge the suitability of investments in the context of the client's total portfolio.
> 2. When Members and Candidates are responsible for managing a portfolio to a specific mandate, strategy, or style, they must make only investment recommendations or take only investment actions that are consistent with the stated objectives and constraints of the portfolio.
>
> (curriculum L1142 to L1152)

### What it requires
- Part 1 applies only in an **advisory relationship**. Consider the client's needs, circumstances, and objectives (curriculum L3106).
- Review the client's knowledge, experience, and financial situation, including: the investment's risk versus the client's constraints; its effect on diversification; whether the client has the means to bear the risk (curriculum L3106 to L3110).
- Test: only what a **prudent person** would be willing to do (curriculum L3110).
- A proper suitability check does **not** prevent losses (curriculum L3106).
- Who carries the duty: members giving advice in an advisory relationship. People who only execute a retail client's specific order, and sell-side analysts, may not be able to judge suitability for the end client (curriculum L3112).

**Developing an investment policy** (curriculum L3114 to L3116)
- **MUST** gather client information at the **start** of the relationship: finances, personal data (age, occupation), attitude to risk, objectives.
- Put it in a **written IPS**: risk tolerance, return requirement, and all constraints (time horizon, liquidity, taxes, legal and regulatory, unique circumstances).
- The IPS should also set out roles and responsibilities and a schedule for reviewing the IPS.

**Risk profile** (curriculum L3118 to L3122)
- Measuring risk tolerance is one of the most important factors.
- Analyze and quantify strategy risk in advance where possible.
- Derivatives and synthetic products: watch the built-in **leverage**. Leverage and limited liquidity bear directly on suitability.

**Updating the IPS** (curriculum L3124 to L3131)
- Update **at least annually** and also **before** material changes to recommendations or decisions for the client (curriculum L3126).
- Changes to look for:
  - Individuals: dependents, tax status, health, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, wealth outside the portfolio, how well income covers needs.
  - Institutions: unfunded pension liabilities, withdrawal rights in a savings plan, a foundation's payout rules.
- If the client withholds information, judge suitability on what the client actually gave you (curriculum L3131).

**Diversification** (curriculum L3133 to L3136)
- Reasonable diversification is the norm, especially for fiduciaries.
- A high-risk investment can be suitable inside the whole portfolio, or when the client's objectives allow speculation.
- You are responsible only on the basis of the information and criteria the client actually provided.

**Unsolicited trade requests** (curriculum L3138 to L3147)
1. If you **know** the request is unsuitable: SHOULD refrain from the trade until you discuss your concerns with the client.
2. Small impact (small trade, little change to risk): educate the client on how it deviates from the IPS, then follow firm policy on client approval. At minimum the client SHOULD acknowledge the discussion and accept the conditions that make it unsuitable.
3. Material impact: SHOULD use the chance to **update the IPS**.
4. Client refuses to change the IPS but insists: some firms allow the trade in a **new unmanaged account**. If no option exists, decide whether to continue the advisory relationship.

**Managing to an index or mandate** (curriculum L3149 to L3151)
- Part 2: invest consistently with the stated mandate. Example: a large-cap income fund buying small-cap start-ups breaks the mandate.
- Managers of pooled funds are **not** responsible for whether the **fund** suits each investor who buys it. Suitability duty attaches only to advisory relationships.

### Recommended procedures
**Investment policy statement** (curriculum L3155 to L3165): cover
- Client identification: (1) type and nature of client, (2) separate beneficiaries, (3) approximate share of total client assets you manage.
- Objectives: (1) return (income, growth, keep purchasing power), (2) risk tolerance (suitability, stability of values).
- Constraints: (1) liquidity, (2) expected cash flows, (3) investable funds (assets, liabilities, commitments), (4) time horizon, (5) taxes, (6) regulatory and legal, (7) preferences, prohibitions, unique needs, (8) proxy voting responsibilities and guidance.
- Performance measurement benchmarks.

**Regular updates** (curriculum L3167 to L3173)
- Review objectives and constraints periodically; compare client constraints with capital market expectations. A change in either can change the allocation.
- **Annual** review is reasonable unless market or business reasons call for more often.
- If a review cannot happen, **document** the attempts.

**Suitability test policies** (curriculum L3175 to L3184): tests should look beyond potential return and include: effect on diversification; investment risk versus assessed risk tolerance; fit with the required strategy.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Investment Suitability: Risk Profile (Smith gives same 20% tech idea to Robertson and Lanai) | Violation | One client's strategy cannot be copied to a client with different needs. The older, wealthier client was the risk-tolerant one. |
| 2 | Investment Suitability: Entire Portfolio (covered calls for risk-averse Crosby) | No violation | Judge the whole portfolio, not each holding; she explained all outcomes and tax effects. |
| 3 | IPS Updating (Jones's net worth rises fourfold) | IPS must be updated | Higher ability to take risk and lower income need; consider more equity and growth stocks. |
| 4 | Following an Investment Mandate (zero-dividend stock in a high-income fund) | Violation unless allowed by the disclosed mandate | Stay inside the mandate. |
| 5 | IPS Requirements and Limitations (Gubler puts 4% in a locked-up VC fund) | Violation of III(A) and III(C) | Lockup breaks a liquidity-focused IPS; being under the equity limit does not help; know the client's business beyond the IPS text. |
| 6 | Submanager and IPS Reviews (Ostrowski picks lowest-fee subadviser) | May be violating | Compare client risk profiles with the manager's strategy; fee alone is not enough; a request to diversify abroad is a time to review IPSs. See V(A). |
| 7 | Investment Suitability: Risk Profile (Snead chases quarterly bonus with high-beta stocks) | Violation; see VI(A) | Strategy drift driven by pay, not by IPS changes, breaks long-term IPSs. |
| 8 | Investment Suitability (Shrub's new fund, no one turned away) | Violation; see V(A) | Set an IPS for each client and recommend only matching products. You cannot refuse a client's direct order, but discuss risks; you may decline new clients whose requests are far out of line. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q20: Brown recommended a stock minutes into the first meeting. The fix is to learn the client's needs, objectives, and risk tolerance **before** any recommendation. Explaining the company or your CFA credential is fine but not the missing step.
- LM1 PP6: Under III(C) you consider investments in the context of the client's **total portfolio**. "Clients' interests first" is III(A); "no misrepresentation" is I(C).
- LM1 PP4: a manager to a specific mandate must follow the portfolio's stated objectives and is **not** required to judge whether the mandate suits all investors.
- LM1 PP7: III(C) inquiry comes **prior to** action, not after.

> **Traps: III(C)**
> - Part 1 applies only to advisory relationships. Order-takers, sell-side analysts, and fund managers running to a mandate do not judge whether the fund suits each buyer (curriculum L3112, L3151).
> - Timing: inquiry before any recommendation; IPS at the start; update at least annually and before material changes.
> - Age alone does not set risk tolerance; wealth matters (Ex 1).
> - A risky asset can be suitable in the total-portfolio view (Ex 2, curriculum L3136).
> - Staying under an asset-class limit does not make an illiquid asset suitable (Ex 5).
> - Withheld information: you are judged on what the client gave you (curriculum L3131).
> - Unsolicited unsuitable trade ladder: refrain and discuss; small impact = educate and get acknowledgment; big impact = update IPS; still insists = unmanaged account or end relationship.
> - Fee-only selection of a subadviser: book says "may be violating" (Ex 6), not a flat violation.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Choosing a sub-manager solely on fees violates suitability." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ) The curriculum's wording is softer: "may be violating" (Ex 6).
> - Judge suitability in the context of the total portfolio and the IPS. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=coTBbAxXxTA, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gsfdl2CzaSc)
> - CFA Society Los Angeles deck: do not act on time-sensitive opportunities without first assessing suitability. (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf) This phrase is not in the 2026 curriculum text, but it fits the "inquiry before action" rule.

## III(D) Performance Presentation

### Standard text
> When communicating investment performance information, Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that it is fair, accurate, and complete. (curriculum L1156)

### What it requires
- Give credible performance information; do not misstate or mislead. Full disclosure of performance data is encouraged (curriculum L3263).
- Covers any practice that misrepresents a record: both performance **presentation** and performance **measurement** (curriculum L3264).
- Covers **past** and **reasonably expected** performance (curriculum L3264).
- Applies to individual accounts, composites, and an analyst's or firm's results (curriculum L3264).
- Do not state or imply that clients will earn a past rate of return (curriculum L3264).
- Not limited to separate-account managers: pooled funds too, and **research analysts** promoting the accuracy of their calls (curriculum L3265).
- Brief presentations: **MUST** make the detailed supporting information available on request (curriculum L3265).
- Best practice (not required): a brief presentation mentions that the information is limited (curriculum L3266).
- It is a "reasonable efforts" duty, not a guarantee (curriculum L1156).

### Key definitions
- GIPS: the best way to meet III(D) for those showing performance of assets they manage. III(D) **recommends**, it does not require, GIPS (curriculum L3272).

### Recommended procedures
**Apply the GIPS standards** (curriculum L3270 to L3272): encourage your firm to comply.

**Without GIPS, you can still comply by** (curriculum L3274 to L3288):
- considering the audience's knowledge and sophistication;
- showing a **weighted composite** of similar portfolios, not one "representative" account;
- including **terminated accounts**, with when they ended;
- disclosures that explain results: say when results are **simulated** or model results; say when a record belongs to a **prior entity**; say whether returns are **gross of fees, net of fees, or after tax**;
- keeping the data and records used to calculate the performance.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Performance Calculation and Length of Time (Taylor: "expect steady 25%") | Violation of III(D) and I(C) | One good year shown as typical; a firm-wide claim must cover all account types; "you can expect" is a guarantee (I(C)). |
| 2 | Performance Calculation and Asset Weighting (Judd's GIPS claim, composites not asset weighted) | Violation | The violation is the false claim of GIPS compliance; claiming GIPS means meeting all requirements. |
| 3 | Performance Presentation and Prior Fund/Employer (McCoy shows G&P record as Mastermind's) | Violation | Prior-firm results may be shown only with disclosure of where they were earned and your role. |
| 4 | Performance Presentation and Simulated Results (Davis's back-tested results) | Violation | Clearly label simulated results and explain they come from applying the model backward. (The book's facts say 1996 to 2003; its comment says 1995 to 2003.) |
| 5 | Performance Calculation and Selected Accounts Only (Kilmer's "balanced" composite) | Violation | Cherry-picking accounts, undisclosed exclusions, and changing composite members distort the record. |
| 6 | Performance Attribution Changes (Purell switches method to flatter stock selection) | Would violate if changed without proper notice | A change made only to look better is unfair. If truly better, report both methods and why. |
| 7 | Performance Calculation Methodology Disclosure (Singh told to show unlabeled best of TWR or MWR) | Following the instruction would violate | Unlabeled, best-of returns are not complete information. |
| 8 | Performance Calculation Methodology Disclosure (Richmond fund picks the higher month-end date) | Clark complies by questioning it | Choosing the more favorable date each month is a problem; raising it shows proper concern. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q21: The firm's stated policy adds new accounts after the first full month. Cupp added a big account early to catch a gain: **violation**, because the result is inaccurate under the stated policy. Distractor cited a GIPS cash-flow rule though the firm does not claim GIPS.
- LM2 Q22: Paper (one of five team managers) showed the whole fund's record as his own: **violation** for not disclosing it was a team result. The prior employer's written permission solved the permission issue, not the team issue.
- LM1 PP9: Performance presentation sits in Standard III, not Standard V.
- Link: LM2 Q6 (I(C)): a true but misleading average driven by one client's penny stock is misrepresentation; "true and accurate" is not a defense.

> **Traps: III(D)**
> - GIPS is recommended, not required. But a GIPS claim that is not fully true is a violation (Ex 2).
> - Brief presentation: details on request is a MUST; noting the limited nature is only best practice.
> - Covers research analysts' track records and pooled funds, not only separate accounts.
> - Prior-employer results are allowed with disclosure of source and role (Ex 3). Team results must be shown as team results (Q22).
> - Simulated, back-tested, or model results must be labeled (Ex 4).
> - No cherry-picking: terminated accounts stay in; no rotating composite members (Ex 5); follow your own stated inclusion policy (Q21).
> - Method changes (attribution, TWR versus MWR, period-end date) need disclosure; never pick whichever looks better (Ex 6 to 8).
> - "You can expect X%" is also I(C) (Ex 1).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - Repeated by several lecturers: brief presentation is fine if details are available on request; include terminated accounts; use weighted composites, not one representative account. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UZwVCjyk4uE, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DZ3nkZcbVyo) These match the curriculum.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles: "GIPS encouraged but not required by the Standards." (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf) Matches curriculum L3272.

## III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must keep information about current, former, and prospective clients confidential unless:
> 1. The information concerns illegal activities on the part of the client or prospective client,
> 2. Disclosure is required by law, or
> 3. The client or prospective client permits disclosure of the information.
>
> (curriculum L1162 to L1168)

### What it requires
- Protect information about **current, former, and prospective** clients (curriculum L3371).
- Scope test (both parts): you got the information because of your special ability to handle part of the client's affairs, **and** it relates to that part of the relationship (curriculum L3371).
- If the law requires disclosure, or the information concerns the client's illegal activity, you **may have an obligation** to report it to the authorities (curriculum L3371). Not automatic.
- **Status of client:** the duty continues after the relationship ends. A client or former client may expressly authorize disclosure, and you may follow its terms (curriculum L3375 to L3377).
- **Laws** (curriculum L3379 to L3381):
  - If the law requires disclosure, comply.
  - If the law requires confidentiality even about illegal activity, do **not** disclose.
  - Rules limiting information flow between departments can also create confidentiality issues.
  - When in doubt, consult compliance or legal counsel **before** disclosing.
- **Electronic information** (curriculum L3383 to L3385):
  - Watch for accidental disclosure.
  - You need not become an IT security expert, but understand your employer's policies.
  - Encourage regular confidentiality training for **all** staff, including receptionists and portfolio associates.
- **CFA Institute investigations** (curriculum L3387 to L3389):
  - III(E) does not stop you cooperating with the Professional Conduct Program (PCP).
  - Where the law allows, treat the PCP as an extension of yourself for investigations of **your own** conduct (shall).
  - You are **encouraged** to cooperate on investigations of **others**.
  - Giving confidential information to the PCP is not a violation; the PCP keeps it confidential.

### Recommended procedures
- Safest approach: share client information only with **authorized fellow employees who also work for that client** (curriculum L3395).
- Before sharing information outside the confidential relationship (and not about illegal acts), ask (curriculum L3395 to L3399):
  - In what context was it disclosed? Is it relevant to the work for the client?
  - Is it background that would help you serve the client better?
- Follow firm rules on electronic communication and storage; if none, encourage some (curriculum L3401).
- **Communicating with clients** (curriculum L3403 to L3407): make sure firm communication methods prevent accidental release; review privacy measures regularly; discuss with clients the right channels for sending confidential information, since not every firm tool is suitable.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Possessing Confidential Information (Connor asked for hospital building plans) | Must refuse | Information is clearly within the confidential relationship. |
| 2 | Disclosing Confidential Information (Moody tips a charity about a client's planned gift) | Violation | Good intentions do not excuse disclosure. |
| 3 | Disclosing Possible Illegal Activity (Samuel and Garcia's improper pension charges) | Steps, not a violation finding | Inform supervisor; employer works with client to fix it; if not, get legal counsel. She may well have a duty to disclose and to resign as manager. |
| 4 | Disclosing Possible Illegal Activity (Bradford suspects CFO embezzling) | Steps | Ask compliance or legal counsel whether rules require reporting. |
| 5 | Accidental Disclosure of Confidential Information (client posts personal details in group page) | No violation | Clear warnings were given and the post was removed quickly; more training or controls may help. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q23 (III(A) and III(E)): Bronson is asked by a big donor for the endowment's financial data while trustees are away. **Do not send it.** Benefit to the client does not override confidentiality. Get permission **before**, not notice after.
- LM2 Q24: Townsend mentions her charity board role and its campaign in her newsletter: **no violation**. Giving client contact details to the charity would be a violation. There is no rule requiring the charity's approval.
- LM1 PP4: III(E) has no "reasonable and adequate basis" test; that phrase belongs to V(A).

> **Traps: III(E)**
> - Covers former and prospective clients too.
> - Three exceptions only: client's illegal activity, required by law, client permits.
> - Illegal activity: you "may have an obligation" to report; consult counsel first. If law demands secrecy, keep it.
> - PCP: sharing client information with the PCP is not a violation (own conduct: treat PCP as yourself where law permits).
> - Benefit to the client or a good cause does not justify disclosure (Ex 2, Q23).
> - Wrong timing: telling the client afterwards is not permission (Q23).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "Trap answer" in a Level II/III set: sharing client information with the CFA Institute PCP is NOT a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Matches curriculum L3389.
> - Advisers sharing client information with outside accountants without client consent IS a violation. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc) Consistent with the three exceptions.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles and 300Hours say confidentiality also applies to "deceased" clients. (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf, https://300hours.com/cfa-level-1-ethics-cheat-sheet/) The word "deceased" does not appear in the 2026 curriculum; the curriculum says "former" clients.

## IV(A) Loyalty

### Standard text
> In matters related to their employment, Members and Candidates must act for the benefit of their employer and not deprive their employer of the advantage of their skills and abilities, divulge confidential information, or otherwise cause harm to their employer. (curriculum L1172 to L1174)

### What it requires
- Do nothing that would injure the firm, deprive it of profit, or deprive it of your skills (curriculum L3476).
- Order: clients' interests **always** come before the employer's (curriculum L3476).
- Follow employer policies only to the extent they do not conflict with law or the Code and Standards (curriculum L3476).
- **Not a blanket rule:** you need not put the employer ahead of important personal and family duties. If personal matters interfere on a "regular or significant basis", you SHOULD talk with the employer (curriculum L3478).

**Employer responsibilities** (curriculum L3480 to L3486)
- Duties run both ways.
- You are **encouraged** (not required) to give your employer a copy of the Code and Standards.
- Employers are **not** bound by the Code and Standards, but should not set conflicting policies.
- Senior management should design pay and incentives that do not encourage unethical behavior.

**Independent practice** (curriculum L3488 to L3494)
- Abstain from independent competitive activity that conflicts with the employer.
- You may still start an independent business while employed.
- If you plan independent practice for pay, you **MUST** notify the employer and describe: (1) the services, (2) expected duration, (3) compensation (curriculum L3490).
- SHOULD not render services until the employer consents to **all** terms (curriculum L3494).

**Leaving an employer** (curriculum L3496 to L3524)
- Keep acting in the employer's interest until the resignation is **effective** (curriculum L3498).
- No bright line between allowed preparation and breach; judge each case.
- Possible violations, especially in combination (curriculum L3498 to L3508):
  1. misappropriating trade secrets;
  2. misusing confidential information;
  3. soliciting the employer's clients before leaving;
  4. self-dealing (taking a business opportunity or information that belongs to the employer);
  5. misappropriating clients or client lists.
- Preparations to compete are generally allowed if they do not breach loyalty (curriculum L3510).
- Do not contact existing **or potential** clients before leaving to solicit them for the new firm (curriculum L3510).
- After giving notice, follow the employer's policy on telling clients about your departure (curriculum L3510).
- Do not take records or files without the previous employer's **written** permission (curriculum L3510).
- After leaving (curriculum L3512 to L3516):
  - skills and experience are not confidential;
  - simple knowledge of former clients' names is generally not confidential, unless an agreement or law says so;
  - convenience copies of firm records SHOULD be erased or returned unless the firm allows you to keep them;
  - contacting former clients is allowed if the contact details do not come from the former employer's records and no noncompete forbids it; public information may be used;
  - review any noncompete agreement before leaving.
- Industry agreements (for example the US "Protocol for Broker Recruiting"): protection applies only if you follow **all** its steps. Under it you may take some general client contact information, must give a copy to local management, and only you may use it at the new firm (curriculum L3518 to L3524).

**Social media** (curriculum L3526 to L3532)
- Follow firm policies, especially when leaving; messages that may reach current clients follow the firm's departure-notice rules.
- Accounts created only for professional reasons, including firm-approved client accounts, are **firm assets**; transfer or delete as directed.
- Best practice: keep separate personal and professional accounts. If one account mixes both, discuss with the employer how to treat it.

**Whistleblowing** (curriculum L3534 to L3536)
- Protecting market integrity and clients comes before your own and your employer's interests.
- If the employer acts illegally or unethically, acts that would normally breach loyalty (going against instructions, breaking policies, copying records) may be justified.
- Only if the intent is clearly to protect clients or the market, **not for personal gain**.

**Nature of employment** (curriculum L3538 to L3545)
- Employee or independent contractor? It depends on the employer's **control**: set hours and location, facilities provided, expenses paid, whether you seek other work, how many clients or employers you have.
- Contractors: duties are set by the oral or written agreement with the client; once agreed, you must follow it.

### Key definitions
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Practice | Any service the employer **currently** offers for pay (curriculum L3494). |
| Undertaking independent practice | Engaging in competing business, as opposed to preparing to start it (curriculum L3494). |
| Self-dealing | Taking for yourself a business opportunity or information that belongs to the employer (curriculum L3506). |

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L3547 to L3567)
Encourage the firm to adopt, if missing:
1. **Competition policy:** know the rules on offering similar services outside the firm. If asked to sign a noncompete, make sure the details are clear **before** signing.
2. **Termination policy:** how departures are disclosed to clients and staff, whether social media updates are allowed, handover of research and accounts, and any agreement on taking client information.
3. **Incident-reporting procedures:** know the whistleblowing policy; many firms must offer confidential, anonymous reporting.
4. **Employee classification:** know your status (part time, full time, contractor) and which policies apply.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soliciting Former Clients (Magee solicits clients and prospects before resigning) | Violation | Duty is to the current employer while employed; soliciting current and prospective clients first is a breach. |
| 2 | Former Employer's Documents and Files (Hightower plans to copy lists, models, and his own spreadsheets) | Violation | Taking **any** employer records without consent, even ones you prepared, in any medium, is a violation. |
| 3 | Addressing Rumors (Winston considers an employee buyout) | Consistent with IV(A) | A buyout rests on employer and client permission; if you doubt management's honesty, consult legal counsel. |
| 4 | Ownership of Completed Prior Work (Clay's study for Bradley) | Would violate if she gives the same ideas to her new firm first | Even on a handshake deal, Bradley gets to act first unless it waives its rights; take none of its materials. |
| 5 | Ownership of Completed Prior Work (unpaid intern Madeline copies software) | Violation | An unpaid intern is an employee (paid in experience); copying firm software is misappropriation. |
| 6 | Soliciting Former Clients (Chisolm contacts former clients at new firm) | No violation, with conditions | Fine if loyal before leaving, no solicitation until gone, and no former-employer materials used. |
| 7 | Starting a New Firm (Allen registers a competing company) | No violation | Registering on her own time, without soliciting or operating, is preparation, not independent practice. |
| 8 | Competing with Current Employer (departing staff want to answer an RFP) | Violation unless permitted | Needs permission from the employer **and** the RFP issuer. |
| 9 | Externally Compensated Assignments (Mota, paid part-time mayor) | No automatic violation | If duties may detract from his job, he should discuss and agree with the employer. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q25: Correct statement: independent practice that could compete with the employer needs the employer's consent. There is no total ban on independent practice, and preparations to compete are allowed if they do not interfere with current duties. (The option says "written consent"; the solution text says "consent".)
- LM2 Q26: Jackman offers US$10,000 to advisers who join with committed assets. An adviser still employed is not eligible, because soliciting the current employer's clients is prohibited. Disclosing the incentive does not cure it. The offer itself is not a violation.
- LM1 PP8: IV(A) = do not deprive the employer of your skills and abilities. Gifts that compromise independence are I(B). IV(B) needs consent from **all parties involved**, not the employer only.

> **Traps: IV(A)**
> - Clients first, then employer. Loyalty to the employer is not a blanket rule over family life.
> - Preparation is fine (registering a company on your own time, Ex 7); competing or soliciting before the resignation is effective is not (Ex 1, Ex 8).
> - The solicitation ban covers potential clients too.
> - "Practice" means services the employer **currently** offers for pay.
> - Taking any employer record is a violation, even your own spreadsheets, even on a home device (Ex 2). Written permission is needed.
> - After leaving, the test is the **source** of contact data (public versus employer records) and any noncompete (Ex 10, Ex 12).
> - Unpaid interns count as employees (Ex 5). No written contract is needed for the duty (Ex 4).
> - Whistleblowing excuses disloyal acts only if aimed at protecting clients or markets, not personal gain.
> - Firm-approved social media accounts belong to the firm.
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - While employed you may not solicit clients using employer records; after leaving, a chance meeting is OK; work done for the employer belongs to the employer. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qd_2KJKO-8c)
> - A recalled practice question ruled a personal blog post ahead of the employer's downgrade a loyalty issue, not MNPI. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q) Compare curriculum Ex 15.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles: "Preparing to compete is not the same as competing; cannot take records; duty of loyalty applies to employees, not independent contractors." (https://www.cfala.org/files/CFA_III_Ethics_July_19_2025(2).pdf) Curriculum nuance: contractors are bound by their agreement with the client (curriculum L3544).
> - CFA Society Los Angeles caveat on "when in doubt, disclose": disclosure is not enough where a Standard requires **consent**, such as employer consent under Standard IV. (https://cfala.org/files/KYee_L3_Ethics_24.pdf)

## IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must not accept gifts, benefits, compensation, or consideration that competes with or might reasonably be expected to create a conflict of interest with their employer's interest unless they obtain written consent from all parties involved. (curriculum L1176 to L1180)

### What it requires
- Get permission from the employer **before** accepting pay or benefits from third parties for (1) services to the employer, or (2) any services that might conflict with the employer's interest (curriculum L3694).
- Covers direct pay from the client and indirect benefits from third parties (curriculum L3694).
- Why: such deals can affect loyalty and objectivity. The employer is entitled to know all such arrangements so it can judge your motives and the **true cost** of your services (curriculum L3698).
- Part-time staff: discuss possible limits on competing services during **negotiation and hiring**; IV(B) applies to limits identified then (curriculum L3699).

### Key definitions
- **Written consent:** any communication that can be documented, such as a retrievable e-mail (curriculum L3694).
- Two triggers in the text: the benefit **competes with**, or **might reasonably be expected to create a conflict** with, the employer's interest.

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L3701 to L3703)
- Make an **immediate written report** to your supervisor **and** the compliance officer of any proposed extra compensation.
- The party offering it should **confirm** the details, including client performance incentives.
- The report should state the nature of the compensation, the approximate amount, and the duration.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Notification of Client Bonus Compensation (Whitman's Monaco trip if returns reach 15%) | Violation | A contingent client bonus must be disclosed in writing and approved by the employer; it may bias him toward that account. |
| 2 | Notification of Outside Compensation (Jones's free club membership as director) | Violation | Nonmonetary benefits can create conflicts just like cash. |
| 3 | Prior Approval for Outside Compensation (Hollis meets ABC Oil CEO, declines travel, attends dinner) | No violation | Approval in advance, declined travel, and disclosure of an unplanned dinner afterward. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q27: A client offers Jurgen pay based on annual capital appreciation. Get employer permission (in writing) **before** accepting. Real concerns (other clients, short-term pressure) do not mean the arrangement is banned.
- LM1 PP8: consent must be from **all parties involved**, not the employer only.

> **Traps: IV(B)**
> - Standard text: written consent from all parties involved. Guidance stresses employer permission; recommended procedure adds confirmation by the offering party.
> - Consent comes **before** accepting.
> - Nonmonetary and performance-contingent benefits count (Ex 1, Ex 2).
> - IV(B) is about pay that competes or conflicts with the employer. Gifts that threaten independence and objectivity are I(B).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - Mnemonic from YouTubers: client gift for **past** performance: disclose to employer; tied to **future** performance: additional compensation needing written employer consent. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W5vlKCv3djc, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8KhntljYnrQ) Consistent with Ex 1.
> - Conflicting claim: one YouTuber recalled a question where a trip for beating next year's benchmark needed only disclosure. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnttDw7EX4Q) This conflicts with the curriculum (Ex 1 and Q27): follow the curriculum.

## IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors

### Standard text
> Members and Candidates must make reasonable efforts to ensure that anyone subject to their supervision or authority complies with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and the Code and Standards. (curriculum L1182 to L1184)

### What it requires
- Promote compliance with laws, rules, regulations, **firm policies**, and the Code and Standards by everyone you supervise (curriculum L3745). (The guidance adds "firm policies"; the Standard text does not.)
- Applies to anyone under your control or influence, **whether or not** they are CFA members or candidates (curriculum L3747).
- Supervisors need in-depth knowledge of the Code and Standards (curriculum L3747).
- What is reasonable depends on how many people you supervise and what they do. You may **delegate**, but must teach delegates how to prevent and detect violations (curriculum L3749).
- **Minimum:** reasonable efforts to **prevent and detect** violations by making sure effective compliance systems exist. An effective system goes beyond writing a code and reviewing actions (curriculum L3751).
- SHOULD run regular education and training, and reward ethical behavior, not just business results (curriculum L3753).
- Limited authority (large firm): still work with superiors to build compliance tools: code of ethics, policies, training, ethical incentives, firm-wide standards such as GIPS or the Asset Manager Code (curriculum L3755 to L3767).
- Inadequate system: bring it to senior management and recommend fixes. If you clearly cannot supervise properly, SHOULD **decline in writing** to accept supervisory duties until reasonable procedures exist (curriculum L3769).

**System for supervision** (curriculum L3771 to L3777)
- Make reasonable efforts so that procedures are established, documented, communicated, **and followed**; then monitored and enforced.
- "Adequate" = meets industry standards, regulations, the Code and Standards, and the firm's circumstances.
- Procedures must exist **before** a violation and target the activities most likely to go wrong.
- When you learn of a possible violation, **MUST promptly** start an assessment of its extent. Not enough: relying on the employee's word, or only reporting up the chain and warning the employee. Meanwhile limit the person's activities or increase monitoring.

**Supervision includes detection** (curriculum L3779 to L3781)
- Make reasonable efforts to **detect** violations; use written procedures and periodic review.
- If you set reasonable procedures and a real compliance program, you **may not** be in violation when someone hides misconduct anyway.
- But you **may** violate IV(C) if you know or should know the procedures are not being followed.

### Key definitions
- **Code of ethics** = broad principles and fiduciary duties for all staff, in plain language. **Compliance procedures** = detailed rules that put the code into daily practice. Keep them **separate** (curriculum L3790 to L3792).

### Recommended procedures (curriculum L3785 to L3857)
**Codes of ethics:** encourage the employer to adopt one, keep it plain and free of detailed procedures, and give it to **clients** (curriculum L3789 to L3793).

**Adequate compliance procedures should** (curriculum L3799 to L3817):
- be in a clear, accessible written manual tailored to the firm, easy to understand;
- name a compliance officer with clear authority and resources;
- describe the supervision hierarchy and duties;
- set checks and balances;
- outline scope, permitted conduct, and how monitoring and testing are documented;
- set procedures for reporting violations and sanctions.

**Once a program exists, a supervisor should** (curriculum L3817 to L3830): send it to staff; update it; keep educating; issue reminders; include professional conduct in performance reviews; review employee actions; enforce after a violation.

**When a violation is found, a supervisor should** (curriculum L3832 to L3840): respond promptly; investigate fully; increase supervision or restrict the wrongdoer meanwhile; review procedures.

**Training** (curriculum L3842 to L3848): regular ethics training plus a code builds a culture of integrity and helps people spot pitfalls.

**Incentives** (curriculum L3852 to L3857): a "succeed at all costs" culture pushes even good people astray. Tie pay to client interests and **how** results are achieved, not **how much** is earned for the firm.

### Application examples
| # | Title | Verdict | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supervising Research Activities (Mattock; staff trade and tip before a downgrade is published) | Violation (Mattock) | Needed procedures to stop and detect trading or tipping on unpublished rating changes. |
| 2 | Supervising Research Activities (Miller passes on an unreviewed merger memo) | Violation (Miller) | Sending research without checking its basis or MNPI risk; a "not reviewed" note does not cure it. |
| 3 | Supervising Trading Activities (Mason ignores trainee's free-riding trades) | Violation (Mason) | Failed to investigate; a supervisor paid on commissions must be extra alert to his own conflict. |
| 4 | Supervising Trading Activities and Record Keeping (Tabbing allocates good fills to the profit-sharing plan) | Violation (Henry), twice | Failed to supervise her futures trading and to set record-keeping procedures; not knowing is no defense when no system exists. |
| 5 | Accepting Responsibility (Rasmussen's concerns dismissed) | Violation by supervisor and compliance officer | They must review concerns raised and build an ethical culture. |
| 6 | Inadequate Procedures (Witt's weak report gets posted) | Lack of compliance procedures (IV(C) issue) | The supervisor must set a review process for all contracted analysts. Witt himself breaches V(A). |
| 7 | Inadequate Supervision (CIO Papis moves allocation to a friend) | Need for review at all levels | Even the CIO needs oversight; firms need procedures to detect such actions. See V(A), V(B), VI(A). |
| 8 | Supervising Research Activities (Graf, junior analyst posts buy call on X) | Violation (Graf) | No procedures to stop research release via social media; train staff on business use of personal social media. |
| 9 | Supervising Research Activities (Chen skips review of a late-night report) | Violation (Chen) | Time pressure and a good track record do not excuse skipping the required review. |

### Practice-problem lessons
- LM2 Q7 (main answer III(A), with I(D) and IV(C)): Anderb got better personal prices from the broker and filed reports only in months with no trades. Supervisor Bates violated IV(C) by not enforcing reporting rules. Owning the same securities as clients, and using the same broker, are not prohibited in themselves.
- LM2 Q33 / VI(B) Ex 4: letting an employee skip personal trading reports is a supervisor violation.
- LM1 PP3: the supervisor standard is broader than detection and prevention alone.

> **Traps: IV(C)**
> - Covers non-members you supervise too.
> - Delegation does not remove your duty.
> - Minimum = reasonable efforts to prevent **and** detect. Training and ethical incentives are SHOULD items.
> - Reasonable procedures plus a real program: an undetected, hidden violation may not be your violation. Knowing or should-know that procedures are ignored: violation.
> - On learning of a problem: promptly investigate. Taking the employee's word, or only warning and reporting up, is not enough.
> - No system and inadequate authority: decline supervision in writing.
> - The same story often has two verdicts: the whistleblower complies (IV(A) Ex 11), the supervisors violate (IV(C) Ex 5).
>
> **Research points (opinion, not curriculum)**
> - "A supervisor with reasonable procedures is not in violation just because someone hid misconduct." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aT8sGNsyFo0) Matches curriculum L3781.
> - CFA Society Los Angeles: "Informing subordinates is not enough; monitoring and detection are required." (https://www.cfala.org/files/EJohnsen_L3_Ethics__Jul26.pdf)
